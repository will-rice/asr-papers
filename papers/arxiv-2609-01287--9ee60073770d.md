# Introduction

Multi-talker automatic speech recognition (MT-ASR) transcribes conversations with multiple speakers, often overlapping, into per-speaker text streams . Cascaded systems that segment mixtures with voice activity detection (VAD) or diarization boundaries, or that first separate sources , are sensitive to boundary errors on the order of tens to hundreds of milliseconds that truncate onsets and leak competing speech into the wrong stream. Serialized output training (SOT)  avoids explicit segmentation, but the acoustic encoder is typically not conditioned on speaker activity. Diarization-conditioned Whisper  injects hard speaker activity. In contrast, we inject a mixture-normalized speaker share $`\mathbf{p}_t\in\Delta^{1}`$ ($`S{=}2`$; $`\sum_s p_{t,s}{=}1`$), estimated by a softmax head or, as an oracle, by a per-frame energy ratio. Overlap is represented as mass near $`(1/2,1/2)`$, rather than independent binary activities.

Soft Posterior Speaker Injection (SPSI) predicts $`\hat{\mathbf{P}}`$ from Whisper encoder states  and injects it (i) after encoder blocks 8, 16 and 24 by feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM)  and (ii) into the decoder as $`K{=}4`$ speaker-memory tokens (Fig. <a href="#fig:arch" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:arch">1</a>). Training combines an SOT recognition loss with a soft diarization loss; on synthetic data, checkpoints are selected by high-overlap validation constrained permutation word error rate (cpWER). Relative to speaker-distinguishable connectionist temporal classification (SD-CTC)  and speaker-aware CTC , SPSI is a continuous _share injector_, rather than a one-speaker-per-frame classifier.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

- We propose a novel SPSI method to address overlapped MT-ASR without irreversible hard segmentation. SPSI injects a mixture-normalized speaker share into Whisper through multi-layer FiLM and decoder speaker-memory prompts.

- Based on SPSI, we present a two-pass encode on a shared Whisper-medium backbone for MT-ASR. It is a simple and effective share injector that requires no external diarization or source separation at inference.

- The comprehensive experiments demonstrate that SPSI reduces cpWER relative to same-backbone SOT on controlled LibriSpeech overlap, with a larger gain under high overlap, and that freeze-posterior overlap-heavy (OV-heavy) adaptation yields a 5.1-point held-out reduction on oracle-segmented LibriCSS .

# Proposed Method

(b) Each FiLM is a per-frame multilayer perceptron (MLP; scale <span class="math inline"><strong>γ</strong></span>, shift <span class="math inline"><strong>β</strong></span>). <span class="math inline"><strong>P</strong></span> is pooled into <span class="math inline"><em>K</em> = 4</span> memory tokens prepended to <span class="math inline"><strong>A</strong><sub>0</sub></span>. Let $`x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}`$ be a 16 kHz mixture and $`M=\mathrm{Mel}(x)`$ its 80-bin log-Mel ($`T_0{\le}3000`$). Whisper-medium ($`C{=}1024`$, $`L{=}24`$) maps $`M`$ to encoder memory $`\mathbf{A}_0\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times C}`$ ($`T{\le}1500`$). English serialized output training (SOT) targets serialize two speakers as $`\mathrm{prefix}`$, `<spk0>`, $`y^{(0)}`$, `<spk1>`, $`y^{(1)}`$, `<eot>`. With teacher forcing, the recognition loss $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ASR}}`$ is token-level cross-entropy on $`\mathrm{Dec}(y_{<i},\mathbf{A})`$ (padding positions are excluded). Acoustic time is indexed by $`t`$ and decoder token steps by $`i`$. $`\mathbf{P}`$ denotes the injected posterior (estimated, oracle, or hard); $`\mathbf{p}_t`$ is row $`t`$.

## Soft Posterior Head

An unconditioned pass $`\mathbf{X}=\mathrm{Enc}(M)`$ is followed by layer normalization (LN), 1-D convolution ($`k{=}5`$), GELU, 1-D convolution ($`k{=}3`$), GELU (hidden dimension $`d{=}256`$), and a linear projection to logits $`\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 2}`$:

```math
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{P}}=\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{Z})\in[0,1]^{T\times 2}.
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
```

Default injection uses $`\mathbf{P}{=}\hat{\mathbf{P}}`$. Ablations replace this estimate with the energy-ratio $`\mathbf{P}^\star`$, exclusive $`\mathbb{I}[\hat{p}_{t,s}{>}1/2]`$, or multi-label oracle voice activity detection (VAD) $`a_{s,t}`$. Native 50 Hz posteriors are linearly interpolated to the encoder length $`T`$.

## Multi-Layer Feature-Wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)

Injection after 1-based blocks $`\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{FiLM}}{=}\{8,16,24\}`$:

```math
\begin{align}
\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_t
&= \mathrm{GELU}(\mathbf{W}_1^{(\ell)}\mathbf{p}_t+\mathbf{b}_1^{(\ell)}),
\label{eq:film_h}\\
[\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\ell)}_t;\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(\ell)}_t]
&= \mathbf{W}_2^{(\ell)}\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_t+\mathbf{b}_2^{(\ell)},
\label{eq:film_gb}\\
\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)}_{t,:}
&\leftarrow
\mathbf{H}^{(\ell)}_{t,:}\odot(1+\tanh\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\ell)}_t)
+\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(\ell)}_t.
\label{eq:film}
\end{align}
```

Pass 1 computes $`\hat{\mathbf{P}}`$ without FiLM; pass 2 re-encodes with <a href="#eq:film" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:film">[eq:film]</a> (shared encoder weights). Ablation injectors (same decoder prompts): single-layer FiLM on $`\mathbf{A}_0`$; cross-attention (Attn) with keys and values $`\mathbf{K}{=}\mathbf{V}{=}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{W}_s`$; and a speaker-mask adapter with gated convolutional branches.

## Decoder Prompts

Let $`\boldsymbol{\mu}=\mathrm{mean}_t\mathbf{p}_t`$, $`\boldsymbol{\mathrm{m}}=\max_t\mathbf{p}_t`$, and $`o=\mathrm{mean}_t\,p_{t,0}p_{t,1}`$. A multilayer perceptron (MLP) maps $`\mathbf{g}=[\boldsymbol{\mu};\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{m}};\,o\mathbf{1}_2]`$ to $`K{=}4`$ tokens $`\mathbf{q}_k`$, followed by LN and prepended as $`\mathbf{A}=[\mathbf{q}_{1:K};\,\mathbf{A}_0]`$. Decoder cross-attention uses queries $`\mathbf{u}_i`$ and keys/values from $`\mathbf{A}`$. Unless noted otherwise, **SPSI** uses this default coupling.

**Training Objective.**

Oracle energy $`e_{s,t}`$ at 50 Hz gives $`p^\star_{t,s}=e_{s,t}/(e_{0,t}+e_{1,t}+\varepsilon)`$ and $`a_{s,t}=\mathbb{I}[e_{s,t}{>}10^{-6}]`$. With $`m_t=a_{0,t}a_{1,t}`$ and active frames $`\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{act}}=\{t:\sum_s p^\star_{t,s}{>}0\}`$,

```math
\begin{align}
\delta_t &= -\sum_s p^\star_{t,s}\log\hat{p}_{t,s},
\label{eq:diar_w}\\
w_t &= 1+(\omega{-}1)m_t,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diar}}
&=
\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{act}}} w_t\delta_t}
{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{act}}} w_t}.
\label{eq:diar}
\end{align}
```

The joint objective is $`\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ASR}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diar}}`$, with $`\lambda{=}0.5`$ and default $`\omega{=}1`$ (a variant with $`\omega{=}2`$ is also trained). When auxiliary modules are present, AdamW uses two parameter groups: backbone $`\eta_{\mathrm{bb}}{=}2{\times}10^{-6}`$, posterior/FiLM/prompt $`\eta_{\mathrm{ex}}{=}5{\times}10^{-5}`$; SOT uses a single group at $`5{\times}10^{-6}`$. On the synthetic set, checkpoints are selected by high-overlap validation constrained permutation word error rate (cpWER) rather than training loss. On LibriCSS we freeze the posterior head $`\phi`$, set $`\lambda{=}0`$, and adapt the remaining parameters $`\theta`$ (Whisper, FiLM, and prompts) so that $`\hat{\mathbf{P}}_\phi`$ remains calibrated from synthetic pretraining:

```math
\begin{equation}
\min_{\theta}\;
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ASR}}(M,\mathbf{y};\,\mathbf{P}{=}\hat{\mathbf{P}}_\phi),
\label{eq:freeze}
\end{equation}
```

then continue on an overlap-heavy (OV-heavy) subset (enriched in OV20–OV40). Joint fine-tuning (FT) additionally updates $`\phi`$ for 4 epochs at $`1{\times}10^{-6}`$/$`1{\times}10^{-5}`$ ($`\lambda{=}0.5`$).

# Experiments

**Data and Metrics.**

We mix LibriSpeech  `*-clean` pairs (different speakers; signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) $`\mathrm{Unif}[-5,5]`$ dB; exponential-decay room impulse response (RIR) $`\mathrm{RT60}\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0.25,0.75]`$ s; noise $`5`$–$`18`$ dB). The overlap ratio $`\rho`$ is the fraction of 50 Hz frames in which both sources are active (not a Jaccard index). Bins: low $`\rho{<}0.25`$, mid $`0.25{\le}\rho{<}0.45`$, high $`\rho{\ge}0.45`$; train/dev/test $`=12`$k$`/1`$k$`/1`$k with quota $`25/40/35\%`$ (test $`n{=}250/400/350`$). We report an utterance-mean two-speaker constrained permutation word error rate (cpWER): the mean over utterances of the lower of concatenated and average per-stream word error rate (WER) after the best speaker permutation (related to concatenated minimum-permutation WER and optimal reference combination WER (ORC-WER) ; not a corpus-level MeetEval score). One-sided paired bootstrap ($`B{=}5000`$) is reported on $`\Delta_{\mathrm{cp}}=\mathrm{cpWER}_{\mathrm{SOT}}-\mathrm{cpWER}_{\mathrm{SPSI}}`$; 95% confidence intervals (CIs) are two-sided. On this synthetic set, all systems use Whisper-medium, greedy decoding (max. 224 tokens), BF16, mini-batch size 4, six epochs, 7$`\times`$A40 GPUs, and high-overlap validation selection.

## Comparisons on Synthetic Overlap

We compare the following same-backbone systems: **serialized output training (SOT)**  ($`\lambda{=}0`$, no injector); **SOT+speaker cross-entropy (CE)** (hard exclusive diarization, no injection); **SOT+speaker-distinguishable CTC lite (SD-CTC-lite)**  (lightweight 29-symbol character CTC, $`\gamma_{\mathrm{sd}}{=}0.3`$; not a full replica); **Pipeline** (oracle or estimated voice activity detection (VAD) segmentation, pretrained Whisper, merged to SOT format); and **SPSI**.

## Domain Transfer on LibriCSS

We segment LibriCSS monaural recordings into $`{\le}30`$ s two-speaker windows using _ground-truth_ time marks ($`n{=}1315`$; sessions 0–6 / 7 / 8–9 for train/dev/test), rather than automatic diarization or full-meeting `asclite`. Official conditions: **0L**/**0S** (0% overlap) and **OV10**–**OV40**. Freeze-posterior fine-tuning (FT) uses backbone/extra learning rates $`5{\times}10^{-7}/2{\times}10^{-6}`$ for 6 epochs, then 4 epochs of overlap-heavy (OV-heavy) training at $`3{\times}10^{-7}/1{\times}10^{-6}`$.

Pooled zero-shot (ZS) SPSI is comparable to SOT (0.466 versus 0.467; Fig. <a href="#fig:libricss_cond" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:libricss_cond">4</a>; bootstrap CI includes 0): slightly better on 0L and OV40, slightly worse on OV20, and comparable on OV10/OV30. SOT+SD-CTC-lite and SOT+speaker CE remain higher (0.479 / 0.476). On held-out sessions 8–9 (Table <a href="#tab:libricss_adapt_ab" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tab:libricss_adapt_ab">1</a>), jointly updating $`\phi`$ does not help SPSI (0.445 versus ZS 0.449) and is worse than SOT Joint (0.408). Freezing $`\phi`$ yields 0.340 versus 0.377 SOT. OV-heavy continuation attains **0.324** versus 0.375 ($`\Delta_{\mathrm{cp}}{=}5.1`$ points, $`p{<}0.001`$), with OV30/OV40 at $`0.458`$/$`0.393`$ (SOT $`0.534`$/$`0.407`$). Attention Joint FT is close (0.329) but freeze/OV-heavy leave it at 0.356/0.348. Held-out ZS ablations (0.451–0.466) all exceed SPSI (0.449); $`\omega{=}2`$, hard-mask, and adapter do not obtain the OV-heavy gain (0.370 / 0.356 / 0.374), and $`\omega{=}2`$ degrades relative to freeze ($`0.337{\to}0.370`$). The best transfer protocol is freeze-posterior SPSI with $`\omega{=}1`$ and OV-heavy data.

# Conclusion and Future Work

Soft Posterior Speaker Injection (SPSI) injects continuous speaker posteriors via multi-layer feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) and decoder prompts for multi-talker automatic speech recognition (MT-ASR). It reduces constrained permutation word error rate (cpWER) relative to serialized output training (SOT) and hard-segmentation pipelines on stratified LibriSpeech overlap; encoder FiLM and decoder prompts are complementary, and hard masks do not outperform SOT. On oracle-segmented LibriCSS, zero-shot cpWER is comparable to SOT. Freeze-posterior training with overlap-heavy (OV-heavy) adaptation yields a 5.1-point held-out reduction versus SOT ($`p{<}0.001`$); hard-mask, adapter, and $`\omega{=}2`$ variants do not match this transfer. Future work includes extending the simplex share beyond two speakers, replacing oracle windowing with automatic segmentation and corpus-level meeting scoring, and collapsing the two-pass encode toward streaming. A remaining issue is posterior calibration under domain shift: jointly updating $`\phi`$ currently hurts transfer, so adapting the head without collapsing $`\hat{\mathbf{P}}`$ is an open direction.
