# Gabor Gabor: E-Gaborformer A Strictly Causal Speech Recognizer with Gabor-Dictionary Convolutions and Delta-Rule Memory

Nir Krakowski Tamir Cohen Ohad Zohar Chen Zarfati nir@deepdub.ai
tamir.cohen@deepdub.ai ohad@deepdub.ai chen@deepdub.ai  
Deepdub  
[https://deepdub.ai](https://deepdub.ai)

###### Abstract

Real-time voice agents need a speech recognizer that never waits for
future audio and whose memory per call does not grow with the length of
the call. We present E-Gaborformer, a strictly causal encoder that
combines Gated DeltaNet-2 recurrent layers with sliding-window attention
in a 3:1 ratio, and builds every temporal convolution from a fixed
dictionary of causal Gabor atoms. Its streaming state is constant, about
19 MB per call at production scale including the prediction network,
where a causal-attention encoder of the same size accumulates over 1 GB
after ten minutes. In a controlled causal study on LibriSpeech,
E-Gaborformer leaves the initial plateau earlier, reaches a given
accuracy up to 18% sooner, and matches or beats an E-Branchformer
trained with the identical front end and recipe. At production scale, a
0.6B-parameter Hebrew and English streaming transducer built on
E-Gaborformer has 28 to 31% lower word error rate (WER) than NVIDIA’s
Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming, a model of the same size, on English
voice-agent traffic, and up to 20% lower Hebrew WER than our previous
production system. The same weights serve strictly causal and
480 ms-lookahead decoding with no loss in accuracy, and a two-class
formulation of the RNN-T lattice trains the transducer 2.6$`\times`$
faster with exact loss and gradients.

## 1 Introduction

A voice agent must transcribe the user while they are still speaking,
for thousands of concurrent calls. Two properties of the acoustic
encoder matter for this setting: _causality_, so that no output waits
for future audio, and a _bounded per-stream state_, so that serving cost
does not grow with call length.

Most strong ASR encoders, such as the Conformer \[11\] and the
E-Branchformer \[19\], are bidirectional. They can be made causal by
masking future frames, but causal self-attention still caches every past
key and value: an 18-layer, $`d{=}1024`$ encoder at 80 ms frames
accumulates about 110 MB of fp32 cache per minute of audio per caller.
Chunked and memory-augmented transformers bound this cost with explicit
truncation \[44, 3, 27\]. Linear-attention recurrences take another
route: they compress the past into a fixed-size state that is updated
once per frame \[18, 43\]. The delta-rule family, DeltaNet \[50\], Gated
DeltaNet \[51\] and Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) \[12\], has recently closed
much of the gap to attention in language modeling, and hybrid stacks
that mix recurrent and attention layers often do better than either
alone \[4, 23, 40\].

We show that a mostly recurrent causal encoder matches attention in
speech recognition, and propose E-Gaborformer. It keeps the
E-Branchformer block and changes three things inside it: a 3:1
GDN-2 / sliding-window-attention global stack, depthwise convolutions
built from a fixed Gabor dictionary, and a change to the GDN-2 output
gate that keeps the recurrent branches active from the first update. The
result is an encoder with a constant per-call state that trains faster
than its attention-based counterpart and, at production scale,
outperforms a state-of-the-art streaming recognizer of the same size on
our English voice-agent traffic.

#### Contributions.

- •
  A hybrid recurrent encoder for causal ASR (Section 4). Three GDN-2
  layers for every sliding-window attention layer, inside the
  E-Branchformer block. GDN-2 branches start 50 to 60$`\times`$ quieter
  than attention branches under CTC; a change to their output gate, at
  no parameter cost, removes the resulting training stalls.
- •
  Gabor-dictionary depthwise convolutions (Section 4.2). Restricting
  convolution kernels to a structured basis is an established idea in
  vision \[15, 36\], and Gabor functions have a long history as speech
  features \[42, 2\]. We apply a fixed, one-sided (causal) Gabor basis
  to the temporal depthwise convolutions of a speech encoder, trained
  from the first step. This differs from fitting and freezing Gabor
  functions after training \[41\].
- •
  A controlled causal comparison (Section 5) against an E-Branchformer
  with an identical front end and recipe, with training curves over ten
  epochs, and a screen of 36 encoder configurations in about 160 runs
  that isolates the contribution of each component.
- •
  A 0.6B-parameter streaming transducer (Section 6) whose prediction
  network interleaves GDN-2 with token attention. On English voice-agent
  traffic it has 28 to 31% lower WER than Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming,
  and one set of weights serves both strictly causal and
  480 ms-lookahead decoding at equal accuracy.
- •
  Exact and fast transducer training (Section 6.6): a two-class RNN-T
  lattice with a closed-form logit gradient, aligned output layers,
  tensor-core depthwise convolutions and weight-preserving length
  regrouping, together 2.6$`\times`$ faster per update with an unchanged
  objective.

## 2 Related work

#### Encoders for ASR.

The Conformer \[11\] interleaves self-attention with a convolution
module. The Branchformer \[33\] runs attention and a
convolutional-gating MLP (cgMLP) in parallel branches, and the
E-Branchformer \[19\] adds a depthwise-convolution merge; it matches or
exceeds the Conformer across tasks \[34\]. FastConformer \[39\]
subsamples 8$`\times`$ for efficiency.

#### Streaming recognizers.

Cache-aware streaming \[27\] trains a FastConformer with limited right
context and reuses cached activations at inference; training with
several right-context sizes lets one model serve several latencies;
dual-mode and dynamic-chunk training \[53, 56\] pursue the same goal.
Classic streaming recognizers bound the state with LSTM encoders \[13\]
or truncated attention context \[57, 44\]. Nemotron 3.5 ASR
Streaming \[29\] applies this recipe to a 0.6B-parameter multilingual
RNN-T with a language prompt. Its attention cache is bounded by a fixed
left context of 56 frames per layer. We use the same multi-lookahead
training idea; our contribution is the encoder and prediction network,
not the streaming recipe.

#### Recurrent and hybrid sequence models.

Linear attention \[18\] and fast-weight programmers \[43\] recast
attention as a recurrence with a matrix-valued state. The delta
rule \[50\] replaces additive writes with error-correcting ones, gating
adds data-dependent forgetting \[51\], and GDN-2 \[12\] decouples
erasing from writing with channel-wise gates. Structured state-space
models \[10, 9\], long convolutions \[35\] and hybrids of recurrent and
local-attention layers \[4, 23, 40\] show that a few attention layers
recover the exact recall that pure recurrences lack. In speech,
Zipformer \[52\] and state-space or Mamba encoders \[16\] are recent
efficient alternatives to the Conformer.

#### Structured and parametrized filters.

Structured receptive fields \[15\] and DCFNet \[36\] express CNN kernels
as learned combinations of a fixed basis (Gaussian derivatives and
Fourier–Bessel functions). Gabor convolutional networks \[26\] modulate
learned kernels with Gabor filters, and GaborNet \[1\] learns the
parameters of Gabor kernels directly. In speech, spectro-temporal Gabor
filters serve as robust features \[42\] and as fixed CNN kernels \[2\],
and SincNet \[38\] and LEAF \[54\] learn band-pass front-end filters
through their center frequencies and bandwidths. Orukeet \[41\] fits a
Gabor function to each nine-tap depthwise kernel of a trained Parakeet
model, replaces the best-fitting half by its fit and freezes it during
further training. Our filters are temporal rather than spectro-temporal,
act inside every encoder block rather than at the input, and are
constrained to the Gabor span from initialization.

#### Efficient transducer training.

The RNN-T loss \[7\] requires joint-network outputs at every (frame,
token) node. Function merging \[22\] avoids storing full log-softmax
outputs, and pruned RNN-T \[20\] restricts the lattice to a band. Fused
cross-entropy kernels avoid materializing large logit matrices in
language models \[48\].

## 3 Preliminaries

#### The E-Branchformer block.

An E-Branchformer layer \[19\] is a macaron block: a half-step
feed-forward layer, a _global_ and a _local_ branch computed in
parallel, a merge, and a second half-step feed-forward layer. The global
branch is multi-head self-attention. The local branch is a cgMLP: a
projection to $`2c`$ channels, a split into value and gate halves, and a
depthwise convolution over time on the gate. The merge concatenates the
two branches, applies a second depthwise convolution and projects back
to $`d`$.

#### Gated DeltaNet-2.

GDN-2 \[12\] keeps, per head, a matrix state
$`S_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times d_{v}}`$ ($`64\times 64`$ in all our
models) and updates it once per frame:

```math
S_{t}=\bigl(I-k_{t}(b_{t}\odot k_{t})^{\!\top}\bigr)\,\mathrm{Diag}\!\left(e^{g_{t}}\right)S_{t-1}+k_{t}\,(w_{t}\odot v_{t})^{\!\top},\qquad o_{t}=S_{t}^{\top}q_{t}. \tag{1}
```

The decay $`g_{t}\leq 0`$ forgets channel by channel, the erase gate
$`b_{t}`$ removes what the current key already stores, and the write
gate $`w_{t}`$ selects which value channels are written. Queries, keys
and values pass through causal short convolutions (kernel 4), keys and
queries are $`\ell_{2}`$-normalized, the decay uses a low-rank
projection (rank 64), and the per-head output passes through a gated
root-mean-square normalization (RMSNorm) before the output projection.
We use the chunked Triton kernels of flash-linear-attention \[49, 46\].

#### CTC and RNN-T.

Both training objectives sum the probability of the reference transcript
over all of its alignments to the encoder frames. Connectionist temporal
classification (CTC) \[8\] adds a blank symbol and predicts one symbol
or blank per frame, independently of the symbols already emitted; we use
it in the controlled study (Section 5). In *intermediate CTC* \[21\], a
second CTC loss is computed from the output of an inner encoder layer
through the same output layer (self-conditioned CTC \[28\] also feeds
that layer’s prediction back into the encoder), and the training loss
becomes $`(1-w)\,L_{\text{CTC}}+w\,L_{\text{CTC}}^{(\ell)}`$; we tried
$`\ell=6`$ and $`w=0.3`$ as an ablation. The recurrent neural network
transducer (RNN-T) \[7\] adds a _prediction network_ that reads the
symbols emitted so far, and a _joint network_ that combines its output
with each encoder frame into a distribution over the vocabulary and
blank. Its loss is the negative log-probability of the transcript summed
over the paths through the frame–symbol lattice, in which blank advances
one frame and a symbol advances one position in the transcript. Unlike
CTC, the transducer conditions each output on the previous symbols, and
it is the model we deploy (Section 6).

#### Causality.

We call a model strictly causal if output frame $`j`$ depends on no
input after frame $`j`$. Output frame $`j`$ covers the eight 10 ms
analysis hops ending at $`80j`$ ms; with a 25 ms STFT window it depends
on audio up to $`80j+12.5`$ ms. This requires masked attention,
left-padded convolutions, a forward-only recurrence and a subsampling
front end that pads only on the left in time.

## 4 E-Gaborformer

![](arxiv-2611-08092v1--7e12cca40c80.figures/inline-1.svg)

Figure 1: One E-Gaborformer block (residual connections omitted). The
global branch is a GDN-2 recurrence in three layers out of four and
causal sliding-window attention in the fourth; both depthwise
convolutions are Gabor-dictionary convolutions.

E-Gaborformer keeps the E-Branchformer block and changes its global
branch, its two depthwise convolutions and the output gate of the
recurrent layers (Figure 1).

### 4.1 Hybrid global branch

The global branch is a GDN-2 recurrence in every layer whose index
(counted from 1) is not a multiple of four, and causal sliding-window
attention over the last $`W{=}128`$ frames (10.24 s) with rotary
position embeddings \[45\] in every fourth layer; we write this pattern
as GGGS (G: GDN-2, S: sliding-window attention). The recurrence can
carry information over arbitrarily long distances in a fixed-size state,
subject to its learned decay; the attention layers provide exact recall
over the recent window, which a compressed state cannot.

### 4.2 Gabor-dictionary convolutions

A depthwise convolution with kernel size $`K`$ applies, per channel
$`c`$, a kernel $`h_{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}`$. Following Gabor’s
time–frequency atoms \[6\], we restrict $`h_{c}`$ to the span of a fixed
dictionary $`\{\phi_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}`$:

```math
h_{c}[\tau]=\sum_{m=1}^{M}a_{c,m}\,\phi_{m}[\tau],\qquad\tau=-(K-1),\dots,0, \tag{2}
```

and learn only the coefficients $`a_{c,m}`$ and a bias. The dictionary
has $`M{=}16`$ unit-norm atoms over lags $`\tau\leq 0`$ (the past): two
Gaussians $`\exp(-\tau^{2}/2\sigma^{2})`$ with $`\sigma\in\{1.5,4\}`$
frames, and seven wave packets at frequencies $`f_{k}`$ log-spaced from
0.03 to 0.4 cycles per frame, each as a cosine and a sine,

```math
\phi^{\cos}_{k}[\tau]\propto e^{-\tau^{2}/2\sigma_{k}^{2}}\cos(2\pi f_{k}\tau),\quad\phi^{\sin}_{k}[\tau]\propto e^{-\tau^{2}/2\sigma_{k}^{2}}\sin(2\pi f_{k}\tau),\quad\sigma_{k}=\mathrm{clip}(0.5/f_{k},\,1,\,(K-1)/2), \tag{3}
```

so that any phase is reachable by linear mixing (Figure 2). The envelope
peaks at the current frame and decays into the past, so every kernel is
causal by construction. With $`K{=}31`$ at 80 ms frames, a filter spans
2.48 s, and the wave packets are centered between 0.375 and 5 Hz, the
range of phrasal and slower syllabic modulation \[5\]. In the controlled
study, whose Gabor ablations ran at 40 ms frames, the same atoms cover
0.75 to 10 Hz.

Unlike filters whose center frequency enters nonlinearly \[38, 54\], the
kernel is linear in its coefficients. It uses 16 parameters per channel
instead of $`K`$ and is built in fp32 before being cast to the
activation type. The constraint acts as a soft smoothness prior: the
dictionary concentrates energy below about 0.4 cycles per frame. It is
not a hard limit, because the one-sided truncation leaks energy from the
highest-frequency atoms above that band. The raw dictionary is
ill-conditioned (Gram condition number about $`8\times 10^{6}`$,
effectively about 12 independent directions); all models use it as is,
and the coefficients are trained with AdamW. The gate convolution’s
coefficients start near zero with a bias of one, so that the gate starts
open; the merge convolution’s start random.

### 4.3 Training stalls of GDN-2 branches

Our first hybrid models used the standard GDN-2 configuration, a SiLU
output gate and an RMSNorm epsilon of $`10^{-5}`$. Four of the 18 causal
runs with GDN-2 layers in round 2 of our screen (Section 5.3) never left
the initial CTC plateau within an epoch, the phase in which the model
emits only blanks and the loss stays flat \[55\] (Table 4). At the first
evaluation, after 250 updates, the GDN-2 branch output had a
root-mean-square (RMS) value of 0.005 to 0.006, against 0.28 to 0.38 for
the attention branch of the same model, 50 to 60$`\times`$ smaller
(Figure 3, right). We attribute this to the output path: SiLU of a
near-zero gate pre-activation is close to zero, and the recurrence
output is small enough to fall below the normalization epsilon, so
RMSNorm does not rescale it. We hypothesize that the rest of the network
then learns to work without the recurrent branch; in the stalled seed,
the branch became louder later without the run leaving the plateau.

We replace the SiLU output gate by a sigmoid, which starts at 0.5, and
lower the RMSNorm epsilon to $`10^{-6}`$. Neither change adds
parameters. The GDN-2 branch then starts at an RMS of about 0.09, and
all 36 later causal runs with GDN-2 layers left the plateau (0 stalls
against 4 of 18 before), after 750 or 1,000 updates against 750 for the
E-Branchformer (Figure 3, left). The fix changes the stall rate; seeds
that did not stall reached similar WER with either gate. All results
after round 2 use the sigmoid gate.

### 4.4 Streaming state size

Because the recurrence, the windowed attention and the convolutions each
look back over a fixed horizon or summarize the past in a fixed-size
state, the inference state of E-Gaborformer does not depend on how long
a stream has run. Each GDN-2 head carries $`d_{k}d_{v}`$ numbers, each
attention layer a ring buffer of $`W`$ keys and values, and each
convolution its last $`K{-}1`$ inputs. Table 1 gives the totals, and
Figure 4 contrasts them with unbounded causal attention. Unlike a pure
sliding-window encoder, whose memory ends at the window, the recurrent
layers also carry context from before it.

## 5 Controlled study on LibriSpeech-100

### 5.1 Setup

All models share one pipeline and one recipe, are trained strictly
causally, and are selected and reported on the development sets only.

- •
  Data: LibriSpeech train-clean-100 \[30\] with speed perturbation (0.9,
  1.0, 1.1; 304 h per epoch); dev-clean and dev-other for evaluation.
- •
  Front end: 128 log-mel bins (25 ms window, 10 ms hop),
  SpecAugment \[32\], global mean–variance normalization, and a causal
  Conv2d stack that subsamples 8$`\times`$ to 80 ms frames.
- •
  Output and loss: a CTC output layer over 5,000 byte-pair-encoding
  (BPE) units, trained with the CTC loss alone (the three
  intermediate-CTC arms of Table 4 excepted); greedy decoding, no
  language model.
- •
  Baseline: a port of ESPnet’s \[47\] LibriSpeech-100 E-Branchformer. At
  its original front end our port has exactly ESPnet’s 26,433,928
  parameters; here it uses the same causal front end as E-Gaborformer.
- •
  Training: Muon \[17, 24\] on hidden weight matrices and AdamW \[25\]
  on all other parameters, peak learning rate $`10^{-3}`$ after 500
  warmup updates with inverse square-root decay, bf16 autocast, about
  2,330 updates per epoch on a single GPU.
- •
  E-Gaborformer: 12 layers, $`d{=}256`$, global pattern GGGS repeated
  three times, GDN-2 with 4 heads of 64, attention window 128 frames,
  Gabor kernel size 31; 27.6M parameters against 26.8M for the
  E-Branchformer.

As sanity checks, every model was able to overfit a single training
utterance, and we verified causality numerically: outputs before time
$`t`$ did not change when the input after $`t`$ was replaced by noise.

### 5.2 E-Gaborformer versus E-Branchformer

Figure 5 shows development-set WER over ten epochs and Table 2
summarizes it. E-Gaborformer leaves the initial blank plateau earlier
and stays ahead on dev-clean for most of training: it reaches a given
dev-clean WER 5 to 18% sooner, in updates, than the E-Branchformer.
After three epochs it leads by 0.9 points on dev-clean and 0.6 on
dev-other on average and is ahead in each of the three seeds (by 1.4,
0.2 and 1.2 points on dev-clean). The gap narrows with further training.
After ten epochs, both models reach 39.2% on dev-other, and
E-Gaborformer is 0.3 points lower on dev-clean (0.35 and 0.22 in the two
seeds). E-Gaborformer thus matches a causal E-Branchformer of the same
size on LibriSpeech-100 after ten epochs, with a streaming state of
about 3 MB (Section 4.4).

### 5.3 Architecture exploration

E-Gaborformer is the result of a staged architecture screen over 36
encoder configurations in about 160 training runs, all with the recipe
above and three seeds per configuration unless noted. Tables 4 and 5
list every configuration, with WER after one and three epochs and the
number of seeds that left the blank plateau within the first epoch.
Because short runs can be dominated by how quickly each arm leaves the
plateau, we compare arms by their gap over the whole run (Table 3). The
Gabor model overtakes unconstrained convolutions after the first epoch
and its lead grows steadily, from 0.5 points at 1.5 epochs to 1.2 at
three, where it is ahead or level in every seed (by 0.0, 1.2 and 2.5
points). E-Gaborformer is ahead of the E-Branchformer on dev-clean at
every evaluation after the first, and the 3:1 hybrid matches the 1:1
hybrid with half the attention layers, so we keep the leaner 3:1 stack.
The configuration selected this way carries over to production scale:
trained as a 0.6B-parameter transducer, it has 28 to 31% lower WER than
Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming on English voice-agent traffic (Section 6).

#### Round 1: bidirectional models.

We first compared the families without a causal constraint (Table 5).
The E-Branchformer was clearly best. A 12-layer GDN-2 encoder reached
23.4 / 40.8 after three epochs against 20.5 / 37.1, and every model with
GDN-2 layers or Gabor convolutions had seeds that stalled on the blank
plateau; none of the three 1:1 hybrid seeds left it within an epoch. An
orthonormal basis of the Gabor span (the raw causal dictionary has a
Gram condition number of about $`8\times 10^{6}`$) removed most stalls
in this round, but did not improve the models that trained normally. A
learning rate of $`2\times 10^{-3}`$ left four of six architectures on
the plateau, and the Conformer did not leave it within 1,250 updates
with AdamW alone; we therefore used Muon with a peak learning rate of
$`10^{-3}`$ throughout.

#### Round 2: causal models.

Under a causal constraint the ranking changed (Table 4, top). The
Conformer left the plateau in only one of three seeds and the
FastConformer only after about 2,000 updates. The E-Branchformer
remained the strongest non-recurrent model at 28.5 / 46.2. The 1:1
hybrid with Gabor convolutions in both the cgMLP gate and the merge
reached 27.4 / 45.3 at three epochs, but this is the mean of the two
seeds that left the plateau; the third stalled. This is the round in
which we diagnosed that problem (Section 4.3).

#### Round 3: sigmoid output gate.

With the sigmoid GDN-2 output gate, every seed of every configuration
trained (Table 4, middle). Replacing both Gabor convolutions by
unconstrained depthwise kernels increased WER by 1.2 points on dev-clean
and 1.1 on dev-other, and removing all attention layers increased it by
1.1 and 1.4. A 3:1 ratio of GDN-2 to attention was as accurate as 1:1
with half the attention layers. Gated attention with QK-normalization in
the attention layers \[37\] changed nothing. Intermediate CTC at layer 6
(Section 3) lowered dev-clean WER by 1.3 points after three epochs.
Figure 6 shows the training curves of the main arms.

#### Round 4: front end.

To reduce encoder compute, we decreased the sequence length by adding a
Conv2d stack to the front end. This increased the frame duration from
40 ms to 80 ms and reduced encoder compute by nearly half, while
maintaining the accuracy of the 40 ms model (27.4 / 44.8 against
27.4 / 45.2). A depthwise-separable 8$`\times`$ subsampler \[39\] was

#### Causal and bidirectional models.

The two settings rank the architectures differently. Bidirectionally,
the GDN-2 encoder trailed the E-Branchformer by 2.9 points on dev-clean
after three epochs; causally, the hybrids matched or led it, which
suggests that recurrence is especially well matched to the causal
setting that streaming requires.

## 6 A streaming transducer at scale

Once the architecture was settled on LibriSpeech, we trained it at the
scale of the recognizer behind our real-time voice agents: Hebrew and
English, telephone-quality and spontaneous speech, strictly causal or
low-lookahead decoding.

### 6.1 Model

- •
  Encoder: 18 E-Gaborformer layers, $`d{=}1024`$, feed-forward and cgMLP
  width 4096 (Gabor convolutions over 2048 channels, kernel 31, in both
  the gate and the merge), 128 mel bins, Conv2d 8$`\times`$ subsampling
  to 80 ms frames. The global stack follows the 3:1 pattern: 14 GDN-2
  layers (16 heads of 64) and 4 sliding-window attention layers (16
  heads, window 128 frames). The encoder has 598M parameters.
- •
  Transducer: an RNN-T \[7\] with a 640-dimensional joint network over a
  13,100-piece SentencePiece vocabulary plus blank. The vocabulary
  extends the 13,087-piece vocabulary of Nemotron 3.5 ASR
  Streaming \[29\] with symbols needed for written-form output (for
  example %, : and currency signs).
- •
  Hybrid prediction network (Section 6.3): four layers alternating GDN-2
  and causal token attention, 17.9M parameters including the embedding.

The full model has 626M parameters, about the size of Nemotron 3.5 ASR
Streaming (0.6B).

### 6.2 Training

Training used about 12k hours of Hebrew and 96k hours of English, with
Hebrew upweighted in sampling, speed perturbation and SpecAugment, on
three or four H200 GPUs with Muon and AdamW in bf16, with roughly
8,500 s of audio per update. The model with a two-layer GDN-2 prediction
network was trained for 115k updates, and the hybrid prediction network
of Section 6.3 for a further 44k. To shorten training, we used Nemotron
3.5 ASR Streaming as a teacher during the first 10,750 updates: its
token embedding initialized ours, and two distillation terms were added
to the RNN-T loss, one matching our encoder output to Nemotron’s
(through a learned projection) and one matching the transducer’s
blank / next-token / other probabilities on the reference alignment
lattice \[31\]. The architecture itself was fixed beforehand by the
teacher-free study of Section 5, and all later training used the RNN-T
loss alone.

### 6.3 A hybrid prediction network

We expected a purely recurrent prediction network to have difficulty
copying recent tokens such as digits, names and parts of an address, and
therefore apply the encoder’s design to the prediction network: it
alternates GDN-2 layers (10 heads of 64) with causal _token attention_
over the last 128 tokens (rotary positions, RMS-normalized queries and
keys), each in a pre-norm residual block. For streaming, each attention
layer keeps a ring buffer of 128 keys and values and a position counter
per stream. The state is therefore of fixed size and can be selected per
row in batched frame-synchronous greedy decoding, exactly like the
recurrent state. Step-by-step decoding matches the full-sequence
computation to numerical precision, including after the ring buffer
wraps.

The hybrid network replaced a two-layer GDN-2 prediction network in an
already trained model. Its first and third layers were copied from the
two trained GDN-2 layers and its attention layers initialized randomly.
With the encoder and the token embedding frozen, the new network was
first distilled \[14\] from the previous model by minimizing the
full-vocabulary KL divergence from the previous model’s output
distribution to the new one’s at every lattice node. Training then
continued with the RNN-T loss, the encoder unfrozen at a reduced
learning rate, and optimizer states carried over by parameter name.

### 6.4 Selectable lookahead

Following cache-aware streaming \[27\], the sliding-window attention
layers use a chunked mask: query $`i`$ attends to keys $`j`$ with
$`i-W<j\leq e(i)`$, where $`e(i)`$ is the last frame of the chunk of
size $`C`$ that contains $`i`$. This gives at most $`C{-}1`$ frames of
lookahead. Because the recurrent layers and the convolutions remain
strictly causal, the bound is the same in every layer and does not
accumulate with depth. During training, $`C`$ is drawn per micro-batch
from $`\{1,2,7,14\}`$ (0, 80, 480 and 1040 ms of lookahead). At
inference, the same weights run strictly causally ($`C{=}1`$) or with up
to 480 ms of lookahead ($`C{=}7`$).

### 6.6 Training efficiency

Profiling a training update of the transducer showed that most of the
time was spent on the full-vocabulary log-softmax, although the RNN-T
loss uses only two entries per lattice node. The changes in Table 8
reduce the time per update by 2.6$`\times`$ relative to torchaudio’s
RNN-T loss with an fp32 joint network, without changing the batch; all
keep the loss and gradients exact except the bf16 joint matrix
multiplications.

#### Two-class RNN-T lattice.

The RNN-T recursion needs only two log-probabilities per lattice node
$`(t,u)`$: emit the next token $`y_{u+1}`$, or emit blank. Standard
implementations nevertheless run their dynamic program over the full
$`(T,U{+}1,V{+}1)`$ log-softmax. We compute the joint logits $`z_{t,u}`$
in chunks, take
$`\ell^{y}_{t,u}=z_{t,u}[y_{u+1}]-\mathrm{lse}(z_{t,u})`$ and
$`\ell^{b}_{t,u}=z_{t,u}[\varnothing]-\mathrm{lse}(z_{t,u})`$, where
$`\mathrm{lse}`$ is the log-sum-exp over the vocabulary and
$`\varnothing`$ the blank, and run the forward-backward recursion on the
two-class lattice. Its gradients
$`\gamma^{y}=\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\ell^{y}`$ and
$`\gamma^{b}=\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\ell^{b}`$ give the logit
gradient in closed form, with $`\mathbf{e}`$ a one-hot vector,

```math
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial z_{t,u}}=\gamma^{y}_{t,u}\,\mathbf{e}_{y_{u+1}}+\gamma^{b}_{t,u}\,\mathbf{e}_{\varnothing}-\bigl(\gamma^{y}_{t,u}+\gamma^{b}_{t,u}\bigr)\,\mathrm{softmax}(z_{t,u}), \tag{4}
```

which is propagated through the joint network by matrix multiplication.
The loss and gradients are identical to those of the full lattice, and
the vocabulary-wide work reduces to one logsumexp pass and one gradient
pass. The approach is related to function merging \[22\]; unlike pruned
RNN-T \[20\], it keeps every path.

#### Aligned output layer.

With 13,101 outputs, the joint network’s matrix multiplications fell
back to slower GPU kernels. Padding the output layer to a multiple of
128 rows, with zero weights and a bias of $`-10^{4}`$ so that the
padding contributes nothing to the softmax, restores the tensor-core
kernels.

#### Toeplitz-form depthwise convolutions.

A depthwise convolution with kernel 31 performs little arithmetic, but
generic implementations issue one unaligned load per tap and output. We
write each channel’s convolution over a block of the batch as a product
with a Toeplitz matrix built from its kernel and evaluate it on tensor
cores. The input gradient is the same operation with the kernel
reversed, and the weight gradient is a small matrix product followed by
diagonal sums. For a $`(30,2048,300)`$ bf16 input, the forward and
backward passes drop from 2.1 ms to 0.54 ms.

#### Length regrouping with preserved weights.

Micro-batches are formed by a token budget and were on average only 61%
real frames. We pool the utterances of all micro-batches of one update,
sort them by length and refill the micro-batches, which raises the fill
to 80–83%. Each utterance keeps the loss weight it had in its original
micro-batch, so the update is unchanged; a unit test verifies equality
to $`10^{-4}`$.

With live data on three GPUs, new tensor shapes also triggered per-shape
setup work and host–device synchronizations stalled the GPUs. Padding
lengths to multiples of 1.28 s, running the subsampling convolutions in
fixed batch chunks, computing sequence lengths on the host and preparing
the next update in a background thread removed these stalls.

## 7 Conclusion

E-Gaborformer shows that a mostly recurrent, strictly causal encoder is
a strong foundation for real-time speech recognition. On LibriSpeech-100
it matched or beat a causal E-Branchformer of the same size (20.5 / 39.2
against 20.8 / 39.2 after ten epochs) and reached a given WER up to 18%
sooner, with a constant streaming state of about 3 MB. Three ingredients
made this work: a few attention layers for exact recall, a GDN-2 output
gate that keeps the recurrent branch active from the first update (0 of
36 runs stalled, against 4 of 18), and Gabor-dictionary convolutions,
which led unconstrained ones by 1.2 points at three epochs in the hybrid
encoder. The design extends naturally to the transducer’s prediction
network and scales to a 0.6B-parameter Hebrew and English recognizer
that serves several lookaheads from one set of weights, keeps a constant
19 MB state per call, trains 2.6$`\times`$ faster with an exact
objective, and has 28 to 31% lower WER than Nemotron 3.5 ASR Streaming
on English voice-agent traffic.

Natural next steps are longer controlled training runs, emission-latency
measurements, and evaluation on multi-minute streams, where a
constant-size state is most valuable.
