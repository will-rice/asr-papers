###### Abstract

Transducer neural networks have emerged as the mainstream approach for streaming automatic speech recognition (ASR), offering state-of-the-art performance in balancing accuracy and latency. In the conventional framework, streaming transducer models are trained to maximize the likelihood function based on non-streaming recursion rules. However, this approach leads to a mismatch between training and inference, resulting in the issue of deformed likelihood and consequently suboptimal ASR accuracy. We introduce a mathematical quantification of the gap between the actual likelihood and the deformed likelihood, namely forward variable causal compensation (FoCC). We also present its estimator, FoCCE, as a solution to estimate the exact likelihood. Through experiments on the LibriSpeech dataset, we show that FoCCE training improves the accuracy of the streaming transducers.

###### Index Terms:

RNN-transducer (RNN-T), transducer neural network, automatic speech recognition (ASR), streaming ASR

## I Introduction

Modern automatic speech recognition (ASR) has witnessed substantial accuracy improvements, primarily attributed to advances in deep learning. While the pursuit of higher accuracy in general ASR remains a priority, recent studies have increasingly emphasized the need to maintain accuracy in challenging scenarios, including spoken named entities \[[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib1), [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib2)\], multi-lingual speech \[[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib3), [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib4)\], and streaming ASR \[[5](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib5), [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib6), [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib7)\]. Notably, the rising demand for on-device and real-time ASR underscores the importance of streaming ASR.

The accuracy of streaming ASR is degraded compared to its non-streaming counterpart, especially when the model is restricted to have low latency. Two separate causes induce the accuracy degradation of the streaming model. The primary cause is information deficiency; the streaming model predicts an output based on a limited input context, whilst the non-streaming model has the advantage of being aware of the entire context. Information deficiency is an inevitable intrinsic property of streaming ASR. The second cause is deformed likelihood. Deep learning ASR models are usually trained based on the maximum likelihood criterion \[[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib8), [9](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib9), [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib10), [11](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib11), [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib12), [13](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib13), [14](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib14), [15](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib15), [16](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib16)\]. Mainstream ASR approaches \[[9](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib9), [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib10), [12](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib12)\] commonly define likelihood by breaking it into local probability terms that are estimated using neural networks with a softmax output layer. These local probabilities are designed to sum up to the likelihood when all local probabilities are modeled with the entire input context, which is the case for non-streaming ASR but not valid for streaming ASR \[[16](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib16)\]. Consequently, naively utilizing mainstream ASR likelihood functions for training the streaming models leads to the deformed likelihood problem.

Several approaches \[[14](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib14), [16](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib16)\] adopt a globally normalized likelihood definition, which bypasses the issue of deformed likelihood in streaming ASR. These methods define globally normalized likelihood as a ratio between the score-sum of accepting paths (corresponding to ground truth data) and the score-sum of all possible paths. By excluding local probability terms, these approaches avoid the deformed likelihood problem. However, they exhibit lower accuracy compared to local probability-based mainstream ASR methods in both streaming and non-streaming scenarios. This limitation restricts their application, as globally normalized likelihood is incompatible with local probability-based models.

Transducer neural networks \[[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib10)\], often called RNN-T, have recently emerged as the dominant approach for streaming ASR, offering a state-of-the-art tradeoff between accuracy and latency. The prevalent paradigm in streaming transducer models relies on a likelihood function derived from the non-streaming recursion rule. Training a transducer with this naive likelihood induces the deformed likelihood problem, resulting in sub-optimal ASR accuracy.

This letter introduces a mathematical perspective on the deformed likelihood problem in streaming transducer training and proposes a novel solution to mitigate it. The key contributions of this paper are as follows:

- •
  We reframe the dynamic programming for the non-streaming transducer likelihood \[[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib10)\] using detailed probabilistic notation and demonstrate its mismatch to a streaming model due to the deformed likelihood problem.
- •
  We quantify the gap between the deformed and the actual likelihood in streaming transducer training, namely “Forward Variable Causal Compensation” (FoCC).
- •
  We propose the FoCC estimator (FoCCE) network, which estimates the actual likelihood instead of the deformed likelihood in streaming transducer training.
- •
  We experimentally show that FoCCE training improves the streaming transducers’ ASR accuracy on the LibriSpeech dataset, reducing the accuracy gap between streaming and non-streaming transducers.

## II Transducer Neural Networks

### II-A Non-streaming Transducer

Given an input sequence $`\mathbf{x}_{1:T}`$ of length $`T`$ and the corresponding target sequence $`\mathbf{y}_{0:U}`$ of length $`U`$ ($`y_{u} \in {\mathbb{N}}`$, $`y_{0}`$ is the start-of-sequence token $`\langle{sos}\rangle`$), a transducer neural network \[[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib10)\] parametrized by $`\theta`$ computes the conditional likelihood $`L_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}`$ as

```math
\begin{array}{r}
{{{L_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}}:={{\log P_{\theta}}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:U},z_{U}} \leq {T{<z_{U + 1}|}\mathbf{x}_{1:T}}})}}},}
\end{array}
```

```math
\mathbf{f}_{1:T} = {{Encoder}_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T})}}
```

where the latent alignment variable $`z_{u} \in {\mathbb{N}}`$ is defined such that $`z_{u} = t`$ means that the target $`y_{u}`$ is aligned to the encoded input $`f_{t}`$. $`{Encoder}_{\theta}{( \cdot )}`$ is a neural network that extracts abstract information from the entire input sequence. The encoder may subsample its input, i.e., $`\mathbf{f}_{1:T} = {{Encoder}_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T'})}}`$ where $`T < T'`$, to reduce the length mismatch between input and target. As any long input sequence can be reshaped into a chunked sequence that has the same length as the encoded sequence, i.e., $`x_{1:T'} = x_{1:T}'`$, we denote the input as $`x_{1:T}`$ for simplicity.

Since the alignments between the inputs and targets are assumed to be monotonic, $`\mathbf{z}_{0:U}`$ is constrained such that

```math
{z_{0} \leq 1 \leq z_{1} \leq z_{2} \leq z_{3} \leq \cdots \leq z_{U}}.
```

The end condition $`z_{U} \leq T < z_{U + 1}`$ in ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E1 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) indicates that the entire target $`\mathbf{y}_{1:U}`$ is aligned to the input $`\mathbf{x}_{1:T}`$ whilst the next target $`y_{U + 1}`$ is not (i.e., only $`\mathbf{y}_{0:U}`$ is included in the inputs).

Dynamic programming is used to obtain $`L_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}`$ in $`\mathcal{O}{({TU})}`$ computations. The forward variable is defined as

```math
{{\alpha_{\theta}{(t,u)}}:={P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \leq t \leq \left. z_{u + 1} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:T} \right.})}}}.
```

From ([3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E3 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) and ([4](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E4 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), at the initial point $`{\alpha_{\theta}{(1,0)}} = {P_{\theta}{({y_{0} = \left. {\langle{sos}\rangle} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:T} \right.})}} = 1`$ and at the boundaries $`{\alpha_{\theta}{(t,{- 1})}} = {\alpha_{\theta}{(0,u)}} = 0`$ for $`t \in {\lbrack 1,T\rbrack}`$ and $`u \in {\lbrack 0,U\rbrack}`$. Local probabilities in $`x`$-axis and $`y`$-axis are respectively defined as

```math
\begin{aligned}
{\phi_{\theta}{(t,u)}:=P_{\theta}{(z_{u + 1} \geq t + 1|}} & {\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u} \leq t \leq z_{u + 1}),} \\
{Y_{\theta}{(t,u)}:=P_{\theta}{(y_{u + 1},z_{u + 1} = t|}} & {\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u} \leq t \leq z_{u + 1}),}
\end{aligned}
```

for $`{t \in {\lbrack 1,T\rbrack}},{u \in {\lbrack 0,U\rbrack}}`$, which is to be estimated by neural networks. With blank probability $`\phi_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ and label probability $`Y_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$, the forward variable can be recursively computed as

```math
\begin{array}{cl}
{{\alpha_{\theta}{(t,u)}} =} & {P_{\theta}{(\mathbf{y}_{0:u},{z_{u}{<{t \leq z_{u + 1}}|}\mathbf{x}_{1:T}})}} \\
 & {+ {P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} = t \leq \left. z_{u + 1} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:T} \right.})}}} \\
 = & {P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \leq {t - 1} \leq \left. z_{u + 1} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:T} \right.})}} \\
 & {\cdot {P_{\theta}{({z_{u + 1} \geq \left. t \middle| {\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \right. \leq {t - 1} \leq z_{u + 1}})}}} \\
 & {+ {P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:{u - 1}},z_{u - 1}} \leq t \leq \left. z_{u} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:T} \right.})}}} \\
 & {\cdot {P_{\theta}{({{y_{u},z_{u}} = \left. t \middle| {\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:{u - 1}},z_{u - 1}} \right. \leq t \leq z_{u}})}}} \\
 = & {{{\alpha_{\theta}{({t - 1},u)}\phi_{\theta}{({t - 1},u)}} + {\alpha_{\theta}{(t,{u - 1})}Y_{\theta}{(t,{u - 1})}}},}
\end{array}
```

according to ([3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E3 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")). The conditional likelihood in ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E1 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) can be obtained by

```math
{{L_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}} = {{\log\alpha_{\theta}}{(T,U)}\phi_{\theta}{(T,U)}}},
```

which is used as a training objective.

In this subsection, we introduce the alignment variable $`z_{u}`$ separately from the target label variable $`y_{u}`$. This separation clarifies the Bayes’ rule within the transducer recursion, as shown in ([6](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E6 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")). We found that the forward-backward algorithm \[[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib10)\], which is a training method for transducer networks in most prior studies, does not obey Bayes’ rule. Therefore this paper focuses on the training based on the forward variable recursion rather than the forward-backward algorithm.

### II-B Streaming Transducer

In the streaming case, as depicted on the left of Figure [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition"), a transducer network $`\theta`$ estimates the local probabilities at the $`t`$-th timestep using only limited input context $`\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}}`$:

```math
{\mathbf{f}_{1:t} = {{CausalEncoder}_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}})}}},
```

```math
\begin{aligned}
{{\overset{\sim}{\phi}}_{\theta}{(t,u)}:=P_{\theta}{(z_{u + 1} \geq t + 1|}} & {\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}},\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u} \leq t \leq z_{u + 1}),} \\
{{\overset{\sim}{Y}}_{\theta}{(t,u)}:=P_{\theta}{(y_{u + 1},z_{u + 1} = t|}} & {\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}},\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u} \leq t \leq z_{u + 1}),}
\end{aligned}
```

where the $`{CausalEncoder}_{\theta}{( \cdot )}`$ is similar to the $`{Encoder}_{\theta}{( \cdot )}`$ in ([2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E2 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), except that its input context is limited by a context-end function $`e:{{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}}`$. In general, $`{e{(t)}} = {\min{(T,{{C{\lceil{t/C}\rceil}} + R})}}`$ with chunk size $`C`$ and right context offset $`R`$.

The streaming transducer network (left) and the FoCCE network (middle) respectively estimate the local probabilities and FoCC values, which are used to estimate the actual likelihood by the modified forward variable recursion rule (red boxes on the right).

Conventional methods train streaming transducer models by maximizing the likelihood given by ([7](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E7 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), with local probabilities in ([6](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E6 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) being substituted by ([9](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E9 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")). This naive approach breaks Bayes’ rule, thereby training models with deformed likelihood. To obtain the actual likelihood, we introduce a probability ratio, namely Forward Variable Causal Compensation (FoCC):

```math
\begin{aligned}
{{\gamma_{\theta}{(t,u)}}:=} & \frac{P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \leq {t{<z_{u + 1}|}\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{({t + 1})}}}}})}}{P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \leq {t{<z_{u + 1}|}\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}}}})}} \\
 = & {\frac{P_{\theta}{({\left. \mathbf{x}_{{{e{(t)}} + 1}:{e{({t + 1})}}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}},\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \right. \leq t < z_{u + 1}})}}{P_{\theta}{(\left. \mathbf{x}_{{{e{(t)}} + 1}:{e{({t + 1})}}} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}} \right.)}}.}
\end{aligned}
```

Note that if $`t`$ and $`t + 1`$ are in the same encoder chunk, i.e., $`{e{(t)}} = {e{({t + 1})}}`$, then $`{\gamma_{\theta}{(t, \cdot )}} = 1`$ as shown in ([10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E10 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")). Thus, FoCC needs to be calculated only for $`t:{{e{(t)}} < {e{({t + 1})}}}`$.

The modified recursion rule can be formulated with the streaming forward variable, which is defined as

```math
\begin{array}{cl}
{{{\overset{\sim}{\alpha}}_{\theta}{(t,u)}}:=} & {P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \leq t \leq \left. z_{u + 1} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}} \right.})}} \\
 = & {P_{\theta}{(\mathbf{y}_{0:u},{z_{u}{<{t \leq z_{u + 1}}|}\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{({t - 1})}}}})}\gamma_{\theta}{({t - 1},u)}} \\
 & {+ {P_{\theta}{({{\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} = t \leq \left. z_{u + 1} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}} \right.})}}} \\
 = & {{\overset{\sim}{\alpha}}_{\theta}{({t - 1},u)}{\overset{\sim}{\phi}}_{\theta}{({t - 1},u)}\gamma_{\theta}{({t - 1},u)}} \\
 & {{+ {{\overset{\sim}{\alpha}}_{\theta}{(t,{u - 1})}{\overset{\sim}{Y}}_{\theta}{(t,{u - 1})}}}.}
\end{array}
```

According to ([1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E1 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), ([9](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E9 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), and ([11](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E11 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), the actual likelihood is

```math
{{L_{\theta}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}} = {{\log{\overset{\sim}{\alpha}}_{\theta}}{(T,U)}{\overset{\sim}{\phi}}_{\theta}{(T,U)}}}.
```

## III Forward Variable Causal Compensation Estimator (FoCCE)

FoCC values $`\gamma_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ defined in ([10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E10 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) are necessary for determining the actual likelihood in streaming transducers, though we could not find any analytic solution to access the probability terms in ([10](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E10 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) based on the transducer network outputs $`{\overset{\sim}{Y}}_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ and $`{\overset{\sim}{\phi}}_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$. For this reason, we propose to approximate $`\gamma_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ with $`\gamma_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ using a separate FoCC estimator (FoCCE) network parametrized by $`\omega`$.

Learning the probability ratio $`\gamma_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ with a neural network is challenging, so we split it into two probability densities $`\chi_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$, $`{\overline{\chi}}_{\omega}{( \cdot )}`$ and let the FoCCE network estimate them:

```math
{\gamma_{\omega}{(t,u)}}:=\left( \frac{\chi_{\omega}{(t,u)}}{{\overline{\chi}}_{\omega}{(t)}} \right)^{\lambda_{\gamma}}
```

where

```math
\begin{aligned}
{{\chi_{\omega}{(t,u)}}:=} & {{P_{\omega}{({\left. \mathbf{x}_{{{e{(t)}} + 1}:{e{({t + 1})}}} \middle| {\mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}},\mathbf{y}_{0:u},z_{u}} \right. \leq t < z_{u + 1}})}},} \\
{{{\overline{\chi}}_{\omega}{(t)}}:=} & {P_{\omega}{(\left. \mathbf{x}_{{{e{(t)}} + 1}:{e{({t + 1})}}} \middle| \mathbf{x}_{1:{e{(t)}}} \right.)}}
\end{aligned}
```

for $`{t \in {\lbrack 1,T)}},{u \in {\lbrack 0,U\rbrack}}`$, and $`\lambda_{\gamma}`$ is a non-negative scaling factor for FoCCE. Intuitively, $`{\overline{\chi}}_{\omega}{( \cdot )}`$ is a probability density of next-chunk input features given the current history of the input sequence (similar to autoregressive predictive coding \[[17](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib17)\]), while the density $`\chi_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ is also conditioned on the target sequence history. With enough model capacity, it is assumed that both $`\gamma_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ and $`\gamma_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ converge to the true probability ratio $`\gamma{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ as training progresses, therefore $`{\gamma_{\theta}{( \cdot , \cdot )}} \approx {\gamma_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}}`$ for well-trained models $`\theta`$ and $`\omega`$.

The probability densities $`\chi_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$, $`{\overline{\chi}}_{\omega}{( \cdot )}`$ in ([14](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S3.E14 "In III Forward Variable Causal Compensation Estimator (FoCCE) ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) are estimated by the FoCCE network $`\omega`$, as illustrated in the middle of Figure [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition"). The architecture of this network is adapted from the conventional transducer networks, as shown below:

```math
\begin{aligned}
{{\chi_{\omega}{(t,u)}} =} & {{{DensityEstimator}_{\omega}^{\chi}{({\lbrack f_{t}^{\chi};g_{u}^{\chi}\rbrack})}},} \\
{{{\overline{\chi}}_{\omega}{(t)}} =} & {{{DensityEstimator}_{\omega}^{\chi}{({\lbrack f_{t}^{\chi};\overset{\rightarrow}{0}\rbrack})}},}
\end{aligned}
```

```math
\begin{aligned}
{f_{t}^{\chi} =} & {{{CausalEncoder}_{\omega}^{\chi}{(x_{1:{e{(t)}}})}},} \\
{g_{u}^{\chi} =} & {{{Predictor}_{\omega}^{\chi}{(y_{0:u})}},}
\end{aligned}
```

where $`\lbrack \cdot ; \cdot \rbrack`$ denotes the concatenation, and $`\overset{\rightarrow}{0}`$ stands for a zero vector. The $`{CausalEncoder}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$ and $`{Predictor}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$ respectively mean an encoder and a prediction network that function similarly to those of a transducer network. The $`{CausalEncoder}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$ operates causally, just like the $`{CausalEncoder}_{\theta}{( \cdot )}`$ described in ([8](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E8 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), using the same context-end function $`e{( \cdot )}`$. In contrast to the joiner of a transducer network, the $`{DensityEstimator}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$ models probability densities in continuous space. To model arbitrary densities, we utilized normalizing flows \[[18](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib18)\] to implement $`{DensityEstimator}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$.

The FoCCE network $`\omega`$ is trained independently from the transducer model, maximizing the objective

```math
{{L_{\omega}^{\chi}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}}:={\sum\limits_{t = 1}^{T - 1}{({{{\overline{\chi}}_{\omega}{(t)}} + {\frac{1}{U}{\sum\limits_{u = 1}^{U}{\chi_{\omega}{(t,u)}}}}})}}}.
```

Based on the FoCC estimation $`\gamma_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$, a streaming transducer network $`\theta`$ is trained to maximize the modified likelihood:

```math
{{L_{\theta,\omega}^{mod}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}}:={{\log{\hat{\alpha}}_{\theta,\omega}}{(T,U)}{\overset{\sim}{\phi}}_{\theta}{(T,U)}}},
```

```math
\begin{array}{cl}
{{{\hat{\alpha}}_{\theta,\omega}{(t,u)}}:=} & {{\hat{\alpha}}_{\theta,\omega}{({t - 1},u)}{\overset{\sim}{\phi}}_{\theta}{({t - 1},u)}{sg}\left( {\gamma_{\omega}{({t - 1},u)}} \right)} \\
 & {{+ {{\hat{\alpha}}_{\theta,\omega}{(t,{u - 1})}{\overset{\sim}{Y}}_{\theta}{(t,{u - 1})}}},}
\end{array}
```

with the initial and boundary values the same as $`\alpha_{\theta}{(t,u)}`$. The modified likelihood in ([18](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S3.E18 "In III Forward Variable Causal Compensation Estimator (FoCCE) ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) approximates the actual likelihood in ([12](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E12 "In II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), mitigating the deformed likelihood problem. The right side of Figure [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ II-B Streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition") depicts the modified forward variable recursion rule described in ([19](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S3.E19 "In III Forward Variable Causal Compensation Estimator (FoCCE) ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")). In this rule, the stop-gradient operator $`{sg}{( \cdot )}`$ is applied to $`\gamma_{\omega}{( \cdot , \cdot )}`$ to prevent the divergence of parameter values. Note that from ([13](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S3.E13 "In III Forward Variable Causal Compensation Estimator (FoCCE) ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")), the modified streaming recursion in ([19](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S3.E19 "In III Forward Variable Causal Compensation Estimator (FoCCE) ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) can be smoothly transitioned into the conventional recursion in ([6](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S2.E6 "In II-A Non-streaming Transducer ‣ II Transducer Neural Networks ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition")) by setting $`\lambda_{\gamma}`$ close to $`0`$.

The whole training objective is given by

```math
\begin{array}{cl}
{{L_{\theta,\omega}^{tot}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}}:=} & {\lambda_{mod}L_{\theta,\omega}^{mod}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}} \\
 & {{+ {\lambda\chi L_{\omega}^{\chi}{(\mathbf{x}_{1:T},\mathbf{y}_{0:U})}}}.}
\end{array}
```

Both the transducer network $`\theta`$ and the FoCCE network $`\omega`$ are learned to maximize $`{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}} \sim {\mathbb{D}}}L_{\theta,\omega}^{tot}{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})}`$ given a training set $`\mathbb{D}`$. Note that the gradient backpropagated from the FoCCE network does not directly affect the transducer network. The transducer network is optimized only to maximize the modified likelihood.

## IV Experimental Results

### IV-A Experimental Setting

We followed the icefall \[[19](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib19)\] framework to train and evaluate the transducer models.

#### IV-A1 Data Preparation

We conducted experiments on the LibriSpeech \[[20](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib20)\] and the TED-LIUM3 \[[21](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib21)\] datasets, with the designated training, validation, and evaluation sets. We transformed speech waveforms into $`80`$-D log mel filterbank energies using a $`25`$ ms Hanning window with a $`10`$ ms stride, which were applied as input for the encoders. The text data was encoded using a byte-pair encoding (BPE) \[[22](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib22), [23](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib23)\], resulting in $`500`$ subword units, which were used as input for predictors.

#### IV-A2 Neural Network Architecture

For the transducer network, we employed the Zipformer \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib24)\] from an existing recipe¹¹1The Zipformer architecture and the training recipe can be found at https://github.com/k2-fsa/icefall/blob/master/egs/librispeech/ASR/zipformer. The small-scaled model recipe is used in this paper.. The Zipformer consists of an encoder with a $`4 \times`$ total subsampling rate, a stateless prediction network \[[25](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib25)\], and a joint network followed by a softmax layer. From the recipe, we made a few modifications to the Zipformer small block parameters: the number of small blocks in each block to $`2`$, feedforward dimension to $`768`$, encoder dimension to $`256`$, and encoder unmasked dimension to $`192`$. For the $`{CausalEncoder}_{\theta}{( \cdot )}`$, we used an attentional block chunk size of $`8`$, resulting in $`160`$ ms of encoder latency.

The FoCCE network $`\omega`$ comprises three main components:

- •
  $`{CausalEncoder}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$: eight stacks of chunk-wise causal convolution modules, which are identical to those in the Zipformer encoder\footreffootnote:recipe but with a kernel size of 9.
- •
  $`{Predictor}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$: a $`128`$-D LSTM layer.
- •
  $`{DensityEstimator}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$: masked autoregressive flow (MAF) \[[26](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib26)\] with a flow depth of $`1`$, two neural blocks per each depth, and a hidden dimension of $`160`$.

To align the chunk boundaries of encoders of the FoCCE network and the transducer network, we stacked the acoustic features into $`4 \times`$ stacked features along the time-axis so that their dimension is $`320`$, which were processed by $`{CausalEncoder}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$ to generate a causal context vector. This vector, in combination with the output from $`{Predictor}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$, acts as the condition for the MAF network $`{DensityEstimator}_{\omega}^{\chi}{( \cdot )}`$; Our model employs a standard Gaussian distribution as the prior.

#### IV-A3 Training

We trained the models for 40 epochs to ensure convergence. We applied SpecAugment \[[27](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#bib.bib27)\]\footreffootnote:recipe to the acoustic features exclusively for the transducer encoder input. We experimentally determined the FoCCE hyperparameters that minimized WERs such that $`\lambda_{\gamma} = 0.05`$, $`\lambda_{mod} = 1`$, and $`\lambda_{\chi} = 0.01`$. For all the experiments of streaming ASR, we used the identical parameter setting for the transducer network. Therefore all transducers have the same computational footprints at inference, and their performances are affected only by the likelihood estimation at training.

#### IV-A4 Evaluation

We assessed the performance of the transducer models in terms of word error rates (WER) using a beam search algorithm with a beam size of $`4`$. We incorporated a left context of 256 frames while evaluating streaming models.

### IV-B Accuracy Improvement by FoCCE training

Table [I](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17537v1#S3.T1 "TABLE I ‣ III Forward Variable Causal Compensation Estimator (FoCCE) ‣ Towards Maximum Likelihood Training for Transducer-based Streaming Speech Recognition") displays the WERs for Zipformer transducers trained using different methods. In LibriSpeech, FoCCE training on streaming transducers resulted in lower WERs, which amounts to $`26.3`$% in test-clean and $`12.3`$% in test-other of WER gaps between non-streaming and streaming baselines. FoCCE training also reduced the streaming and non-streaming WER gap by $`17.7`$% in TED-LIUM3 test set. The extent of WER improvement was sensitive to the hyperparameter $`\lambda_{\gamma}`$, which arises from the fact that $`\lambda_{\gamma}`$ is calculated based on the division of two probability densities in continuous feature space, rather than two probabilities in discrete output space.

## V Future Work

We introduce FoCCE, the estimator for FoCC, that helps mitigate the deformed likelihood problem. Our experiments show that FoCCE reduces WERs in streaming transducer training. Future research should focus on refining the estimation process for FoCCE.
