# Response to Reviewer \# 3

- This paper introduces a new supervised pre-training approach for text-based emotion recognition guided by a LLM. The model, MERITS-L, utilizes a hierarchical training method for emotion recognition in conversations, combining speech and text modalities, and achieves superior performance on two of the three datasets evaluated.\
  <span style="color: blue"> Thank you for the comments. However, we fail to understand the reviewer’s remark of minor originality even though the reviewer notes indicate the novelty aptly.</span>

- The term “LLM-guided text pre-training" is ambiguous. From my reading, it appears the authors fine-tuned a RoBERTa model using LLM-labeled data. If the authors intend to convey that supervised data was used for pre-training, wouldn’t "fine-tuning" be a clearer term?\
  <span style="color: blue">The term LLM guidance is used to indicate that the unsupervised data was automatically labeled (for sentiment class) using LLMs, which then provided pseudo-labels for pre-training the RoBERTa model. As fine-tuning is done typically on human-labeled datasets, we prefer to call this difference out as LLM “teacher” guided training.</span>

- How did the authors ensure the annotation quality of GPT-3.5-turbo?...Its performance in many emotion classification tasks is often outperformed by fine-tuned models such as Llama3. Why not enhance data quality through approaches like voting or human proofreading?\
  <span style="color: blue">The performance comparison suggested by the reviewer was already provided in Sec. **Sec.IV.D** of the paper. The accuracy of $`52.98\%`$ is observed for GPT-3.5 Turbo while LLaMA has an overlap of $`50.91\%`$ with the ground truth labels provided in the pre-training dataset. This justified the use of GPT-3.5 model. We also prefer not to use human proofreading, as the main motivation of the work is to utilize unsupervised data for model training using automated pseudo-labels.</span>

- Does the MERITS-L model achieve the best results on the MELD dataset? It appears that models like FacialMMT-RoBERTa report higher F1 scores (including some recent under-reviewing papers with superior results).\
  <span style="color: blue">We respectfully disagree with the reviewer on comments. The paper mentioned uses the **visual modality along with speech and text along with the speaker information** and thus comparison with MERITS-L, which uses only speech and text modalities, is unfair. Further, we are unable to infer what works have been mentioned by the reviewer as “recent under-reviewing papers". At the time of ICASSP writing, we had performed a thorough literature review before making the claims. Since no direct references have been given by the reviewer, we continue to hold on to the claims made in the paper of “MERITS-L achieves the best performance when compared with SoTA models for MELD and CMU-MOSI” among **speech-text models not using speaker information.**</span>

- <span style="color: blue">All other minor modifications, as suggested by the reviewer, will be performed in the camera-ready version of the paper. </span>
