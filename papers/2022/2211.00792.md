# Introduction

In the field of natural language processing (NLP), language model (LM) pre-training has achieved remarkable success and become a dominant paradigm. With well-designed self-supervised objectives and a dramatic increase in model capacity, large-scale LMs  are pre-trained on a vast amount of text-only data to acquire versatile linguistic knowledge . Such pre-trained LMs (PLMs) provide rich representations for boosting the performance of downstream NLP tasks while alleviating the heavy requirement of supervised data.

Inspired by the great success in NLP, there has been an increasing interest in adopting a PLM for end-to-end automatic speech recognition (E2E-ASR). Several works have introduced PLMs to E2E-ASR via N-best hypothesis rescoring  and knowledge distillation . More recently, others have attempted to fine-tune a PLM directly for E2E-ASR . This enables a model to exploit the LM’s powerful representations during training and inference while requiring a complex mechanism for bridging the speech-text gap and careful adjustment for stabilizing the fine-tuning process.

BERT-CTC is another possibility we have explored for incorporating a pre-trained masked LM, i.e., BERT, into the formulation of connectionist temporal classification (CTC) . BERT-CTC efficiently uses BERT _without fine-tuning_ to explicitly condition CTC on context-aware linguistic information, alleviating the conditional independence in the output dependency. While BERT-CTC has shown promising results, its performance is still limited due to a discrepancy in its output vocabulary. BERT-CTC predicts a sequence via a masked LM-based iterative refinement algorithm  (e.g., Table <a href="#tb:decoding_example" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tb:decoding_example">[tb:decoding_example]</a>) with repeated updates on BERT embeddings. This BERT-based refinement forces the model to be trained on the BERT vocabulary, which is often too large for ASR training and is likely to have a mismatch against a target ASR domain. In fact, as shown in , the BERT-CTC performance becomes less significant when compared with standard CTC and transducer-based models trained on a vocabulary constructed from ASR training text. A similar issue has been reported in , which has mentioned that alphabetic languages (e.g., English) are prone to the vocabulary mismatch due to the difference in subword units.

In this work, we propose **BE**RT-**C**TC-**Tra**nsducer (BECTRA), an extension of BERT-CTC that is capable of handling the vocabulary mismatch and exploiting BERT knowledge for improving ASR performance. BECTRA formulates E2E-ASR based on the transducer , which comprises an encoder enhanced by BERT-CTC and a decoder (i.e., prediction/joint networks) trained with an ASR-specific vocabulary. Such a separate decoder enables a model to learn text generation more accurately in a suitable output unit, still benefiting from the BERT-conditioned framework. Moreover, BECTRA models E2E-ASR in a more accurate formulation than BERT-CTC, thanks to the transducer’s autoregressive characteristic relying less on conditional independence assumptions. With the combination of the transducer and BERT-CTC, we also propose a novel inference algorithm for BECTRA, which takes advantage of both autoregressive and non-autoregressive decoding algorithms.

# Background: BERT-CTC for end-to-end ASR

Let $`O\!=\!(\bm{\mathrm{o}}_t\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^F| t\!=\!1,\cdots,T)`$ be an input sequence of length $`T`$, and $`W^{\mathsf{b}} \!=\!( w^{\mathsf{b}}_n\!\in\!\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}} | n\!=\!1,\cdots,N )`$ be the corresponding output sequence of length $`N`$, where $`\bm{\mathrm{o}}_t`$ is an $`F`$-dimensional acoustic feature at frame $`t`$, $`w^{\mathsf{b}}_n`$ is an output token at position $`n`$, and $`\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ is a vocabulary of BERT  trained on a target language. Note that the superscript ($`{\mathsf{b}}`$) indicates the use of the BERT vocabulary. The objective of E2E-ASR is to formulate the direct mapping from $`O`$ to $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$ by modeling a posterior distribution $`p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|O)`$ using a single deep neural network.

BERT-CTC  formulates $`p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|O)`$ by introducing a masked sequence $`\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}\!=\!(\tilde{w}^{\mathsf{b}}_n\!\in\!\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}} \cup \{\varnothing\} | n\!=\!1,\cdots,N)`$, which is obtained by replacing some tokens in an output sequence $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$ with a special mask token $`\varnothing`$ (see Table <a href="#tb:decoding_example" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tb:decoding_example">[tb:decoding_example]</a> for example). Considering all possible masking patterns compatible with $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$, $`p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|O)`$ is factorized as

```math
\begin{equation}
    p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|O) = \sum_{\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}} p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}},O) p(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}|O). \label{eq:p_bc_W_O}
\end{equation}
```

Similar to CTC , $`p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}},O)`$ in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_O">[eq:p_bc_W_O]</a> is marginalized over all possible alignment paths between $`O`$ and $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$ as

```math
\begin{equation}
    p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}},O) \approx
    \sum_{A^{\mathsf{b}}\in\mathcal{B}^{\text{--}1}(W^{\mathsf{b}})} p(A^{\mathsf{b}}|W^{\mathsf{b}},O) p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),
    \label{eq:p_bc_W_tW_O}
\end{equation}
```

where $`A^{\mathsf{b}}\!=\!(a^{\mathsf{b}}_t\!\in\!\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}} \cup \{\epsilon\} | t\!=\!1,\cdots,T)`$ is a frame-level token sequence obtained by augmenting $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$ with a blank symbol $`\epsilon`$, and $`\mathcal{B}:A^{\mathsf{b}}\!\mapsto\!W^{\mathsf{b}}`$ is the collapsing function  that removes token repetitions and blank symbols from $`A^{\mathsf{b}}`$. To obtain Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O]</a>, BERT-CTC assumes reasonable conditional independence of $`\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ and $`O`$. The alignment probability $`p(A^{\mathsf{b}}|W^{\mathsf{b}},O)`$ is further factorized using the probabilistic chain rule as

```math
\begin{equation}
    \text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:p_bc_W_tW_O}}
    \approx \sum_{A^{\mathsf{b}}} \prod_{t=1}^{T} p(a^{\mathsf{b}}_t|\cancel{a^{\mathsf{b}}_1,\cdots,a^{\mathsf{b}}_{t-1}},W^{\mathsf{b}},O) p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),
    \label{eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2}
\end{equation}
```

which makes the same conditional independence assumption identical to CTC. Assuming $`p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}})`$ as a strong prior probability modeled by a pre-trained masked LM, BERT-CTC models the product of $`p(a^{\mathsf{b}}_t|W^{\mathsf{b}},O)`$ and $`p(W^{\mathsf{b}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}})`$ in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2]</a> as

```math
\begin{equation}
    \text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2}} \triangleq \sum_{A^{\mathsf{b}}} \prod_{t} p(a^{\mathsf{b}}_t|\text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),O), \label{eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3}
\end{equation}
```

where $`\text{BERT}(\cdot)`$ indicates contextual embedding obtained from the output of BERT . Different from standard CTC formulation, in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3]</a>, BERT-CTC conditions the token emission probability on BERT’s contextual linguistic information. This enables a model to not only utilize BERT knowledge for E2E-ASR but also explicitly relax the conditional independence assumption made in CTC. For more detailed derivation from Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O]</a> to Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3]</a>, see Sec. 3 of .

The blue region in Fig. <a href="#fig:bectra" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:bectra">1</a> depicts the BERT-CTC architecture, consisting of an audio encoder, BERT, and a concatenation network. The token emission probability in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3]</a> is computed as

```math
\begin{align}
    &\hspace{-0.06cm}p(a^{\mathsf{b}}_t|\text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),O) = \sigma(\text{ConcatNet}_t(E, H)) \in [0, 1]^{|\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}}|+1}, \hspace{-0.02cm} \label{eq:p_bc_at} \\
    &\hspace{-0.06cm}E = \text{AudioEnc}(O) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times D},\ \ H = \text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D},
\end{align}
```

where $`D`$ is the dimension of hidden vectors $`\bm{\mathrm{e}}_t\!\in\!E`$ and $`\bm{\mathrm{h}}_n\!\in\!H`$ in each encoded sequence, and $`\sigma(\cdot)`$ is a softmax layer. In Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_at" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_at">[eq:p_bc_at]</a>, $`\text{ConcatNet}_t(\cdot)`$ indicates the $`t`$-th output of the concatenation network, which adopts the self-attention mechanism  for learning inner/inter dependencies within/between the concatenated $`E`$ and $`H`$.

**Training** The ojective function of BERT-CTC is defined by the negative log-likelihood of Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_O">[eq:p_bc_W_O]</a> expanded with Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3]</a>:

```math
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{bec}} &= -\log \sum_{\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}} \sum_{A^{\mathsf{b}}} \prod_{t} p(a^{\mathsf{b}}_t|\text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),O) p(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}|O) \nonumber \\
    &\le -\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}} \sim \mathcal{M}(W^{\mathsf{b}})} \bigg[ \log \sum_{A^{\mathsf{b}}} \prod_{t} p(a^{\mathsf{b}}_t|\text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),O) \bigg], \label{eq:L_bec}
\end{align}
```

where the intractable marginalization over $`\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ is rewritten under expectation with respect to a sampling distribution $`\mathcal{M}(W^{\mathsf{b}})`$. Following , the sampling process is performed by first sampling the number of tokens from a uniform distribution as $`M\!\sim\!\mathcal{U}(1,N)`$ and then randomly masking $`M`$ tokens in a ground-truth sequence $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$. The summation over $`A^{\mathsf{b}}`$ is efficiently computed as in CTC .

**Inference** Algorithm <a href="#algo:bertctc_inference" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="algo:bertctc_inference">[algo:bertctc_inference]</a> represents a pseudocode for BERT-CTC inference, which is inspired by the mask-predict algorithm  combined with CTC inference . The algorithm gradually generates an output sequence $`\hat{W}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ by iterating the following procedure $`K`$ times. At each iteration $`k\!\in\!\{1,\cdots,K\}`$, a masked sequence $`\hat{W}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ is fed into BERT to obtain contextual embeddings $`H`$ (line 4). Given $`H`$ and an encoder output $`E`$, $`\hat{W}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ is updated with a newly predicted sequence, which is obtained via the best path decoding  using the framewise token probability from Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_at" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_at">[eq:p_bc_at]</a> (line 5). Then, $`M`$ tokens having the lowest probability scores are replaced with the mask token $`\varnothing`$ (lines 6 and 7), where $`M`$ is calculated from a linear decay function $`\lfloor |\hat{W}^{\mathsf{b}}| \cdot \frac{K - k}{K} \rfloor`$ similar to .

**Overview** Figure <a href="#fig:bectra" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:bectra">1</a> illustrates the proposed model, namely **BE**RT-**C**TC-**Tra**nsducer (BECTRA), that formulates E2E-ASR based on the transducer  and BERT-CTC. BECTRA constructs an encoder as the BERT-CTC model, where the output of the concatenation network in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_at" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_at">[eq:p_bc_at]</a> is used to calculate the transducer loss. The joint and prediction networks are trained with an ASR-specific vocabulary, here a vocabulary constructed from the transcription of a target corpus. BECTRA enables more suitable training of E2E-ASR compared to BERT-CTC, unconstrained from the BERT vocabulary.

Let $`W^{\mathsf{a}}\!=\!( w^{\mathsf{a}}_m\!\in\!\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}} | m\!=\!1,\cdots,M)`$ be an $`M`$-length output sequence corresponding to an input sequence $`O`$ and output sequence in the BERT vocabulary $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$, where $`\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}`$ is a vocabulary constructed from ASR training text. The superscript ($`\mathsf{a}`$) indicates the use of the ASR vocabulary. Similar to Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_O">[eq:p_bc_W_O]</a>, BECTRA formulates E2E-ASR by marginalizing the conditional probability $`p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|O)`$ over all possible masked sequences as

```math
\begin{equation}
    p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|O) = \sum_{\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}} p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}},O) p(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}|O).
    \vspace{-0.05cm}
    \label{eq:p_bt_W_O}
\end{equation}
```

Note that, in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_W_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_W_O">[eq:p_bt_W_O]</a>, $`\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ is obtained by masking a sequence in the BERT unit $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$ ($`\neq W^{\mathsf{a}}`$). As in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O]</a>, $`p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}},O)`$ is factorized by considering possible alignment paths and making conditional independence assumptions as

```math
\begin{equation}
    p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}},O) \approx \sum_{A^{\mathsf{a}}\in\mathcal{B'}^{\text{--}1}(W^{\mathsf{a}})}
    p(A^{\mathsf{a}}|W^{\mathsf{a}},O) p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),
    \label{eq:p_bt_W_tW_O}
\end{equation}
```

where $`A^{\mathsf{a}}\!=\!(a^{\mathsf{a}}_u\!\in\!\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}\cup\{\epsilon\}|u\!=\!1,\cdots,T\!+\!M)`$ is an alignment path between $`O`$ and $`W^{\mathsf{a}}`$, defined by the transducer , and $`\mathcal{B}':A^{\mathsf{a}}\!\mapsto\!W^{\mathsf{a}}`$ is the collapsing function. The alignment probability $`p(A^{\mathsf{a}}|W^{\mathsf{a}},O)`$ is further factorized by the probabilistic chain rule _without a conditional independence assumption_ (cf. Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2]</a>) as

```math
\begin{align}
    &\hspace{-0.16cm}\resizebox{7.5cm}{!}{$\displaystyle
    \text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:p_bt_W_tW_O}} = \sum_{A^{\mathsf{a}}} \prod_{u=1}^{T+M} p(a^{\mathsf{a}}_u|a^{\mathsf{a}}_1,\cdots,a^{\mathsf{a}}_{u-1},W^{\mathsf{a}},O) p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),$} \\
    &\hspace{-0.16cm}\resizebox{7.9cm}{!}{$\displaystyle
    \text{\phantom{Eq.~\eqref{eq:p_bt_W_tW_O}}} \approx \sum_{A^{\mathsf{a}}} \prod_{u=1}^{T+M} p(a^{\mathsf{a}}_u|\underbrace{w^{\mathsf{a}}_1,\cdots,w^{\mathsf{a}}_{m_{u}-1}}_{=\mathcal{B}'(a^{\mathsf{a}}_1,\cdots,a^{\mathsf{a}}_{u-1})},W^{\mathsf{a}},O) p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}})$,}
    \label{eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b}
\end{align}
```

where $`m_u`$ is the number of tokens predicted up to an index of $`u`$. In Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b">[eq:p*bt_W_tW_O_2b]</a>, we approximate $`(a^{\mathsf{a}}\_1,\cdots,a^{\mathsf{a}}*{u-1})\!\approx\!(w^{\mathsf{a}}_1,\cdots,w^{\mathsf{a}}_{m_u-1})`$ as performed in the transducer, which is reasonable since $`W^{\mathsf{a}}`$ can be decided uniquely by the collapsing function. Similar to BERT-CTC in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_3]</a>, BECTRA models Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b">[eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b]</a> as

```math
\begin{equation}
    \text{Eq.~\eqref{eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b}} \triangleq \sum_{A^{\mathsf{a}}} \prod_{u} p(a^{\mathsf{a}}_u|w^{\mathsf{a}}_{<m_{u}},\text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),O), \label{eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_3}
\end{equation}
```

where we assume $`p(W^{\mathsf{a}}|\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}})`$ can be modeled by BERT, as $`W^{\mathsf{b}}`$ and $`W^{\mathsf{a}}`$ are convertible to each other via their tokenizers.

The architecture of BECTRA is shown in Fig. <a href="#fig:bectra" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:bectra">1</a>, and the token emission probability in Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_3" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_3">[eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_3]</a> is computed as

```math
\begin{align}
    &p(a^{\mathsf{a}}_u|w^{\mathsf{a}}_{<m_{u}},\text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),O) \nonumber \\ &\hspace{0.5cm}=\sigma(\text{JointNet}(\text{ConcatNet}_t(E,H),\bm{\mathrm{q}}_{m_u})) \in [0,1]^{|\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}|+1}, \hspace{-0.1cm} \label{eq:p_bt_at} \\
    &\hspace{-0.056cm}\bm{\mathrm{q}}_{m_u}\!= \text{PredictionNet} (w^{\mathsf{a}}_1,\cdots,w^{\mathsf{a}}_{m_u-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^D, \label{eq:q_m}
\end{align}
```

where $`\bm{\mathrm{q}}_{m_u}`$ is a $`D`$-dimensional hidden vector obtained by encoding the previous non-blank tokens using the prediction network. In Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_at" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_at">[eq:p_bt_at]</a>, the joint network maps the combined outputs of the concatenation and prediction networks into a joint space. The adoption of the prediction network allows the model to explicitly capture causal dependency in output tokens, which is one of the key differences BECTRA has an advantage over BERT-CTC.

**Training** By substituting Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_3" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_3">[eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_3]</a> into Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_W_O" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_W_O">[eq:p_bt_W_O]</a>, the transducer loss of BECTRA is derived in the same manner as Eq. <a href="#eq:L_bec" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:L_bec">[eq:L_bec]</a> as

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{tra}} \triangleq -\mathbb{E}_{\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}} \sim \mathcal{M}(W^{\mathsf{b}})} \bigg[ \log \sum_{A^{\mathsf{a}}} \prod_{u} p(a^{\mathsf{a}}_u|w^{\mathsf{a}}_{<m_{u}},\text{BERT}(\tilde{W}^{\mathsf{b}}),O) \bigg], \label{eq:L_tra}
\end{equation}
```

where the summation over $`A^{\mathsf{a}}`$ is efficiently computed via dynamic programming . The objective function of BECTRA is defined by combining $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{bec}}`$ from Eq. <a href="#eq:L_bec" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:L_bec">[eq:L*bec]</a> and $`\mathcal{L}*{\mathsf{tra}}`$ from Eq. <a href="#eq:L_tra" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:L_tra">[eq:L_tra]</a> as

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{bectra}} = (1 - \lambda) \mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{bec}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{tra}},
    \label{eq:L_bt}
\end{equation}
```

where $`\lambda\!\in\!(0,1)`$ is a tunable parameter.

# Experiments

## Experimental Setting

We used the ESPnet toolkit  for conducting the experiments, and all the codes and recipes are made publicly available at <https://github.com/YosukeHiguchi/espnet/tree/bectra>.

**Data** We evaluated models using various corpora with different amounts of data, speaking styles, and languages, including LibriSpeech (LS) , TED-LIUM2 (TED2) , and AISHELL-1 (AS1) . We also trained models on the _train-clean-100_ subset of LS (LS-100) for performing additional investigations and analyses. To obtain the vocabulary $`\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}`$ from ASR transcriptions, we used SentencePiece  to construct subword vocabularies for LS-100, LS and TED2, where each vocabulary size $`|\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}|`$ was set to $`300`$, $`5\text{k}`$, and $`500`$, respectively. For AS1, we used character-level tokenization with $`4231`$ Chinese characters.

**Evaluated models** For a baseline model, we trained Conformer-Transducer (Cfm-T) implemented in ESPnet , which is similar to the model in Fig. <a href="#fig:bectra" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:bectra">1</a> but without the concatenation, CTC, and BERT components. BERT-CTC  is also a baseline model trained based on $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{bec}}`$ from Eq. <a href="#eq:L_bec" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:L_bec">[eq:L*bec]</a>. BECTRA is the proposed model trained based on $`\mathcal{L}*{\mathsf{bectra}}`$ from Eq. <a href="#eq:L_bt" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:L_bt">[eq:L_bt]</a>.

**Network architecture** For the audio encoder, we constructed the Conformer architecture , which consisted of two convolutional neural network layers followed by a stack of $`12`$ encoder blocks. The number of heads $`d_{\mathsf{h}}`$, dimension of a self-attention layer $`d_{\mathsf{model}}`$, dimension of a feed-forward network $`d_{\mathsf{ff}}`$, and kernel size $`K`$ were set to $`4`$, $`256`$, $`1024`$, and $`31`$, respectively. For the transducer-based models, the prediction network was a single long short-term memory layer with $`256`$ hidden units. For the models using BERT, the concatenation network was the Transformer encoder  with $`6`$ blocks, and $`d_{\mathsf{h}}`$, $`d_{\mathsf{model}}`$, and $`d_{\mathsf{ff}}`$ were set to $`4`$, $`256`$, and $`2048`$, respectively. We used a BERT$`_{\text{BASE}}`$ of each language provided in HuggingFace : English  ($`|\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}}|\!=\!30522`$) and Mandarin  ($`|\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}}|\!=\!21128`$). The output dimension $`D`$ of the audio encoder $`\bm{\mathrm{e}}_t`$, BERT $`\bm{\mathrm{h}}_n`$, and prediction network $`\bm{\mathrm{q}}_{m_u}`$ were all adjusted to match $`d_{\mathsf{model}}`$ ($`=256`$).

**Training and decoding** We mostly followed configurations provided by the ESPnet2 recipe for each dataset. The models were trained to 100 epochs for LS-100 and AS1, and 70 epochs for TED2 and LS. The Adam optimizer  with Noam learning rate scheduling  was used for weight updates, where warmup steps and a peak learning rate were set to $`15`$k and $`2\text{e-}3`$, respectively. We augmented speech data using speed perturbation  with a factor of $`3`$ and SpecAugment . Similar to , we adopted the intermediate CTC regularization  for the audio encoder, where an auxiliary CTC loss was applied to the $`6`$-th layer and calculated based on the ASR vocabulary $`\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}`$. For BECTRA training, we set $`\lambda`$ to $`0.5`$ in Eq. <a href="#eq:L_bt" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:L_bt">[eq:L_bt]</a>. After training, a final model was obtained for evaluation by averaging model parameters over ten checkpoints with the best validation performance. For the number of iterations in BERT-CTC decoding (in Alg. <a href="#algo:bertctc_inference" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="algo:bertctc_inference">[algo:bertctc_inference]</a>), we set $`K`$ to $`20`$ for BERT-CTC and $`10`$ for BECTRA. We performed the beam search decoding with a beam size of $`10`$ for Cfm-T and $`5`$ for BECTRA.

## Difficulty of Training ASR with BERT Vocabulary

<span id="ssec:exp_bert_vocab" label="ssec:exp_bert_vocab"></span> In Table <a href="#tb:main_results" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tb:main_results">[tb:main_results]</a>, we compare LS-100 results on Cfm-T trained with the the BERT or ASR vocabulary ($`\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{b}}`$ vs. $`\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}`$). At a glance, Cfm-T resulted in significantly worse WERs by using the BERT vocabulary, suggesting unsuitable ASR training with the word-level and domain-mismatched BERT units. We also mention that the large vocabulary size leads to increasingly high memory consumption during the transducer training , which makes it difficult to train a model, especially on large datasets. In contrast, BERT-CTC in Table <a href="#tb:main_results" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tb:main_results">[tb:main_results]</a> achieved decent results by effectively dealing with the vocabulary mismatch, explicitly using BERT information .

## Main Results

<span id="ssec:exp_main" label="ssec:exp_main"></span> Table <a href="#tb:main_results" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tb:main_results">[tb:main_results]</a> lists the main results of all the tasks evaluated in the word error rate (WER) or character error rate (CER). Comparing Cfm-T (with $`\mathcal{V}^{\mathsf{a}}`$) and BERT-CTC, BERT-CTC performed worse than Cfm-T in several tasks due to the vocabulary discrepancy. For example, in TED2, a severe domain mismatch exists between the BERT training text and ASR transcription (i.e., Wikipedia vs. lecture) that caused unsuitable ASR training for BERT-CTC. Overall, BECTRA achieved the best result across all of the tasks. The gain from Cfm-T demonstrates the effectiveness of exploiting BERT knowledge via the BERT-CTC-based encoder. Moreover, BECTRA improved BERT-CTC by generating text in a suitable output unit and mitigating the conditional independence assumption (Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2">[eq:p_bc_W_tW_O_2]</a> vs. Eq. <a href="#eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b">[eq:p_bt_W_tW_O_2b]</a>). Another notable observation was that, with more data in LS-960, the performance gap between Cfm-T and BECTRA was reduced, and the usage of BERT became less influential. We assume that LS-960 contained enough text data that the models were able to learn rich linguistic information in the LibriSpeech-specific domain.

Table <a href="#tb:decoding_example" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tb:decoding_example">[tb:decoding_example]</a> shows an example decoding process of BECTRA. BERT-CTC succeeded at resolving most of the substitution errors via iterative refinement using BERT. The transducer decoding was particularly effective at adjusting deletion and insertion errors in BERT-CTC outputs, e.g., “poetryry”$`\rightarrow`$“poetry” in Table <a href="#tb:decoding_example" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tb:decoding_example">[tb:decoding_example]</a>.

## Trade-off between WER and Inference Speed

Figure <a href="#fig:iterations" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:iterations">[fig:iterations]</a> depicts the trade-off between WER and real-time factor (RTF) on the LS dev-{clean, other} sets. RTF was measured using a single V100 GPU with a batchsize of $`1`$. Looking at the results from greedy decoding ($`K\!\le\!1`$ and $`B\!=\!1`$), Cfm-T resulted in the best performance with the lowest WER and RTF. The performance gain was more substantial for increasing the number of iterations in BERT-CTC than for increasing a beam size in Cfm-T. BECTRA greatly benefited from beam search decoding at $`K\!=\!1`$, but the improvement became small with more iterations $`K\!>\!1`$. This indicates that BERT-CTC decoding refined the output sequence enough that the search space was reduced during beam search decoding, and only a small beam size ($`K\!=\!3`$ or $`5`$) is adequate without degrading the inference speed. All in all, BECTRA with $`K\!=\!10`$ and $`B\!\in\![1,5]`$ resulted in the preferable performance balancing the trade-off reasonably well, taking advantage of both fast non-autoregressive decoding of BERT-CTC and accurate autoregressive decoding of Cfm-T.

# Conclusion

We proposed BECTRA, a novel E2E-ASR model formulated by the transducer and BERT-CTC. BECTRA adopts BERT-CTC for the audio encoder, aiming to exploit BERT for capturing contextual information. In addition, prediction and joint networks are trained with an ASR-specific vocabulary, which enables the model to perform ASR in a suitable unit. One of the limitations of BECTRA is its non-streaming property due to the BERT-CTC’s non-autoregressive formulation. The possible solutions include the adoption of the two-pass modeling  or blockwise-attention mechanism .
