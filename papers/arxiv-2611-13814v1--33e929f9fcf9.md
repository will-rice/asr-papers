# Swanada: Split-Encoder Capability-Routed Conformer CTC and Sub-30 MB On-Device Speech Recognition Across Twelve Scripts

Hemanth HM Affiliation: [https://h3manth.com](https://h3manth.com)   
$`\cdot`$   
[https://github.com/hemanth/swanada](https://github.com/hemanth/swanada)

###### Abstract

Client-side automatic speech recognition (ASR) across Indic and
Classical scripts eliminates cloud transcription costs, network tail
latency, and server-side audio retention, yet standard neural exports
face four systems bottlenecks in web browsers and mobile runtimes:
(1) monolithic per-language Conformer checkpoints require
$`\sim`$177 MiB per script ($`>2.1`$ GiB across 12 scripts), exhausting
browser CacheStorage quotas; (2) exported relative positional encoding
tables embed 10.2–20.5 MB of static FP32 constants while $`1\times 1`$
pointwise Conv1d operators trigger slow or unsupported ConvInteger
kernels in WebAssembly SIMD; (3) sliding-window CTC decoding flickers on
boundary tokens as right context arrives; and (4) mobile WebAudio
pipelines suffer hardware sample-rate mismatches (44.1/48 kHz vs.
16 kHz) and silent AudioWorklet stalls under iOS WKWebView and Android
backgrounding.

We present Swanada, an open-source 16 kHz streaming ASR system spanning
Classical Sanskrit, Indian English, and ten Indian languages (sa, en,
hi, kn, mr, bn, ta, te, ml, gu, pa, or) across browser WebAssembly,
Node.js, and Python. Swanada pairs capability-routed shared INT8
Conformer encoders—a 17-layer 177.38 MiB Studio tier ($`d=512`$, 115.2M
parameters) for desktop/server runtimes and a distilled 14-layer
25.35 MiB Mobile tier ($`d=256`$, 23.56M parameters) for phones—with
twelve hot-swappable 515.0 KiB native-script linear CTC heads
($`257\times 512`$), shrinking 12-language fleet storage by
$`11.6\times`$ (183.4 MiB Studio) and $`67.8\times`$ (31.39 MiB Mobile;
25.85 MiB first load). A zero-dependency protobuf tail patcher exposes
the pre-head representation in $`<2`$ ms without shipping separate
backbone files, while dynamic ONNX trigonometric positional synthesis,
MatMul-backed pointwise convolutions, and post-quantization QInt8 head
calibration preserve sub-30 MB mobile accuracy. On $`N=480`$ held-out
studio utterances and $`N=1{,}280`$ unseen public test clips across 16
open benchmarks (FLEURS, LibriSpeech, GLOBE, AMI, and wild Sanskrit),
Swanada achieves 5.43% held-out CER and 8.87% 12-language open
FLEURS+Wild CER on Studio (18.56% and 19.23% on 25.35 MiB Mobile) at
$`0.0097\times`$–$`0.0264\times`$ 4-thread CPU RTF, delivering 30.6 ms
median streaming TTFT with 0.00% committed-prefix churn across 72
streaming sessions.

## 1 Introduction

Running speech-to-text directly inside a browser tab or local edge
process removes per-minute API billing, round-trip network jitter, and
transmission of private microphone audio to remote servers. Voice input
for South Asian digital public infrastructure, Sanskrit recitation
study, bilingual developer tools, and offline accessibility interfaces
requires native script fidelity across Brahmic and Latin orthographies,
sub-100 ms streaming partial updates, and reliable execution within
smartphone browser memory budgets, complementing client-side speech
synthesis \[9\].

Convolution-augmented Transformers (Conformers) \[7\] trained with
Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) \[6\] are attractive for
on-device ASR because frame-synchronous emission avoids the $`O(U^{2})`$
autoregressive decoder loop and hallucination modes of
sequence-to-sequence models such as Whisper \[13\]. However, deploying
Conformer-CTC across twelve scripts inside real desktop and mobile
browsers exposes four concrete systems bottlenecks:

1.  1.  Monolithic Fleet Storage vs. Joint-Vocabulary Dilution: Shipping one
        quantized Conformer-Large per language requires
        $`12\times 177.38=2{,}128.5`$ MiB, exceeding mobile browser cache
        budgets. Conversely, pooling twelve scripts into a single flat
        output softmax ($`>3{,}000`$ classes) inflates final projection
        compute by $`12\times`$ at every frame and permits cross-script
        token leakage during noisy frames.
2.  2.  ONNX Constant Bloat and ConvInteger Quantization Gaps: Standard
        Conformer exports materialize a static $`2L_{\max}\times d`$ FP32
        relative positional encoding buffer (20.48 MB at $`d=512`$, 10.24 MB
        at $`d=256`$ for $`L_{\max}=5{,}000`$), consuming over a third of a
        30 MB mobile budget on uncompressible constants. Simultaneously,
        exporting $`1\times 1`$ pointwise convolutions inside Conformer
        macaron blocks as Conv nodes either leaves weights in FP32 or emits
        ConvInteger operators lacking fast WebAssembly SIMD kernels in
        onnxruntime-web.
3.  3.  Quantization Feature Drift Under Narrow Student Bottlenecks:
        Compressing a 115.2M-parameter teacher into a $`<30`$ MB student and
        applying dynamic INT8 weight/activation quantization perturbs
        top-layer hidden norms and directions, degrading pre-trained linear
        CTC decision boundaries unless explicitly recalibrated on quantized
        ONNX activations.
4.  4.  Streaming Prefix Flicker and Mobile WebAudio Fragility: Evaluating
        bounded audio windows causes trailing subwords to oscillate as right
        acoustic context arrives, while mobile browsers lock AudioContext to
        hardware rates (44.1/48 kHz) and intermittently stall AudioWorklet
        threads under iOS WKWebView rules.

To address these constraints across browser ES modules, Node.js, and
Python without custom WebAssembly builds, we present Swanada (Figures 1
and 2). Our contributions are threefold:

- $`\bullet`$
  Runtime Protobuf Tail Surgery and Modular 12-Script CTC: We decouple
  shared pan-Indic acoustic encoding from twelve script-isolated
  257-token linear heads (515.0 KiB each), using a zero-dependency
  binary ONNX GraphProto patcher that exposes the shared
  $`512`$-dimensional encoder representation in $`<2`$ ms and switches
  languages in $`<20`$ ms with $`<10^{-5}`$ logit deviation against
  monolithic graphs.
- $`\bullet`$
  Sub-30 MB Mobile Conformer Distillation and QInt8 Head Calibration: We
  design a 25.35 MiB Mobile Conformer-Small combining zero-table dynamic
  relative positional encoding, MatMul-backed pointwise convolutions for
  100% SIMD QInt8 coverage, stationary-hyperplane 4-loss distillation
  from the 177.38 MiB teacher, and post-export QInt8 head calibration.
- $`\bullet`$
  Two-Zone LCP Streaming and Hardened Mobile Capture: We pair causal
  Wiener + notch/high-pass enhancement with a monotonic
  Longest-Common-Prefix (LCP) streaming stabilizer over bounded 3.84 s
  windows and watchdog-guarded 16 kHz WebAudio capture, validating
  accuracy and latency across $`N=480`$ held-out clips, $`N=1{,}280`$
  open public test clips, $`N=150`$ multi-accent/slang clips, and
  $`N=72`$ streaming sessions.

## 2 Split-Encoder Architecture and Native Orthography

Swanada processes 16,000 Hz mono PCM audio using 80-channel log-mel
filterbanks ($`25`$ ms Povey/Hann window, $`10`$ ms hop, 80–7,600 Hz mel
span) with per-utterance variance normalization. A two-stage strided 2D
convolutional front-end ($`4\times`$ temporal subsampling to $`40`$ ms
frame rate $`T^{\prime}=\lfloor(T_{\text{mel}}-1)/4\rfloor+1`$) feeds a
stack of macaron-style Conformer blocks \[7\], each comprising a
half-step feed-forward module, multi-head self-attention with relative
positional encoding \[15\], depthwise separable convolution ($`k=31`$,
Swish/GLU gating), and a second half-step feed-forward module,
terminating in final layer normalization layer_norm_84
($`\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times T^{\prime}\times 512}`$).

### 2.1 Script-Isolated Vocabularies and Classical Transliteration

Joint multilingual tokenizers either Romanize South Asian
scripts—erasing phonemic contrasts between dental and retroflex stops,
short/long Dravidian mid-vowels ($`e/\bar{e},o/\bar{o}`$), and Sanskrit
vocalic sonorants—or allocate thousands of sparse Unicode classes that
slow every frame’s output projection. Instead, Swanada assigns each
language
$`\ell\in\{\texttt{sa},\texttt{en},\texttt{hi},\texttt{kn},\texttt{mr},\texttt{bn},\texttt{ta},\texttt{te},\texttt{ml},\texttt{gu},\texttt{pa},\texttt{or}\}`$
an independent native-script SentencePiece \[10\] vocabulary of
$`V=256`$ subword pieces plus 1 CTC blank ($`K=257`$ classes,
2.85–3.41 KB JSON metadata per language).

Conditioning framewise decoding strictly on the active language head
$`\mathcal{H}_{\ell}=(\mathbf{W}_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell})`$ with
$`\mathbf{W}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{257\times 512}`$ and
$`\mathbf{b}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{257}`$ guarantees zero out-of-script
Unicode emissions (e.g., preventing Devanagari matras during Bengali or
Kannada speech) while keeping each projection head at exactly
$`257\times 512\times 4+257\times 4=527{,}364`$ bytes (515.0 KiB raw
IEEE-754 float32; 527,780 bytes as standalone head\_\<lang\>.onnx). For
Classical Sanskrit (sa), a deterministic finite-state post-processor
optionally transliterates decoded Devanagari into lossless ISO 15919 /
Roman IAST, preserving aspirated stops, retroflex sibilants (_ṣ_),
vocalic $`\text{\@text@daccent{r}}/\bar{\text{\@text@daccent{r}}}`$,
visarga (_ḥ_), anusvāra (_ṃ_), avagraha (’), and Vedic pitch accents.

### 2.2 Zero-Dependency Runtime Protobuf Tail Surgery

Exporting separate encoder-only and monolithic checkpoints would
duplicate storage across distribution channels, whereas running a
monolithic single-language ONNX model discards the hidden states needed
by the other eleven languages. During session initialization, Swanada’s
runtime (exposeEncoderHiddenOutput in JavaScript and Python) patches the
serialized ModelProto byte buffer in memory in $`<2`$ ms without
external protobuf libraries (Figure 1).

Given an Uint8Array buffer, a lightweight wire-format reader scans
top-level ModelProto varint tags to locate the GraphProto payload
(field 7, wire tag 0x3a). If layer_norm_84 is not yet listed among
GraphProto.output entries (field 12, wire tag 0x62), the patcher
synthesizes a 54-byte ValueInfoProto descriptor declaring layer_norm_84
as an unconstrained FLOAT tensor ($`\texttt{elem\_type}=1`$), appends
0x62 plus the varint-prefixed descriptor to the tail of GraphProto, and
updates the outer ModelProto length prefix in a single contiguous
allocation.

Once ONNX Runtime produces
$`\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{T^{\prime}\times 512}`$, the active language
head $`\mathcal{H}_{\ell}`$ executes directly in host SIMD/typed-array
memory (or via cached head\_\<lang\>.onnx in Python/Node.js):

```math
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W}_{\ell}^{\top}+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{b}_{\ell}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{T^{\prime}\times 257} \tag{1}
```

Switching languages replaces only the 515.0 KiB
$`(\mathbf{W}_{\ell},\mathbf{b}_{\ell})`$ buffer in $`<20`$ ms without
reloading or recompiling the Conformer session, achieving maximum
absolute logit deviation $`<10^{-5}`$ relative to monolithic PyTorch and
ONNX graphs while compressing the 12-language Studio fleet from
2,128.5 MiB to 183.4 MiB ($`11.6\times`$) and the Mobile fleet to
31.39 MiB ($`67.8\times`$).

## 3 Sub-30 MB Mobile Distillation and QInt8 Calibration

While the 17-layer Studio Conformer-Large
($`d=512,d_{\text{ff}}=2048,n_{h}=8`$, 177.38 MiB QInt8) runs at
$`0.0264\times`$ RTF on 4-thread desktop CPUs, downloading 186 MB over
cellular networks or holding $`>350`$ MB peak WebAssembly heap inside
mobile Safari/Chrome tabs triggers background tab eviction. We therefore
distill a dedicated $`<30`$ MB Mobile Conformer-Small
(swanada_mobile_ctc_int8.onnx, 26,577,061 bytes = 25.35 MiB) engineered
around three ONNX/WASM operator constraints.

### 3.1 Zero-Table Positional Synthesis and MatMul Convolutions

#### Dynamic Relative Positional Encoding.

Standard Conformer implementations pre-allocate a static sinusoidal
lookup tensor $`\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{(2L_{\max}-1)\times d}`$ during
module construction so slice operations can index relative offsets
$`[-(T^{\prime}-1),\dots,T^{\prime}-1]`$. When exported to ONNX,
$`\mathbf{P}`$ is frozen as an unquantized FP32 initializer consuming
$`9{,}999\times 256\times 4=10.24`$ MB even at $`d=256`$. We replace
static table slicing with an ONNX-traceable DynamicRelPositionalEncoding
subgraph that stores only the 128-element inverse-frequency vector
$`\omega_{i}=10000^{-2i/d}`$ (512 bytes) and synthesizes exact relative
sinusoidal embeddings dynamically for the current frame length
$`T^{\prime}`$ via Shape $`\to`$ Range$`(T^{\prime}-1,-T^{\prime},-1)`$
$`\to`$ outer Mul $`\to`$ interleaved Sin/Cos, matching static
Relative-PE bit-for-bit while saving 10.24 MB of constant storage.

#### MatMul-Backed Pointwise Convolutions.

In standard Conformer convolution modules, channel expansion
($`d\to 2d`$ for GLU) and projection ($`d\to d`$) are written as
nn.Conv1d with kernel size $`k=1`$. Dynamic ONNX quantization on Conv
operators emits ConvInteger, which suffers severe scalar fallback
penalties or operator rejection in browser WASM runtimes, forcing
practitioners to leave convolution blocks in FP32. Because a
$`1\times 1`$ convolution over transposed sequence layout
$`[B,T^{\prime},d]`$ is algebraically identical to a dense matrix
multiplication, we reformulate both pointwise stages as nn.Linear
(MatMul) around a narrow causal-compatible depthwise Conv1d($`k=31`$,
groups $`=176`$) retained in FP32 ($`<0.28`$ MB total across 14 layers).
Applying quantize_dynamic over op_types_to_quantize=\[’MatMul’\] with
QInt8 weights then quantizes 100% of attention, feed-forward,
subsampling projection, and pointwise convolution parameters to
1 byte/parameter on fast WebAssembly SIMD MatMulInteger kernels.

### 3.2 SVD Warm-Start and Stationary-Hyperplane Distillation

The Mobile student uses $`L_{S}=14`$ Conformer layers at width
$`d_{S}=256`$, $`d_{\text{ff}}=1024`$, $`n_{h}=8`$ heads, and
convolution channel width $`176`$, followed by a learned adapter
$`\operatorname{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}}\mathbf{h}_{S}+\mathbf{b}_{\text{proj}})\in\mathbb{R}^{512}`$
that maps the 256-dimensional student state back into the Studio
teacher’s 512-dimensional layer_norm_84 coordinate space (23.56M total
parameters).

To accelerate convergence across twelve scripts without random-init
collapse, we project teacher layers
$`\{0,1,3,4,6,7,9,10,11,12,13,14,15,16\}`$ onto the top-256 singular
subspace of the teacher’s hidden activations via truncated SVD. During
distillation (20 epochs, bf16 mixed precision, AdamW
$`\text{lr}=1.1\times 10^{-3}`$, batch size 64), we freeze all twelve
target projection heads equal to the Studio teacher heads
($`\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{(S)}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{(T)}`$) so target
decision hyperplanes remain stationary, minimizing a four-term alignment
objective over frame-aligned student states $`\mathbf{H}_{S}`$ and
teacher states $`\mathbf{H}_{T}`$:

```math
\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}&=\lambda_{\cos}\!\left(1-\frac{\mathbf{H}_{S}\cdot\mathbf{H}_{T}}{\|\mathbf{H}_{S}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{T}\|_{2}}\right)+\lambda_{\text{mse}}\|\mathbf{H}_{S}-\mathbf{H}_{T}\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\quad+\lambda_{\text{kl}}\tau^{2}D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(p_{T}^{(\tau)}\parallel p_{S}^{(\tau)}\right)+\lambda_{\text{ctc}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{CTC}}(\mathbf{Z}_{S},y)\end{split} \tag{2}
```

with $`p^{(\tau)}=\sigma(\mathbf{Z}/\tau)`$, $`\lambda_{\cos}=2.5`$,
$`\lambda_{\text{mse}}=2.5`$, $`\lambda_{\text{kl}}=0.38`$,
$`\lambda_{\text{ctc}}=0.16`$, and temperature $`\tau=1.5`$, combining
feature-space hint alignment \[14\] with temperature-scaled logit
distillation \[8\] and ground-truth CTC supervision \[6\].

### 3.3 Post-Quantization QInt8 Head Calibration and Quarantine

Exporting the 256-dim student to QInt8 ONNX inserts per-tensor
DynamicQuantizeLinear nodes before every MatMul, introducing cumulative
rounding noise
$`\Delta\mathbf{H}=\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{\text{int8}}-\mathbf{H}_{S}`$ at
layer*norm_84. Rather than shipping uncalibrated float heads, we execute
the actual exported swanada_mobile_ctc_int8.onnx checkpoint in ONNX
Runtime across quarantined training utterances per language to extract
true quantized features $`\tilde{\mathbf{H}}*{\text{int8}}`$, then
fine-tune each 515.0 KiB mobile head
$`(\mathbf{W}_{\ell}^{(M)},\mathbf{b}_{\ell}^{(M)})`$ under:

```math
\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cal}}(\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{(M)})&=0.28\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CTC}}(\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_{\ell},y)+0.72\,\tau^{2}D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(p_{T}^{(\tau)}\parallel\tilde{p}_{M}^{(\tau)}\right)\\
&\quad+\gamma\|\mathbf{W}_{\ell}^{(M)}-\mathbf{W}_{\ell}^{(T)}\|_{F}^{2}\end{split} \tag{3}
```

with $`\gamma=5\times 10^{-5}`$, committing calibrated weights to
heads_mobile/\<lang\>.bin only when held-out validation CER strictly
improves over uncalibrated heads.

#### Strict Test-Set Quarantine.

Across both Studio joint adaptation (Shrutilipi/Vistaar \[2\],
FLEURS \[4\] train/dev, Sushrota \[1\] train, IndicTTS, GLOBE \[16\]
train, and AMI \[3\] train) and Mobile distillation/calibration, an
explicit quarantine filter blocks every audio SHA-1 and normalized
reference string belonging to the 480-clip held-out suite and all 16
official public test splits, guaranteeing zero data leakage.

## 4 Two-Zone LCP Streaming and Mobile Capture

Real-time dictation requires sub-100 ms incremental feedback without
word revisions jumping backward across the screen or unbounded memory
growth during long sessions (Figure 2).

### 4.1 Causal Spectral Front-End Enhancement

Laptop and smartphone microphones introduce DC offset, HVAC rumble,
50/60 Hz mains hum, and stationary room noise that trigger spurious CTC
insertions during pauses. Before mel extraction, Swanada applies a
causal $`O(N)`$ time/frequency front-end: (1) a second-order Butterworth
high-pass biquad at $`f_{c}=80`$ Hz paired with cascaded narrowband
notch biquads at $`50`$ Hz and $`60`$ Hz ($`Q=30`$); (2) an
energy-adaptive noise-floor tracker calibrated over initial low-energy
frames; and (3) a decision-directed Ephraim–Malah \[5\] minimum
mean-square error (MMSE) Wiener spectral gain floor ($`\beta=0.08`$,
smoothing $`\alpha_{\text{dd}}=0.92`$) that suppresses stationary
background hiss without clipping unvoiced Indic aspirates or sibilants.

### 4.2 Monotonic Two-Zone LCP Stabilization

Evaluating CTC over an unbounded growing waveform scales per-step
latency linearly with session duration $`O(T)`$, whereas naive sliding
windows overwrite previously displayed words whenever window boundaries
bisect a syllable. Swanada bounds per-step compute to $`O(1)`$ using a
rolling PCM window of maximum length $`W_{\max}=61{,}440`$ samples
($`3.84`$ s at 16 kHz; $`1.60`$ s stride retention upon rollover) paired
with a two-zone Longest-Common-Prefix (LCP) state machine
(StreamingTranscriber):

1.  1.  Committed Zone ($`c_{t}`$): An append-only sequence of tokens
        finalized across prior windows or confirmed across $`M`$ consecutive
        right-context decode steps; once emitted in $`c_{t}`$, characters
        are immutable.
2.  2.  Volatile Tail ($`v_{t}`$): The active right-edge hypothesis decoded
        from the current rolling window after aligning against the tail of
        $`c_{t}`$ via token-level LCP matching.

At streaming step $`t`$ arriving every $`\Delta t=100`$ ms ($`1{,}600`$
samples, or $`280`$ ms coalesced mobile steps), let $`h_{t}`$ denote the
greedy CTC decode after collapsing consecutive duplicate token IDs and
removing blank index $`0`$. Tokens in $`h_{t}`$ that match the longest
suffix-prefix overlap with $`c_{t-1}`$ anchor the window splice;
interior tokens $`h_{t}[i]`$ lying at least $`\delta_{\text{guard}}`$
frames left of the right window boundary that remain invariant across
consecutive hypotheses ($`\operatorname{LCP}(v_{t-1},v_{t})`$) advance
monotonically into $`c_{t}`$, guaranteeing zero committed-prefix churn
($`|c_{t-1}\setminus\operatorname{prefix}(c_{t})|=0`$).

### 4.3 Capability Routing and Watchdog-Guarded WebAudio

Swanada’s runtime router (modelTier: ’auto’) inspects User-Agent,
navigator.deviceMemory, and Cross-Origin Isolation (crossOriginIsolated)
at initialization:

- $`\bullet`$
  Mobile / Constrained Hosts: Android phones, iOS/iPadOS WKWebView
  browsers (Safari, Chrome CriOS), and devices reporting $`\leq 4`$ GB
  RAM route to the 25.35 MiB Mobile tier stored persistently in
  CacheStorage (0 network refetches after initial load). Under
  SharedArrayBuffer isolation on 8-core Android phones, WASM uses
  $`\min(4,C-2)`$ SIMD threads; on single-threaded iOS WKWebView, it
  pins 1 SIMD thread with an inferBusy backpressure lock that coalesces
  incoming PCM frames into a ring buffer rather than queueing stale
  worker jobs.
- $`\bullet`$
  Desktop / Server Hosts: Desktop browsers, Node.js, and Python load the
  177.38 MiB Studio tier on 4 SIMD threads (with instant user toggle
  between tiers in the Web Studio UI).
- $`\bullet`$
  Hardware-Rate Capture + 900 ms Watchdog: Because mobile OS audio
  stacks ignore requested AudioContext sampleRate: 16000 options and run
  natively at 44.1 kHz or 48 kHz, Swanada resamples hardware buffers
  cleanly to 16 kHz PCM inside an AudioWorkletNode. To survive silent
  WebKit/Blink AudioWorklet thread suspension after permission prompts,
  a 900 ms zero-frame watchdog automatically hot-swaps capture to a
  synchronous ScriptProcessorNode fallback without dropping the
  microphone stream.

## 5 Empirical Evaluation

We evaluate Swanada across four reproducible benchmark suites totaling
1,982 utterances ($`>4.3`$ hours of 16 kHz human speech), reporting
Character Error Rate (CER), Word Error Rate (WER), and Real-Time Factor
(RTF) with $`B=1{,}000`$ non-parametric bootstrap 95% confidence
intervals:

1.  1.  Held-Out 12-Language Studio Suite ($`N=480`$): 40 native studio
        utterances per language ($`2{,}185.6`$ s total audio) strictly

## 6 Discussion & Limitations

#### Spontaneous Meeting Overlap and Reverberant Recitation.

Open-benchmark evaluation in Table 2 highlights two challenging acoustic
regimes: spontaneous multi-party meetings (ami_meeting_ihm: 41.50%
Studio / 51.63% Mobile CER) containing overlapping talkers,
disfluencies, and sub-second backchannels, and crowd-recorded Classical
Sanskrit (sushrota_wild_test: 18.92% Studio / 40.15% Mobile CER)
featuring temple/hall reverberation and chanted _sandhi_ compounds.
While the 25.35 MiB Mobile tier preserves strong intelligibility on
clean and moderately noisy speech (fleurs_kn 9.60%, librispeech_clean
16.91%), $`4.9\times`$ fewer parameters limit robustness under severe
reverberation and cross-talk.

#### Greedy Subword CTC vs. Agglutinative WER.

Swanada uses zero-dependency greedy CTC decoding without an external
$`n`$-gram or neural language model to keep per-language overhead at
515.0 KiB. In morphologically rich, agglutinative Dravidian scripts (ml,
ta, kn, te) and Sanskrit _samāsa_ compounds where a single
whitespace-delimited word spans 12–25 characters, a single vowel-length
or virama error flips the entire word, yielding 28%–38% Studio WER
despite low 4.5%–7.5% CER. Optional compact KenLM fusion is a natural
extension where memory budgets permit.

#### Explicit Language Selection and Single-Threaded WebViews.

Conditioning on a selected script head eliminates cross-script character
bleed and $`12\times`$ softmax overhead, but requires either user
language selection or a lightweight acoustic language-ID pass when the
spoken language is unknown. On iOS WKWebView browsers lacking
SharedArrayBuffer support without Service Worker Jetsam risk, WASM runs
single-threaded ($`0.036\times`$ Mobile RTF vs. $`0.0097\times`$
4-thread), making the 25.35 MiB Mobile tier essential for cool,
responsive phone dictation.

## 7 Related Work

#### Multilingual and Indic Speech Recognition.

Gulati et al. \[7\] introduced Conformers combining
self-attention \[15\] and depthwise convolutions. Whisper \[13\] and
Meta MMS \[12\] scaled multilingual ASR across dozens to over a thousand
languages, while AI4Bharat Vistaar/IndicConformer \[2\] and
Sushrota \[1\] established strong server-side baselines for Indian
languages and Classical Sanskrit. Unlike monolithic PyTorch/NeMo
checkpoints ($`180\text{--}600+`$ MB per model) or autoregressive
decoders prone to looping on noisy browser audio, Swanada targets
sub-30 MB split-encoder WebAssembly execution with hot-swappable 515 KiB
native-script heads.

#### Knowledge Distillation and Browser Speech Systems.

Hinton et al. \[8\] and Romero et al. \[14\] established logit and
intermediate-representation distillation. Client-side speech toolkits
such as whisper.cpp, Transformers.js, and sherpa-onnx bring quantized
models to WebAssembly, while Ninada \[9\] introduced runtime binary ONNX
graph splitting and capability-routed mobile/studio specialists for
24 kHz Indic TTS. Swanada extends this split-graph, capability-routed
paradigm to 16 kHz streaming ASR via zero-table relative-PE synthesis,
MatMul-backed QInt8 Conformer blocks, post-quantization head
calibration, and zero-churn LCP stabilization.

## 8 Conclusion

We presented Swanada, an open-source client-side speech recognition
system spanning Classical Sanskrit, Indian English, and ten Indian
languages across browser WebAssembly, Node.js, and Python. By combining
$`<2`$ ms binary ONNX GraphProto tail surgery, twelve hot-swappable
515.0 KiB native-script CTC heads, a distilled 25.35 MiB Mobile
Conformer-Small with dynamic positional synthesis and QInt8-calibrated
heads alongside a 177.38 MiB Studio Conformer-Large, and two-zone LCP
streaming stabilization, Swanada achieves 5.43% held-out CER, 8.87%
12-language open FLEURS+Wild CER (19.23% Mobile) across 1,280 public
test clips, $`0.0097\times`$–$`0.0264\times`$ CPU RTF, and 30.6 ms
median streaming TTFT with 0.00% committed-prefix churn. Source code,
reproducible benchmark harnesses, and open weights are available at
[https://github.com/hemanth/swanada](https://github.com/hemanth/swanada).
