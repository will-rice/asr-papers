**Abstract**

What does it take to create the Babel Fish, a tool that can help individuals translate speech between any two languages? While recent breakthroughs in text-based models have pushed machine translation coverage beyond 200 languages, unified speech-to-speech translation models have yet to achieve similar strides. More specifically, conventional speech-to-speech translation systems rely on cascaded systems composed of multiple subsystems performing translation progressively, putting scalable and high-performing unified speech translation systems out of reach. To address these gaps, we introduce **SeamlessM4T**—**M**assively **M**ultilingual & **M**ultimodal **M**achine **T**ranslation—a single model that supports speech-to-speech translation, speech-to-text translation, text-to-speech translation, text-to-text translation, and automatic speech recognition for up to 100 languages. To build this, we used 1 million hours of open speech audio data to learn self-supervised speech representations with <span class="smallcaps">w2v-BERT 2.0</span>. Subsequently, we created a multimodal corpus of automatically aligned speech translations, dubbed <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessAlign</span>. Filtered and combined with human-labeled and pseudo-labeled data (totaling 406,000 hours), we developed the first multilingual system capable of translating from and into English for both speech and text. On <span class="smallcaps">Fleurs</span>, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span> sets a new standard for translations into multiple target languages, achieving an improvement of 20% <span class="smallcaps">BLEU</span> over the previous state-of-the-art in direct speech-to-text translation. Compared to strong cascaded models, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span> improves the quality of into-English translation by 1.3 <span class="smallcaps">BLEU</span> points in speech-to-text and by 2.6 <span class="smallcaps">ASR-BLEU</span> points in speech-to-speech. On <span class="smallcaps">CVSS</span> and compared to a 2-stage cascaded model for speech-to-speech translation, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span>’s performance is stronger by 58%. Preliminary human evaluations of speech-to-text translation outputs evinced similarly impressive results; for translations from English, XSTS scores for 24 evaluated languages are consistently above 4 (out of 5). For into English directions, we see significant improvement over <span class="smallcaps">Whisper-Large-v2</span>’s baseline for 7 out of 24 languages. To further evaluate our system, we developed <span class="smallcaps">Blaser 2.0</span>, which enables evaluation across speech and text with similar accuracy compared to its predecessor when it comes to quality estimation. Tested for robustness, our system performs better against background noises and speaker variations in speech-to-text tasks (average improvements of 38% and 49%, respectively) compared to the current state-of-the-art model. Critically, we evaluated <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span> on gender bias and added toxicity to assess translation safety. Compared to the state-of-the-art, we report up to 63% reduction in added toxicity in our translation outputs. Finally, all contributions in this work—including models, inference code, finetuning recipes backed by our improved modeling toolkit <span class="smallcaps">Fairseq2</span>, and metadata to recreate the unfiltered 470,000 hours of <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessAlign</span>—are open-sourced and accessible at <https://github.com/facebookresearch/seamless_communication>.

# Introduction

_The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy’s_ Babel Fish, _Star Trek’s_ Universal Translator, and _Doctor Who’s_ Tardis Translation Circuit are all variants of the same thing—computational devices that grant the ability to translate between any two languages. Casting aside their chimeric origins, the social need for realizing such visions has never been greater. For one, an increasingly interconnected world calls for the development of technologies that can facilitate and streamline multilingual contact both online and offline. Moreover, the proliferation of mobile devices and the platform economy worldwide provides the vehicle for on-demand speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) to become a staple in most people’s lives.

Despite the centrality of speech in everyday communication, machine translation (MT) systems today remain text-centric. Speech support, if and when present, is often seen as cursory to its text-based counterpart. While single, unimodal models such as No Language Left Behind (NLLB; ) push text-to-text translation (T2TT) coverage to more than 200 languages, unified S2ST models are far from achieving similar scope or performance. This modality-based disparity could be attributed to many causes, but audio data scarcity and modeling constraints remain key obstacles. The very challenge around why speech is harder to tackle from an MT standpoint—that it encodes more information and expressive components—is also why it is superior at conveying intent and forging stronger social bonds between interlocutors.

Bringing the Babel Fish into technical reality hinges on developing foundational speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) systems. Today, existing systems of such kind suffer from three main shortcomings. One, they tend to focus on high-resource languages such as English, Spanish, and French, leaving many low-resource languages behind. Two, they mostly service translations from a source language into English (X–eng) and not vice versa (eng–X). Three, most S2ST systems today rely heavily on cascaded systems composed of multiple subsystems that perform translation progressively—e.g., from automatic speech recognition (ASR) to T2TT, and subsequently text-to-speech (TTS) synthesis in a 3-stage system. Attempts to unify these multiple capabilities under one singular entity have led to early iterations of end-to-end speech translation systems . However, these systems do not match the performance of their cascaded counterparts , which are more equipped to leverage large-scale multilingual components (e.g., NLLB for T2TT or Whisper for ASR ) and unsupervised or weakly-supervised data.

To address these limitations, we introduce **<span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span>** (**M**assively **M**ultilingual & **M**ultimodal **M**achine **T**ranslation), a unified system that supports ASR, T2TT, speech-to-text translation (S2TT), text-to-speech translation (T2ST), and S2ST(see <a href="#tbl:tasks" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="tbl:tasks">[tbl:tasks]</a> for an overview). To build this, we used 1 million hours of open speech audio data to learn self-supervised speech representations with <span class="smallcaps">w2v-BERT 2.0</span>. Subsequently, we created a multimodal corpus of automatically aligned speech translations of more than 470,000 hours, dubbed <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessAlign</span>. We then combined a filtered subset of this corpus with human-labeled and pseudo-labeled data, totaling 406,000 hours. Drawing on this assembled dataset, we developed the first multitasking system that performs S2ST from 100 languages to English (100-eng) and from English to 35 languages (eng-35), S2TT for 100-eng and eng-95 languages, ASR for 96, zero-shot T2ST for 95-eng and eng-35 languages, as well as T2TT for 95-eng and eng-95(see <a href="#tbl:coverage" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="tbl:coverage">[tbl:coverage]</a> for an overview).

We find that <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span>, the larger model of the two we release, outperforms the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) end-to-end S2TT model (<span class="smallcaps">AudioPaLM-2-8B-AST</span> ) by 4.2 <span class="smallcaps">BLEU</span> points on <span class="smallcaps">Fleurs</span>  when translating into English (i.e., an improvement of 20%). Compared to cascaded models, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span> improves translation accuracy by over 2 <span class="smallcaps">BLEU</span> points. When translating from English, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span> improves on the previous SOTA (<span class="smallcaps">XLS-R-2B-S2T</span> ) by 2.8 <span class="smallcaps">BLEU</span> points on <span class="smallcaps">CoVoST</span> 2 , and its performance is on par with cascaded systems on <span class="smallcaps">Fleurs</span>. On the S2ST task, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span> outperforms strong 3-stage cascaded models (ASR, T2TT and TTS) by 2.6 <span class="smallcaps">ASR-BLEU</span> points on <span class="smallcaps">Fleurs</span>. On <span class="smallcaps">CVSS</span>, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span> outperforms a 2-stage cascaded model (<span class="smallcaps">Whisper-Large-v2</span>+ <span class="smallcaps">YourTTS</span> ) by a large margin of 8.5 <span class="smallcaps">ASR-BLEU</span> points (a 50% improvement). Preliminary human evaluations of S2TT outputs evinced similarly impressive results. For translations from English, XSTS scores for 24 evaluated languages are consistently above 4 (out of 5); for into English directions, we see significant improvement over <span class="smallcaps">Whisper-Large-v2</span>’s baseline for 7 out of 24 languages.

In addition, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span> further outperforms <span class="smallcaps">Whisper-Large-v2</span>  on <span class="smallcaps">Fleurs</span> ASR with an average word error rate (<span class="smallcaps">WER</span>) reduction of 45% over 77 overlapping languages. When evaluating T2TT on <span class="smallcaps">Flores</span> , our model matches the performance of NLLB-3.3B  when translating into English and improves by 1 chrF point on average when translating from English. To further evaluate <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span>’s performance in S2TT and S2ST, we developed <span class="smallcaps">Blaser 2.0</span>, a language and modality-agnostic evaluation metric for text or speech translation. <span class="smallcaps">Blaser 2.0</span> enables evaluation across speech and text modalities with similar accuracy to its predecessor —BLASER —when it comes to quality estimation. We also evaluated model robustness against background noises and speaker variations by creating open robustness benchmarks based on <span class="smallcaps">Fleurs</span>. Result-wise, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span> is more robust than <span class="smallcaps">Whisper-Large-v2</span> against background noises and speaker variations with an average improvement of 38% and 49%, respectively.

Regarding Responsible AI, we focused on added toxicity and gender bias evaluation. On average, we find a low prevalence of added toxicity, varying between 0.11% and 0.21% across modalities, datasets, and translation directions. We significantly reduce added toxicity in all conditions when compared to state-of-the-art models (ranging from 26% to 63%). The greatest added toxicity reduction is achieved for S2TT when compared to <span class="smallcaps">Whisper-Large-v2</span>. Beyond this, we also evaluated for gender bias on the <span class="smallcaps">Multilingual HolisticBias</span> datasets and found that <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span> overgeneralizes to masculine forms when translating from neutral terms (with an average preference of $`\sim`$<!-- -->10%) while showing a lack of robustness when varying gender by an amount of $`\sim`$<!-- -->3%. For these conditions, <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span> achieved comparable results to state-of-the-art models. We document these effects to motivate further mitigation efforts.

To spur further research in speech translation and to make our work available to the community, we open-source the following at <https://github.com/facebookresearch/seamless_communication>:

- <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T</span> models, including model weights for <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Large</span>(2.3B parameters) and <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessM4T-Medium</span>(1.2B parameters), as well as their inference code and fine-tuning recipes powered by our new modeling toolkit <span class="smallcaps">Fairseq2</span>.[^1]

- Tools for creating aligned speech data, including metadata to recreate the unfiltered 470,000 hours of <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessAlign</span>, <span class="smallcaps">Stopes</span>-based pipelines[^2] to create alignments similar to <span class="smallcaps">SeamlessAlign</span>, and <span class="smallcaps">Sonar</span> for speech encoders in 37 languages and text encoders in 200 languages.[^3]

- A text-free S2ST automatic evaluation model, <span class="smallcaps">Blaser 2.0</span>, inclusive of model weights and inference scripts.

The rest of the article is structured as follows: <a href="#sec:appendix:problem" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="sec:appendix:problem">[sec:appendix:problem]</a> describes the sociotechnical dimensions of multimodal translation and motivates why speech is an important modality to tackle in the context of MT research. It also includes the list of languages and evaluation metrics that our work covers. <a href="#sec:appendix:data" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="sec:appendix:data">[sec:appendix:data]</a> discusses how we created a corpus of automatically aligned speech translations of more than 470,000 hours by developing an extended speech-language identification system and a new multimodal text embedding space imperative to our data mining process. <a href="#sec:appendix:modeling" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="sec:appendix:modeling">[sec:appendix:modeling]</a> details the various modeling techniques we devised to train a multimodal and multitasking translation model that supports multiple languages for source and target sides in both text and speech. <a href="#sec:appendix:evaluation" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="sec:appendix:evaluation">[sec:appendix:evaluation]</a> documents the automatic and human evaluation of our translation outputs, and the robustness of our models in various settings. <a href="#sec:appendix:ra" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="sec:appendix:ra">[sec:appendix:ra]</a> focuses on our Responsible AI effort, where we evaluated our model outputs for bias and toxicity. Finally, we conclude in <a href="#sec:conclusion" data-reference-type="ref+Label" data-reference="sec:conclusion">[sec:conclusion]</a>, where we discuss the social impact of our work while reflecting on existing challenges and future possibilities.
