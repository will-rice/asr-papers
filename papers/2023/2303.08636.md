# HYBRIDFORMER: IMPROVING SQUEEZEFORMER WITH HYBRID ATTENTION AND NSR MECHANISM

Yuguang Yang Yu Pan Jingjing Yin Ximalaya Inc., ShangHai, China Jiangyu Han Ximalaya Inc., ShangHai, China Lei Ma Heng Lu

###### Abstract

SqueezeFormer has recently shown impressive performance in automatic speech recognition (ASR). However, its inference speed suffers from the quadratic complexity of softmax-attention (SA). In addition, limited by the large convolution kernel size, the local modeling ability of SqueezeFormer is insufficient. In this paper, we propose a novel method HybridFormer to improve SqueezeFormer in a fast and efficient way. Specifically, we first incorporate linear attention (LA) and propose a hybrid LASA paradigm to increase the model’s inference speed. Second, a hybrid neural architecture search (NAS) guided structural re-parameterization (SRep) mechanism, termed NSR, is proposed to enhance the ability of the model to extract local interactions. Extensive experiments conducted on the LibriSpeech dataset demonstrate that our proposed HybridFormer can achieve a 9.1% relative word error rate (WER) reduction over SqueezeFormer on the test-other dataset. Furthermore, when input speech is 30s, the HybridFormer can improve the model’s inference speed up to 18%. Our source code is available online¹.

Index Terms—  ASR, SqueezeFormer, hybrid LASA, NSR mechanism

## 1 Introduction

^(†)^(†)\*Yu Pan and Yuguang Yang are co-first authors and contribute equally. ^(†)^(†)$`\dagger`$ Lei Ma and Heng Lu are the corresponding author.^(†)^(†1) Code: https://github.com/yygle/HybridFormer

Over the past years, end-to-end (E2E) based speech recognition has become mainstream for ASR society. Among them, the transformer-based methods \[1, 2, 3, 4\] are adept at exploiting long-term interactions, while convolution neural network (CNN) based methods \[5, 6, 7\] perform well in extracting local context-dependent features. Afterward, by incorporating CNN into transformer, Conformer \[8\] is proposed to capture both global and local acoustic information at the same time.

Although Conformer has shown promising performance in various speech related tasks \[9, 10, 11\], authors of SqueezeFormer \[12\] argued that the design choices of Conformer are sub-optimal and there is great unnecessary temporal redundancy in the deep layers of the model. Therefore, as shown in the left part of Fig.1, SqueezeFormer refines the micro and macro architectures of Conformer to improve the ASR performance. Besides, by computing attention over a sequence with reduced length, its computational overhead can be further decreased.

Nevertheless, in this paper, we believe that SqueezeFormer can be improved in several aspects. First, like other SA-based ASR models, SqueezeFormer also suffers from the quadratic temporal-space complexity concerning the input sequence length, especially in the unsqueeze blocks. Second, although SqueezeFormer is a hybrid attention-convolution model like Conformer, constrained by the large convolution kernel size (i.e., 31), its ability to exploit local features is still relatively limited.

Motivated by the aforementioned analysis, we propose a fast and efficient method termed HybridFormer to bridge this gap. First, we propose a novel hybrid Linear Attention\[13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18\] and Softmax Attention (LASA) paradigm, based on SqueezeFormer’s architecture, to alleviate the quadratic complexity of the SA operation. Second, we present a novel hybrid NAS-guided SRep mechanism (NSR) and apply it to the convolution module to improve system performance without increasing any computational complexity during inference. Third, we contribute the first AED\[19\] based SqueezeFormer on WeNet \[20\] toolkit and the core code of our proposed HybridFormer is also available online.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we describe the details of the proposed method HybridFormer. In Section 3, the experimental dataset and setups are introduced to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method. The conclusions are drawn in Section 4.

## 2 PROPOSED METHODOLOGY

### 2.1 Overview

As illustrated in the right part of Fig. 1, the encoder part of our proposed HybridFormer is mainly composed of subsampling block, HybridFormer LA block and HybridFormer SA block. In particular, the proposed hybrid NSR mechanism is adopted in all these blocks.

The decoder is the vanilla transformer which is not the main focus in this work. Besides the cross-entropy loss, connectionist temporal classification loss \[21\] is combined to jointly train our proposed method E2E.

### 2.2 Hybrid RoPE-based LASA Paradigm

To obtain faster inference speed, a natural idea is to use the LA operator to replace the SA operator. Nonetheless, according to the studies of \[13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18\], the performance of SA-based ASR models generally performs better than LA-based ASR models due to many factors, such as the approximation errors of low-rank decomposition or kernel-based LA methods.

Hence, on account of the above-mentioned analysis, we present a novel hybrid LASA paradigm which is shown in Fig. 1 (Right), i.e., use LA and SA operators on different layers of the model upon its sampling rate to balance the inference speed and performance. Moreover, since the relative position embedding (RPE) used by SqueezeFormer is incompatible with the LA module, we adopt RoPE\[14\]-based SA and LA operators to mitigate this mismatch.

To be specific, assume $`{I\hspace{0pt}S_{N}} = {\{{{x_{i},i} \in {\lbrack 1,N\rbrack}}\}}`$ denotes the N-length input sequence, where $`x_{i}`$ is the $`i^{t\hspace{0pt}h}`$ token of $`I\hspace{0pt}S_{N}`$, and $`d`$ is the dimension. The proposed RoPE-based LASA paradigm first computes the corresponding query $`q_{m}`$ , key $`k_{n}`$, and value $`v_{n}`$ of the $`x_{m}`$ and $`x_{n}`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
q_{m} & {= {f_{q}\hspace{0pt}{(x_{m},m)}} = {R_{\Theta,m}^{d}\hspace{0pt}W_{q}\hspace{0pt}x_{m}}} \\
k_{n} & {= {f_{k}\hspace{0pt}{(x_{n},n)}} = {R_{\Theta,n}^{d}\hspace{0pt}W_{k}\hspace{0pt}x_{n}}} \\
v_{n} & {= {f_{v}\hspace{0pt}{(x_{n},n)}} = {W_{v}\hspace{0pt}x_{n}}}
\end{aligned}
```

where $`x_{m}`$ and $`x_{n}`$ are the $`m^{t\hspace{0pt}h}`$ and $`n^{t\hspace{0pt}h}`$ embeddings, $`m`$ and $`n`$ indicate the positions, $`W_{q}`$, $`W_{k}`$, and $`W_{v}`$ are the trainable matrices of the linear projection layer of $`q_{m}`$, $`k_{n}`$, and $`v_{n}`$, $`\Theta = {\{{{\theta_{i} = 10000^{- {{2\hspace{0pt}{({i - 1})}}/d}}},{i \in {\lbrack 1,2,\ldots,{d/2}\rbrack}}}\}}`$ is a set of pre-defined hyper-parameters, $`R_{\Theta,m}^{d}\hspace{0pt}W_{q}\hspace{0pt}x_{n}`$ is a special case of $`{({x_{m}\hspace{0pt}W_{q}})}\hspace{0pt}e^{i\hspace{0pt}m\hspace{0pt}\theta}`$ when $`d = 2`$, where $`\theta`$ donates the angle of rotation here.

```math
R_{\Theta,m}^{d} = \begin{pmatrix}
{\cos{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{1}}} & {- {\sin{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{1}}}} & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\
{\sin{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{1}}} & {\cos{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{1}}} & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & {\cos{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{d/2}}} & {- {\sin{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{d/2}}}} \\
0 & 0 & \cdots & {\sin{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{d/2}}} & {\cos{m\hspace{0pt}\theta_{d/2}}}
\end{pmatrix}
```

is the rotary matrix with $`\Theta`$. Then, the attention weight $`A_{m\hspace{0pt}n}`$ is computed using $`q_{m}`$ and $`k_{n}`$:

```math
A_{m\hspace{0pt}n} = {q_{m}^{T}\hspace{0pt}k_{n}} = {x_{m}^{T}\hspace{0pt}W_{q}^{T}\hspace{0pt}R_{\Theta,{n - m}}^{d}\hspace{0pt}W_{k}\hspace{0pt}x_{n}}
```

where $`R_{\Theta,{n - m}}^{d} = {{(R_{\Theta,m}^{d})}^{T}\hspace{0pt}R_{\Theta,n}^{d}}`$. More detailed information can be referred to \[14\].

When the sampling rate is 80ms, the RoPE-based SA operator is adopted, which is defined as:

```math
{H\hspace{0pt}y\hspace{0pt}b\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}d_{S\hspace{0pt}A}} = {\sum\limits_{n = 1}^{N}{\frac{\text{exp}\hspace{0pt}{({A_{m\hspace{0pt}n}/\sqrt{d}})}}{\sum_{j = 1}^{N}{\text{exp}\hspace{0pt}{({A_{m\hspace{0pt}j}/\sqrt{d}})}}}\hspace{0pt}v_{n}}}
```

When the sampling rate is 40ms which is relatively low, the RoPE-based LA operator is incorporated for acceleration, which is defined as:

```math
{H\hspace{0pt}y\hspace{0pt}b\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}d_{L\hspace{0pt}A}} = \frac{\sum_{n = 1}^{N}{{({R_{\Theta,m}^{d}\hspace{0pt}\varphi\hspace{0pt}{({W_{q}\hspace{0pt}x_{m}})}})}^{T}\hspace{0pt}{({R_{\Theta,n}^{d}\hspace{0pt}\varphi\hspace{0pt}{({W_{k}\hspace{0pt}x_{n}})}})}\hspace{0pt}v_{n}}}{\sum_{n = 1}^{N}{\varphi\hspace{0pt}{({W_{q}\hspace{0pt}x_{m}})}^{T}\hspace{0pt}\varphi\hspace{0pt}{({W_{k}\hspace{0pt}x_{n}})}}}
```

where $`\varphi\hspace{0pt}{( \cdot )}`$ is the non-negative function.

### 2.3 NSR: Hybrid NAS-guided SRep Mechanism

Despite the fact that SqueezeFormer is a convolution-enhanced transformer that could integrate the advantages of attention and convolution operations, we note that its convolution kernel size (i.e., 31) is relatively large, which weakens its capability to exploit local interactions to a certain extent.

Consequently, we propose an effective hybrid NAS-guided SRep mechanism called NSR and apply it to the convolution module to address this issue. Noticeably, our NSR mechanism mainly consists of training and inference stages, among which the training stage includes search and re-train stages. The training phase aims to search for the most suitable convolution kernel combination for the current model using our proposed Darts \[23\] like technique, so as to improve the model’s performance. The goal of the inference phase is to convert the multi-branch model into a single-branch model, so that no additional computational cost will be added during the inference phase.

#### 2.3.1 Training Phase of our NSR-based Convolution Module

As shown in Fig. 2, the training phase of the proposed NSR-based convolution module starts with a gating mechanism \[22\], which is formed of a point-wise convolution layer and a gated linear unit (GLU) layer, then followed by our NSR-based depth-wise convolution layer which contains a trunk 31×1 convolution branch and additional convolution branches searched by our proposed NAS technique, a batchnorm (BN) layer, a Swish activation layer, and a point-wise convolution layer.

![Refer to caption](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.08636/assets/images/search-phase.png)

Fig. 2: Training schematic of NSR-based convolution module

As for NAS-searched branches, a set of candidate operations $`C = {\{ C^{(i,j)},i \in {\lbrack 0,1,\ldots,30\rbrack},j \in {\lbrack 0,1,\ldots,11\rbrack}\}}`$ is set up as the search space, where $`C`$ represents the Convolution with BN operations, $`i`$ represents the convolution operator with different kernel size (from 0 to 30, 0 represents the branch with only BN layer) and $`j`$ represents the $`j^{t\hspace{0pt}h}`$ block. To make the search space continuous, we adopt the operation of \[23, 24\]:

```math
\alpha_{(i,j)} = \frac{\text{exp}\hspace{0pt}{(c_{(i,j)})}}{\sum_{k = 0}^{30}{\text{exp}\hspace{0pt}{(c_{k,j})}}}
```

where $`\alpha\hspace{0pt}{(i,j)}`$ is the learnable weights parameterized by the vector $`c\hspace{0pt}{(i,j)}`$. The main task of NAS is to capture the most suitable convolution operators by jointly training $`\alpha`$ and the model weights $`w`$, and its pseudo-code is described in Algorithm 1.

| Algorithm 1: _NSR_-NAS guided SRep mechanism                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Stage1 (search):                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 1. Create a mixed operation $`C^{(i,j)}`$ and the corresponding $`\alpha^{i,j}`$                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 2. Split the training dataset equally into train1 and train2                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| 3. while warm-up do:                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|  Update weight $`w`$ by descending $`{\nabla_{w}L_{t\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}n\hspace{0pt}1}}\hspace{0pt}(w,\alpha)`$                                                                                                                                                  |
| 4. while not converged do:                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|  1) Update $`\alpha`$ by descending $`{\nabla_{\alpha}L_{t\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}n\hspace{0pt}2}}\hspace{0pt}\left( {w - {\xi\hspace{0pt}{\nabla_{w}L_{t\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}n\hspace{0pt}1}}\hspace{0pt}(w,\alpha)}},\alpha \right)`$ |
|  2) Update $`w`$ by descending $`{\nabla_{w}L_{t\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}n\hspace{0pt}1}}\hspace{0pt}(w,\alpha)`$                                                                                                                                                      |
| 5. Average the best checkpoints of $`L_{v\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}l}`$ with $`\alpha`$                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|  ($`L_{{{t\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}n}/v}\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}l}`$ stands for the loss on the train or valid dataset)                                                                                                                                               |
| Stage2 (re-train):                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 1. Crop branches with negative learnable weights $`\alpha^{(i,j)}`$                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| 2. Re-softmax the positive learnable weights as $`\alpha'`$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 3. Train the new model weight $`w'`$ with the retained branches and $`\alpha'`$                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|  by descending $`{\nabla_{w'}L_{t\hspace{0pt}r\hspace{0pt}a\hspace{0pt}i\hspace{0pt}n}}\hspace{0pt}\left( w',\alpha' \right)`$                                                                                                                                                                   |

#### 2.3.2 Inference Phase of our NSR-based Convolution Module

As depicted in Fig. 3, the inference structure of the NSR-based convolution module will be converted into one single branch, and its corresponding parameters will be transformed as well. To be specific, the BN layer and convolution layer will be merged first. Then, the parameters of the NAS-searched and trunk convolution branches will also be merged, as the NAS-searched convolution branches can be considered as the degraded 31×1 convolution via simple algebraic transformation \[25\]. In this way, the NSR-based convolution module only has one single branch which is the 31×1 convolution layer during inference time.

![Refer to caption](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.08636/assets/images/inference-phase.png)

Fig. 3: Inference schematic of NSR-based convolution module

Accordingly, the ability of our model to exploit local features is greatly improved while not increasing any computational cost.

## 3 EXPERIMENTS

### 3.1 Configurations

To verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, we conduct experiments on the public LibriSpeech \[26\] dataset. We train all models on the LibriSpeech training dataset, and evaluate them on LibriSpeech test-clean and test-other datasets.

Specifically, audios are converted to 80-dimensional FBank. SentencePiece \[27\] is used to construct a 5000 sub-word byte pair encoding tokenizer using the transcripts of LibriSpeech, and SpecAugment \[28\] is used for data augmentation. All methods are optimized by Adamw \[29\], where the learning rate is 0.001, and the warmup ratio is 0.2 with the NoamAnnealing scheduler. All models contain 12 encoder blocks and 6 decoder blocks. As for the encoder, the convolution kernel size is 31, the number of attention heads is 4, the hidden dimension of the attention and FFN layers are 256 and 1024. As per the decoder, a single directional transformer with 4 attention heads is used, and the hidden dimension of the FFN layer is 2048. All experiments are conducted on WeNet and uniformly trained under 8 A100 GPUs.

### 3.2 Main Results

In this section, we compare the WER and inference speed of our proposed HybridFormer with baseline SqueezeFormer and Conformer, which are illustrated in Table 1 and Fig. 4. In order to make a fair comparison, two evaluation methods CTC greedy search (CTC) and attention rescoring (Rescore) are adopted.

|               |           |            |         |            |         |
| ------------- | --------- | ---------- | ------- | ---------- | ------- |
| Model         | Params(M) | Test Clean |         | Test Other |         |
|               |           | CTC        | Rescore | CTC        | Rescore |
| Conformer     | 22.15     | 3.82       | 3.28    | 9.77       | 8.86    |
| Squeezeformer | 22.22     | 3.51       | 3.07    | 9.28       | 8.44    |
| HybridFormer  | 21.45     | 3.34       | 2.96    | 8.44       | 7.70    |

Table 1: WER (%) comparison of different models.

In terms of the WER performance, it can be observed from Table 1 that our proposed HybridFormer consistently achieves the best WER with minimum parameters. Particularly, when we use CTC, our proposed method obtains a 4.8% and a 9.1% relative WER reduction on test-clean and test other datasets compared with baseline SqueezeFormer. When the Rescore is utilized, the proposed HybridFormer obtains a 3.6% and an 8.8% relative WER reduction on test-clean and test-other datasets.

![Refer to caption](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.08636/assets/images/speed.png)

Fig. 4: Comparison of Inference speed on LibriSpeech.

In terms of inference speed, all methods are tested on a single A10 GPU. From Fig. 4, we can see that our HybridFormer has the best performance. Compared with baseline SqueezeFormer, when the input speech is 30s length, the proposed HybridFormer increases the inference speed by relatively 18%.

## 4 CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, we propose a fast and efficient ASR method termed HybridFormer, which is a hybrid NSR-augmented LASA attention-convolution model. With our proposed hybrid LASA paradigm and NSR mechanism, HybridFormer could not only accelerate the inference speed but also enhance the model’s feature representation ability, thus improving the recognition performance. Extensive experiments performed on the public LibriSpeech benchmark prove the effectiveness of our proposed method.
