Orson Mengara¹ ¹ INRS-EMT, University of Québec, Montréal, QC, Canada.\
{orson.mengara@inrs.ca}

###### Abstract

Since the advent of generative artificial intelligence \[[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib1)\],\[[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib2)\], every company and researcher has been rushing to develop their own generative models, whether commercial or not. Given the large number of users of these powerful new tools, there is currently no intrinsically verifiable way to explain from the ground up what happens when LLMs (large language models) learn. For example, those based on automatic speech recognition systems, which have to rely on huge and astronomical amounts of data collected from all over the web to produce fast and efficient results, In this article, we develop a backdoor attack called “MarketBackFinal 2.0”, based on acoustic data poisoning, MarketBackFinal 2.0 is mainly based on modern stock market models. In order to show the possible vulnerabilities of speech-based transformers that may rely on LLMs.

###### Index Terms:

Backdoor, Stock market , Bayesian approach, optimization, Adversarial machine learning, Poisoning attacks, Stock exchange, Derivative instruments.

## II Data Poisoning attack Machine Learning

Let $`\mathcal{D} = \left\{ \left( {\mathbf{x}}_{i},y_{i} \right) \right\}_{i = 1}^{N}`$ be a clean training set, and $`C:{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow}`$ $`\mathcal{Y}`$ denotes the functionality of the target neural network. For each sound $`{\mathbf{x}}_{i}`$ in $`\mathcal{D}`$, we have $`{\mathbf{x}}_{i} \in \mathcal{X} = {\lbrack 0,1\rbrack}^{C \times W \times H}`$, and $`y_{i} \in \mathcal{Y} = {\{ 1,\ldots,J\}}`$ is the corresponding label, where $`J`$ is the number of label classes. To launch an attack, backdoor adversaries first need to poison the selected clean samples $`\mathcal{D}_{p}`$ with covert transformation $`T{( \cdot )}`$. Then the poisoned samples are mixed with clean ones before training a backdoored model, the process of which can be formalized as: $`\mathcal{D}_{t} = {\mathcal{D} \cup \mathcal{D}_{p}}`$, where $`{\mathcal{D}_{p} = {\left( x_{i}',y_{t} \right) \mid x'} = {T{(x)}}},{\left( x_{i},y_{i} \right) \in \mathcal{D}_{p}}`$. The deep neural network (DNNs) is then optimized as follows:

```math
{{\min\limits_{\mathbf{\Theta}}{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{N_{b}}{\mathcal{L}\left( {f\left( {\mathbf{x}}_{i};\mathbf{\Theta} \right)},y_{i} \right)}}} + {\sum\limits_{j = 1}^{N_{p}}{\mathcal{L}\left( {f\left( {\mathbf{x}}_{r}';\mathbf{\Theta} \right)},y_{t} \right)}}}.
```

where $`N_{b} = {|\mathcal{D}|}`$ , $`N_{p} = \left| \mathcal{D}_{p} \right|`$.

However, the third party can poison the training dataset to hijack the model in the future. We assume that the model owner and the trusted third party play the roles of defender and attacker, respectively, in the threat model. The threat model is shown in Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ II Data Poisoning attack Machine Learning ‣ Trading Devil Final: Backdoor attack via Stock market and Bayesian Optimization").

The knowledge that an attacker can access can generally be classified \[[55](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib55)\] into two categories. Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ II Data Poisoning attack Machine Learning ‣ Trading Devil Final: Backdoor attack via Stock market and Bayesian Optimization"): White and black box settings. In a white-box environment, the adversary understands and controls the target dataset and model, including the ability to access and modify the dataset, as well as parameters and the structure of the model. However, in the stricter framework of the black-box, the attacker is only able to manipulate part of the training data but has no knowledge of the structure and parameters of the target model, such as weights, hyperparameters, configurations, etc.

The adversary seeks to have the target model behave as expected on benign data while operating in a way described by the adversary on samples that have been poisoned, as seen in Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ II Data Poisoning attack Machine Learning ‣ Trading Devil Final: Backdoor attack via Stock market and Bayesian Optimization"). A formulation of the enemy’s objective is:

$`{{\min\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}}\mathcal{L}}\left( \mathcal{D}^{b},\mathcal{D}^{p},\mathcal{M}^{\ast} \right)} =`$$`\sum\limits_{x_{i} \in \mathcal{D}^{b}}{l\left( {\mathcal{M}^{\ast}\left( x_{i} \right)},y_{i} \right)}`$$`{+ {\sum\limits_{x_{j} \in \mathcal{D}^{p}}{l\left( {\mathcal{M}^{\ast}\left( {x_{r} \circ \varepsilon} \right)},y_{t} \right)}}},`$

### III-A Bayesian optimization formalization.

Formally, the purpose of BO (Bayesian-Optimization) \[[56](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib56)\],\[[57](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib57)\] is to retrieve the optimum $`\mathbf{x}^{\star}`$ of a black-box function (Figure [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ III-A Bayesian optimization formalization. ‣ III Adversarial Machine Learning in Finance via Bayesian Approach ‣ Trading Devil Final: Backdoor attack via Stock market and Bayesian Optimization")) $`f{(\mathbf{x})}`$ where $`\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}`$ and $`\mathcal{X}`$ is the input space where $`f{(\mathbf{x})}`$ can be observed.

We want to retrieve $`\mathbf{x}^{\star}`$ such that,

```math
{\mathbf{x}^{\star} = {{\arg{\min\limits_{\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}}f}}{(\mathbf{x})}}},
```

assuming minimization (or maximization): We can define a BO method by,

```math
{\mathcal{A} = {(\mathcal{M},{\alpha{( \cdot )}},{p{({{f{(\mathbf{x})}} \mid \mathcal{D}})}})}},
```

where $`f{(\mathbf{x})}`$ is the black-box that we want to optimize (Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ III-A Bayesian optimization formalization. ‣ III Adversarial Machine Learning in Finance via Bayesian Approach ‣ Trading Devil Final: Backdoor attack via Stock market and Bayesian Optimization")), $`\mathcal{M}`$ is a probabilistic surrogate model, $`\alpha{( \cdot )}`$ is an acquisition, or decision, function, and $`p{({{f{(\mathbf{x})}} \mid \mathcal{D}})}`$ is a predictive distribution of the observation of $`\mathbf{x}`$ and $`\mathcal{D} = \left\{ \left( \mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i} \right)\mid{i = {1,\ldots,t}} \right\}`$ is the dataset of previous observations (Figure [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ III-A Bayesian optimization formalization. ‣ III Adversarial Machine Learning in Finance via Bayesian Approach ‣ Trading Devil Final: Backdoor attack via Stock market and Bayesian Optimization")) at iteration $`t`$.

BA measures the classifier’s accuracy on clean (benign) test examples. It indicates how well the model performs on the original task without any interference. ASR, in turn, measures the success of the backdoor attack, i.e., in causing the model to misclassify poisoned test examples. ```math


where $`{\mathbb{I}}{( \cdot )}`$ is the indicator function, $`\mathcal{M}^{\ast}`$ is the target model, and $`x_{i} \circ \varepsilon`$ and $`y_{t}`$ denote the poisoned sample and target label, respectively.

```math
{BA} = \frac{\sum_{i = 1}^{M}{{\mathbb{I}}\left( {{\mathcal{M}^{\ast}\left( x_{i} \right)} = y_{i}} \right)}}{M}
```

## Concepts of LIBOR Market.

###### Definition .1 (LIBOR Market).

The $`\delta_{i}`$-forward-LIBOR rate $`L_{i}{(t)}`$ is the simple yield for the time interval $`\left\lbrack t_{i - 1},t_{i} \right\rbrack`$, i.e. with $`\delta_{i} = {t_{i} - t_{i - 1}}`$ we define:

$`\begin{array}{r}
{{{L_{i}{(t)}} = {L\left( t;t_{i - 1},t_{i} \right)} = {\frac{1}{\delta_{i}}\frac{{P\left( t,t_{i - 1} \right)} - {P\left( t,t_{i} \right)}}{P\left( t,t_{i} \right)}}}.}
\end{array}`$

By the definition of the $`t_{i}`$-forward measure $`{\mathbb{Q}}_{i}:={\mathbb{Q}}_{t_{i}}`$, $`L_{i}{(t)}`$ is a $`{\mathbb{Q}}_{i}`$-martingale. Thus, it is an immediate consequence that if we want to model log-normal forward-LIBOR rates in a diffusion setting, we have to choose the following dynamics under $`{\mathbb{Q}}_{i}`$ :

```math
{{dL_{i}{(t)}} = {L_{i}{(t)}\sigma_{i}{(t)}dW_{i}{(t)}}},
```

Here, $`W_{i}(`$.$`)`$ is a (for the moment one-dimensional) $`{\mathbb{Q}}_{i}`$-Brownian motion, $`\sigma_{i}{(t)}`$ a bounded and deterministic function.

###### Remark.

The risk-free deposit rates $`D_{s}^{i}`$ for deposits between $`t_{i}`$ and $`t_{i + 1}`$ are defined by,

```math
{{1 + {\delta_{i}D_{s}^{i}}} = \frac{P^{D}\left( s,t_{i} \right)}{P^{D}\left( s,t_{i + 1} \right)}}.
```

The factors $`\delta_{i}`$ are the accrual factors or day count fractions and represent the fraction of the year spanned by the interval $`\left\lbrack t_{i},t_{i + 1} \right\rbrack`$ .

```math
{dD_{t}^{j}} = {{{\gamma_{j}\left( D_{t}^{j},t \right)} \cdot d}W_{t}^{j + 1}}
```

$`\gamma_{j}{({0 \leq j \leq {n - 1}})}`$ are $`m`$-dimensional functions. The Brownian motion change between the $`N_{t}`$ and the $`P^{D}\left( t,t_{j + 1} \right)`$ is given by,

```math
{{dW_{t}^{j + 1}} = {{dW_{t}} + {\nu\left( t,t_{j + 1} \right)dt}}}.
```

$`{\nu\left( t,t_{j + 1} \right)} - {\nu\left( t,t_{j} \right)}`$ can be written \[[98](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib98)\] as:

```math
{{\nu\left( t,t_{j + 1} \right)} - {\nu\left( t,t_{j} \right)}} = {\frac{1}{D_{t}^{j} + \frac{1}{\delta_{j}}}\gamma_{j}\left( D_{t}^{j},t \right)}
```

```math
{{dW_{t}^{j + 1}} = {{- {\sum\limits_{i = {j + 1}}^{n - 1}{\frac{1}{D_{t}^{i} + \frac{1}{\delta_{i}}}\gamma_{i}\left( D_{t}^{i},t \right)dt}}} + {dW_{t}^{n}}}}.
```

$`\begin{array}{r}
{{dL_{t}^{j}} = {{- {\left( {\sum\limits_{i = {j + 1}}^{n - 1}{{{\frac{1}{D_{t}^{i} + \frac{1}{\delta_{i}}}\gamma_{i}\left( D_{t}^{i},t \right)} \cdot \gamma_{j}}\left( D_{t}^{j},t \right)}} \right)dt}} +}} \\
{{{{\gamma_{j}\left( D_{t}^{j},t \right)} \cdot d}W_{t}^{n}}.}
\end{array}`$

by incorporating a diffusion approximation with $`a_{i} \leq {1/\delta_{i}}`$ we have,

```math
{\gamma_{j}{(D,t)}} = {\alpha{(t)}\left( {D + a_{j}} \right)\gamma_{j}}
```

$`\begin{array}{r}
{{dD_{t}^{j}} = {{- {\alpha^{2}{(t)}\left( {\sum\limits_{i = {j + 1}}^{n - 1}{{\frac{D_{t}^{i} + a_{i}}{D_{t}^{i} + \frac{1}{\delta_{i}}}\gamma_{i}} \cdot \gamma_{j}}} \right)\left( {D_{t}^{j} + a_{j}} \right)dt}} +}} \\
{{{{\alpha{(t)}\left( {D_{t}^{j} + a_{j}} \right)\gamma_{j}} \cdot d}W_{t}^{n}}.}
\end{array}`$

with $`\alpha`$ a deterministic scalar function, $`a`$ a constant vector of dimension $`n`$ and $`\left( \gamma_{j} \right)_{j = {1,\ldots,n}}`$ vectors of dimension $`m`$.

###### Theorem 1.

## Concepts of hedging.

For hedging ²⁰²⁰20[Hedging concepts](https://www.math.columbia.edu/%C2%A0smirnov/over99.pdf) and risk management, the delta \[[99](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib99)\],\[[100](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib100)\] $`\Delta = \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}`$, i.e., the first partial derivative of the option’s value with respect to the index level: For the Greeks of a European call option.

```math
\Delta = \frac{\partial C}{\partial S} = {N\left( d_{1} \right)}
```

The gamma is the second partial derivative with respect to the index level,

```math
\gamma = \frac{\partial^{2}C}{\partial S^{2}} = \frac{N'\left( d_{1} \right)}{S\sigma\sqrt{T - t}}
```

The theta of an option is, by convention, the negative first partial derivative with respect to time-to-maturity $`t^{\ast} = {T - t}`$

```math
\theta = {- \frac{\partial C}{\partial t^{\ast}}} = {{- \frac{SN'\left( d_{1} \right)\sigma}{2\sqrt{T - t}}} - {re^{- {r{({T - t})}}}KN\left( d_{2} \right)}}
```

The rho of an option is the first partial derivative with respect to the short rate $`r`$

```math
\rho = \frac{\partial C}{\partial r} = {K{({T - t})}e^{- {r{({T - t})}}}N\left( d_{2} \right)}
```

The vega-which is obviously not a Greek letter-is the first partial derivative with respect to the volatility $`\sigma`$,

```math
{\mathbf{v}\mathbf{e}\mathbf{g}\mathbf{a}} = \frac{\partial C}{\partial\sigma} = {SN'\left( d_{1} \right)\sqrt{T - t}}
```

Hedging dynamics can be defined as:

```math
\Delta_{t}^{P} \equiv \frac{\partial P_{t}}{\partial S_{t}}
```

```math
{{dP_{t}} - {\Delta_{t}^{P}dS_{t}}} = 0
```

Investment banks are also often interested in replicating the payoff of such a put (or another option).

This is accomplished by setting up a replication portfolio consisting of $`\Delta_{t}^{P}`$ units of the underlying and $`\gamma_{t} \equiv {P_{t} - {\Delta_{t}^{P}S_{t}}}`$ units of the risk-less bond $`B_{t}`$ such that the resulting portfolio value equals the option value at any time $`t`$,

```math
P_{t} = {{\Delta_{t}^{P}S_{t}} + {\gamma_{t}B_{t}}}
```

or

```math
{dP_{t}} = {{\Delta_{t}^{P}dS_{t}} + {\gamma_{t}dB_{t}}}
```

For a plain vanilla put option this generally implies being short the underlying $`\left( {\Delta_{t}^{P} < 0} \right)`$ and long the bond. For a call option $`\left( {\Delta_{t}^{C} > 0} \right)`$ this implies the opposite.

A replication strategy $`{\left( \Delta_{t}^{P},\gamma_{t} \right),t} \in {\{ 0,\ldots,{\tau - {\Delta t}}\}}`$, is called self-financing if for $`t > 0`$ and $`\tau`$ being the exercise date (i.e, $`\tau = T`$ for a European option)

```math
{{{\Delta_{t}^{P}S_{t}} + {\gamma_{t}B_{t}}} = {{\Delta_{t - {\Delta t}}^{P}S_{t}} + {\gamma_{t - {\Delta t}}B_{t}}}}.
```

Self-financing: The value $`V_{t}`$ of a portfolio containing $`\psi_{t}`$ units of bonds and $`\phi_{t} = \left( \phi_{t,1},\ldots,\phi_{t,m} \right)^{\top}`$ units of various stocks at a given time $`t`$ is given by

```math
{V_{t} = {{B_{t}\psi_{t}} + {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m}{\phi_{t,i}S_{t,i}}}} = {{B_{t}\psi_{t}} + {\phi_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{t}}}}.
```

The particular value of the portfolio at time $`t`$ typically depends on the units of assets $`\left( \psi_{t},\phi_{t} \right)`$ held just before time $`t`$. The stochastic process $`\left\{ {{\left( \psi_{t},\phi_{t} \right),t} \geqslant 0} \right\}`$ can thus be interpreted as a trading strategy.

$`\left\{ {{\mathcal{F}_{t},t} \geqslant 0} \right\}`$.

```math
{dV_{t}} = {\left( {{B_{t}d\psi_{t}} + {\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\top}d\phi_{t}}} \right) + {\psi_{t}dB_{t}} + {\phi_{t}^{\top}d\mathbf{S}_{t}}}
```

The portfolio is called self-financing if

```math
{{B_{t}d\psi_{t}} + {\mathbf{S}_{t}^{\top}d\phi_{t}}} = 0
```

in which case

```math
{V_{T} = {V_{0} + {\int_{0}^{T}{\psi_{t}{dB_{\ell}}}} + {\int_{0}^{T}{\phi_{t}^{\top}{d\mathbf{S}_{t}}}}}}.
```

Arbitrage-free market: A market model is called arbitrage-free on $`\lbrack 0,T\rbrack`$ if, in essence, for every self-financing trading strategy $`\left\{ {{\left( \psi_{t},\phi_{t} \right),0} \leqslant t \leqslant T} \right\}`$,


## Concepts of Diffusion Riemannian.

Consider a solution $`u:{{\mathcal{M} \times {\lbrack 0,T\rbrack}}\rightarrow{\lbrack 0,\infty)}}`$ ,

```math
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = {\Delta u}
```

with $`{\int_{\mathcal{M}}{u{( \cdot ,0)}{d\mu_{g}}}} = 1`$, where $`\mu_{g}`$ is the Riemannian ²⁹²⁹29[Ricci-Riemann](https://bjlkeng.io/posts/hyperbolic-geometry-and-poincare-embeddings/) volume measure.

```math
{{\frac{d}{dt}{\int_{\mathcal{M}}{u{( \cdot ,t)}{d\mu_{g}}}}} = {\int_{\mathcal{M}}{\Delta u{d\mu_{g}}}} = 0},
```

The integral of $`u`$ over $`\mathcal{M}`$ remains one for each $`t \in {\lbrack 0,T\rbrack}`$, and in particular, $`u`$ can be viewed as a probability density of a measure $`\nu{(t)}`$ defined by $`{d\nu{(t)}}:={u{( \cdot ,t)}d\mu_{g}}`$.

$`\nu{(t)}`$ represents the probability distribution of a particle moving on the manifold under Brownian motion, with initial probability distribution $`\nu{(0)}`$,

Suppose $`g{(\tau)}`$ is a family of Riemannian metrics on $`\mathcal{M}`$, for $`\tau \in`$ $`\lbrack 0,T\rbrack`$. We call a flow of measures $`v{(\tau)}`$ a diffusion \[[116](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14573v7#bib.bib116)\] (representing the probability distribution of a Brownian particle as above) if $`{d\nu{(\tau)}} = {u{( \cdot ,\tau)}d\mu_{g{(\tau)}}}`$ with ,

```math
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau} = {{\Delta_{g{(\tau)}}u} - {\left( {\frac{1}{2}{{tr}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\tau}}} \right)u}}
```

$`\frac{\partial g}{\partial\tau} = {2{{Ric}{({g{(\tau)}})}}}`$, $`g{( \cdot )}`$ a Ricci flow with respect to a ’reverse’ time coordinate $`\tau`$ (that is, $`\tau = {C - t}`$ for some constant $`C`$ ),

```math
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau} = {{\Delta_{g{(\tau)}}u} - {Ru}}
```

$`\frac{d}{d\tau}{\int_{\mathcal{M}}{{d\nu}{(\tau)}}}`$$`= {\frac{d}{d\tau}{\int_{\mathcal{M}}{u{( \cdot ,\tau)}{d\mu_{g{(\tau)}}}}}}`$$`=`$$`\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left( {{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}d\mu_{g{(\tau)}}} + {u\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}d\mu_{g{(\tau)}}}} \right)`$$`= {\int_{\mathcal{M}}{\Delta_{g{(\tau)}}u{d\mu_{g{(\tau)}}}}} = 0`$

If $`g{(\tau)}`$ is a flow of Riemannian metrics for $`\tau \in {\lbrack a,b\rbrack}`$, and $`\nu{(\tau)}`$ is a diffusion (as defined above) then given any $`f:{{\mathcal{M} \times {\lbrack a,b\rbrack}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}}`$ solving $`{- \frac{\partial f}{\partial\tau}} = {\Delta_{g{(\tau)}}f}`$,

```math
{{\frac{d}{d\tau}{\int_{\mathcal{M}}{f{( \cdot ,\tau)}{d\nu}{(\tau)}}}} = 0}.
```
