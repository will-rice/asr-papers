## Abstract

Previous work on Tibetan automatic speech recognition (ASR) has relied on character error rate (CER) or syllable error rate (SER), since the absence of explicit word boundaries in Tibetan orthography complicates the calculation of word error rate (WER). In this paper, we instantiate Segmented Word Error Rate (SWER) — a word-level evaluation framework applying automatic segmentation before WER computation — across three methods (dictionary-based BoTok-SWER, LLM-based Gem-SWER, and BERT-UPOS-based BERT-SWER), and conduct a comprehensive benchmark of five ASR architectures and six adaptation strategies evaluated under CER, SER, and all three SWER variants. We empirically characterize how these metrics relate across experimental contexts. In architecture comparison, SER reveals ranking differences that CER’s character-level granularity obscures; BoTok-SWER correlates strongly with SER (<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$r = 0.914$ </tex-math></inline-formula>) and adds diagnostic value for downstream applications that rely on dictionary-based lexical processing; and Gem-SWER — the most human-aligned segmenter (<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$\kappa = 0.656$ </tex-math></inline-formula>) — provides the most independent word-level signal (<inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$r = 0.537$ </tex-math></inline-formula> with SER). Under parameter-efficient fine-tuning, the metric correlation structure changes substantially: CER–SER correlation drops to <inline-formula> <tex-math notation="LaTeX">$r = 0.421$ </tex-math></inline-formula>, and BoTok-SWER reveals severity of sequence-level collapse that CER does not capture. Our findings demonstrate that no single metric suffices for Tibetan ASR evaluation and that the appropriate metric combination depends on experimental context.
