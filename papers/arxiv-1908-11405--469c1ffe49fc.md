47. Gaussian genuine white noise is a particular case of a \protect \emph {Gaussian
    process}. Gaussian processes, often considered in machine learning, are
    defined as those processes with a multivariate Gaussian distribution over all
    finite collections of observable random variables $\{ X_t \}_{t \in \protect
  \mathcal {T}}$, where $\protect \mathcal {T}$ is any finite set of
    times~\cite [Ch.~6.4]{Bish06a}\cite {Gort19a}. There is only one Gaussian
    process consistent with each choice of second-order statistics. Therefore,
    Gaussian genuine white noise is the \protect \emph {only} Gaussian process
    which is a white noise. (Infinitely many other processes can also have
    low-order multivariate Gaussian distributions but, if they are not
    multivariate Gaussian distributed to all orders, they are not ``Gaussian
    processes'' in the technical sense.).
