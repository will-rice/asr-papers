## Abstract

A consistently challenging problem in automatic speech recognition (ASR) is a lack of consistent accent variability., particularly in individual and distributed, as well as privacy sensitive, settings. Traditional solutions tend to require a central training that threatens user privacy and makes the deployment scale poorly. In generality, existing ASR models are not able to adapt to accent diversity in real time and are either inherently biased to certain demographics or computationally inefficient. In order to solve these problems, we suggest AccentFed, a new FL framework that combines Transformer-based ASR with accent conditioned embeddings. Through this architecture, collaborative training is enabled across multilingual edge devices, without sharing raw audio data and with adaptive, privacy preserving recognition. Experimental results indicate that AccentFed yields 21.3% WER and 14.7% CER which is satisfying in light of the recognition accuracy that can be obtained under accent diverse conditions. Although AccentFed error rates are higher than those of centralized models, AccentFed is ethical, real time adaptable, robustly generalizing accented speech, making it desirable for globally inclusive ASR applications.
