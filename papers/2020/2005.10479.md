# Introduction

Over the past few years, thanks to the advances in deep learning, significant progress has been made in <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span>. Both <span acronym-label="dnn" acronym-form="singular+short">dnn</span>/<span acronym-label="hmm" acronym-form="singular+short">hmm</span> hybrid systems and <span acronym-label="e2e" acronym-form="singular+short">e2e</span> systems have attained surprisingly good performance in close-talk scenarios . However, it is still a challenging task to recognize speech signals in far-field scenarios, where background noise and reverberation are commonly observed and even interfering speech from other speakers is involved . In recent years, many studies have been focusing on the far-field speech recognition task, including the combination of the speech enhancement frontend and <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span> backend and noise robust adaptation approaches . Meanwhile, it is commonly observed that speech processing with multiple microphones usually outperforms the single-microphone one, because additional spatial information can be exploited. Therefore, many existing microphone array signal processing methods can be utilized as the frontend for end-to-end far-field speech recognition, such as the multi-channel Weiner filter , <span acronym-label="mvdr" acronym-form="singular+short">mvdr</span> and <span acronym-label="mpdr" acronym-form="singular+short">mpdr</span> beamforming , multi-frame beamforming , etc. In addition, reverberation is also an important problem in real scenarios, which can lead to dramatic degradation in the <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span> performance . Various deep learning based methods have been proposed for dereverberation, including <span acronym-label="dnn" acronym-form="singular+short">dnn</span> based approaches incorporating the <span acronym-label="wpe" acronym-form="singular+short">wpe</span> algorithm and complex ideal ratio mask based approach for denoising and dereverberation .

In this work, we propose a novel <span acronym-label="e2e" acronym-form="singular+short">e2e</span> architecture that can perform dereverberation, beamforming and recognition simultaneously. Inspired by the recently developed unified convolutional beamformer for simultaneous denoising and dereverberation, named <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> , we reformulate <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> by replacing eigenvalue decomposition with an equivalent matrix inverse operation, which makes it differentiable and more stable. The new architecture consists of a frontend and an <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span> backend. In the frontend, two novel architectures are explored for joint speech dereverberation, enhancement and separation. In the backend, a joint <span acronym-label="ctc" acronym-form="singular+short">ctc</span> / attention-based encoder-decoder model is used to recognize each separated speech stream. Note that our proposed framework can be used for both single-speaker and multi-speaker scenarios. And in this paper, we mainly focus on the multi-speaker case, which is a more difficult task. It is worth noting that this end-to-end architecture is optimized only based on the final <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span> criterion, which was also proven feasible in previous works . Our experiments show that our newly proposed method outperformed the conventional end-to-end ASR systems in both single-speaker and multi-speaker reverberant conditions.

# End-to-End Multi-Channel ASR

This section reviews the end-to-end multi-channel speech recognition system for both single-speaker $`(J = 1)`$ and multi-speaker $`(J > 1)`$ conditions, as shown in Fig. <a href="#fig:MIMO_Speech" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:MIMO_Speech">1</a>. Without loss of generality, we consider the input speech as a mixture of $`J \ (J \ge 1)`$ different speakers. For simplicity, we consider the noise as the $`0`$-th source ($`j=0`$) in the input signal.

The model is composed mainly of two modules, namely the frontend for speech separation and the backend for ASR. The frontend is a mask-based multi-source neural beamformer. First, the masking network estimates the masks $`\mathbf{M}_c^j`$ for every source $`j \in \{0, 1, \dots, J\}`$ on each channel $`c \in \{1, \dots, C\}`$ from the input spectrum $`\mathbf{X}_c = \left(x_{t, f, c}\right)_{t, f} \in \mathbb{C}^{T \times F}`$:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \resizebox{0.9\columnwidth}{!}{
    $\mathbf{M} = \left(m_{t, f, c}^{j}\right)_{t, f, c, j} = \text{MaskEstimator}(\mathbf{X}) \in \mathbb{C}^{T \times F \times C \times (J+1)} \,,$
    }
\end{align}
```
where $`m_{t, f, c}^{j} \in[0,1]`$, $`T`$ and $`F`$ represent time and frequency dimensions respectively. Second, the multi-source neural beamformer separates the mixture into $`J`$ streams using the <span acronym-label="mvdr" acronym-form="singular+short">mvdr</span> formalization . The estimated masks are used to compute the cross-channel <span acronym-label="psd" acronym-form="singular+short">psd</span> matrices $`\mathbf{\Phi}^{j}`$ and then the time-invariant filter $`\mathbf{g}^{j}_f`$ for each speaker $`j`$:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{\Phi}^{j}_f &= \frac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^T m^j_{t,f}} \sum_{t=1}^T m^{j}_{t,f} \mathbf{x}_{t,f} \mathbf{x}^{H}_{t,f} \; \in \mathbb{C}^{C \times C} \,, \\
    \mathbf{g}^j_f &= \frac{(\sum _{i \neq j} \mathbf{\Phi}^{i}_f)^{-1} \mathbf{\Phi}^{j}_f}{\text{Trace}((\sum _{i \neq j} \mathbf{\Phi}^{i} _f)^{-1} \mathbf{\Phi}^{j}_f)} \mathbf{u} \; \in \mathbb{C}^{C} \label{eq:mvdr-filter} \,,
\end{align}
```
where $`\mathbf{x}_{t,f} = \{x_{t,f,c}\}_{c=1}^C`$, $`m^{j}_{t,f} = \frac{1}{C} \sum_{c=1}^C m^{j}_{t,f,c}`$, $`(\cdot)^H`$ represents the conjugate transpose, and $`\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{C}`$ is a vector denoting the reference microphone estimated by an attention mechanism .

Finally, the separated speech $`\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathrm{enh},j}`$ of each speaker $`j`$ is derived by applying the filters $`\mathbf{g}^{j}`$ to the input speech $`\mathbf{X}`$, from which the log Mel-filterbank feature with global mean and variance normalization ($`\text{GMVN-LMF}(\cdot)`$) is further extracted:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \hat{x}^{\mathrm{enh}, j}_{t,f} &= (\mathbf{g}^{j}_f)^{H} \mathbf{x}_{t,f} \; \in \mathbb{C} \,, \\
    \mathbf{O}^{j} &= \text{GMVN-LMF}(|\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathrm{enh},j}|) \,,
\end{align}
```
where $`\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{\mathrm{enh},j} \!\in\! \mathbb{C}^{T \times F}`$, and $`\mathbf{O}^j`$ is the extracted feature for <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span>.

The backend is a joint <span acronym-label="ctc" acronym-form="singular+short">ctc</span>/attention-based encoder-decoder model for single-channel speech recognition. First, the encoder transforms the feature $`\mathbf{O}^{j}=\{\mathbf{o}^{j}_1,\dots,\mathbf{o}^{j}_T\}`$ of each speaker $`j`$ into a high-level representation $`\mathbf{H}^{j}=\{\mathbf{h}^{j}_1,\dots,\mathbf{h}^{j}_L\} \ (L \leq T)`$ with subsampling. Then, the representation is processed by the attention-based decoder to generate the output token sequences $`\mathbf{Y}^{j}=\{y^{j}_1,\dots,y^{j}_N\}`$. The <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span> process is formulated as follows:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{H}^{j} &= \text{Encoder}(\mathbf{O}^j) \,, \\[-1ex]
    \mathbf{y}^{j}_n &\sim \text{Attention-Decoder} (\mathbf{H}^{j}, \mathbf{y}^{j}_{n-1}) \,,
\end{align}
```
where $`\mathbf{y}_n^j`$ is the posterior probability vector for the $`n`$-th token. Note that in the multi-speaker case, i.e. $`J > 1`$, in order to solve the label ambiguity problem, the <span acronym-label="pit" acronym-form="singular+short">pit</span> technique is further applied in the <span acronym-label="ctc" acronym-form="singular+short">ctc</span> module to determine the order of the label sequences. The whole model is optimized with only the ASR loss $`\mathcal{L}`$ combining the attention and CTC losses with the determined label sequence order.

# End-to-End ASR with Unified Frontend

In this section, we introduce the proposed multi-channel speech recognition architecture for coping with the reverberant speech. First, we describe the mask-based WPE model for multi-channel dereverberation. Then, we show a cascade integration method which incorporates the mask-based WPE model followed by the model introduced in last Section, where the WPE filter coefficients are estimated for each speaker. Furthermore, another frontend architecture extending the <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> beamformer is designed, which unifies the dereverberation and beamforming modules with our new formulation.

## Mask-based WPE model

The mask-based <span acronym-label="wpe" acronym-form="singular+short">wpe</span> algorithm is introduced in this subsection. First, the input spectrum $`\mathbf{X} = (\mathbf{x}_{t,f})_{t,f}`$ is fed into a neural network to estimate a time-frequency mask $`\mathbf{m} = (m_{t,f,c})_{t,f,c}`$, as formulated below:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{m} &= \mathrm{MaskEstimator}(\mathbf{X}) \qquad\qquad \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times F \times C} \label{eq:mask} \,,\\
    \mathbf{x}_{t, f} &= \mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t, f} + \mathbf{n}_{t, f} \approx \sum_{j=1}^J \mathbf{v}^j_f s^j_{t, f} + \mathbf{n}_{t, f} \quad \in \mathbb{C}^{C}\,, \label{eq:signal}
\end{align}
```
where $`\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t, f}`$ is the direct path and early reflection of the source signal, $`s^j_{t, f}`$ is the $`j`$-th source signal, $`\mathbf{v}_f = [v_{f}^{(0)}, v_{f}^{(1)}, \cdots,`$ $`v_{f}^{(C-1)}]^T \in \mathbb{C}^{C}`$ is the steering vector, and $`\mathbf{n}_{t, f} \in \mathbb{C}^{C}`$ is the noise and late reverberation of source signals.

With the estimated mask, the time-variant power $`\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,f}`$ of the input signal can be estimated by Eq. (<a href="#eq:psd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:psd">[eq:psd]</a>), and then the signal can be dereverberated via a standard WPE procedure:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{\lambda}_{t,f} &= \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^C \frac{m_{t,f,c}}{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\tau=1}^T m_{\tau,f,c}} \left|x_{t,f,c}\right|^2 \quad \in \mathbb{R} \label{eq:psd} \,, \\
    \hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{wpe}}_{t,f} &= \mathrm{WPE}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t,f}, \mathbf{\lambda}_{t,f}\right) \label{eq:wpe} \,,
\end{align}
```

## Unified dereverberation and beamforming

The original <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> beamformer aims to eliminate the late reverberation and noise from the noisy signal, while keeping the direct signal undistorted. It combines the ideas of <span acronym-label="wpe" acronym-form="singular+short">wpe</span> and <span acronym-label="mpdr" acronym-form="singular+short">mpdr</span> beamformer , and optimizes their filters at the same time, with the constrained optimization objective below:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \bar{\mathbf{w}}=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}_{\bar{\mathbf{w}}} \sum_{t} \frac{\left|\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{f}^{H} \bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t, f}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{t, f}} \quad \text { s.t. } \quad \mathbf{w}_{0, f}^{H} \mathbf{v}_{f}=v_{f}^{(\text{ref})}
\end{align}
```
where $`\bar{\mathbf{w}} = [\mathbf{w}_{0,f}^T, \mathbf{w}_{D,f}^T, \mathbf{w}_{D+1,f}^T, \cdots, \mathbf{w}_{K+D-1,f}^T]^T \in \mathbb{C}^{C(K+1)}`$ is the <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> filter coefficient, $`\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t,f} = [\mathbf{x}_{t,f}^T, \mathbf{x}_{t-D,f}^T,`$ $`\mathbf{x}_{t-D-1,f}^T, \cdots, \mathbf{x}_{t-K-D+1,f}^T]^T \in \mathbb{C}^{C(K+1)}`$ is the concatenation of input signals of current and previous frames, $`D`$ is the delay parameter, $`K`$ is the number of filter taps, $`v_{f}^{(\text{ref})}`$ is the value of the steering vector at the reference channel, and $`\lambda_{t,f}`$ is the power of the desired signal as in Eq. (<a href="#eq:psd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:psd">[eq:psd]</a>).

By solving the above constrained optimization problem, we can calculate the <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> filter $`\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{f}`$ and the enhanced signal $`\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{\text{enh}}`$ by the following formulas:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \bar{\mathbf{w}}_{f} &= \frac{\mathbf{R}_{f}^{-1} \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{f}}{\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{f}^{H} \mathbf{R}_{f}^{-1} \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{f}} \left(v_f^{(\text{ref})}\right)^* & \in \mathbb{C}^{C(K+1)} \label{eq:wpd} \,,\\
    \mathbf{R}_f &= \sum_{t=D}^{T} \frac{\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t-D,f} \bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t-D,f}^{H}}{\lambda_{t,f}} & \in \mathbb{C}^{C(K+1) \times C(K+1)} \label{eq:corr_mat} \,, \\
    \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t, f}^{\text{enh}} &= \bar{\mathbf{w}}_{f}^H \bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t, f} & \in \mathbb{C} \,,\qquad\quad \label{eq:wpd_enh}
\end{align}
```
where $`\mathbf{R}_f`$ is the power normalized covariance matrix, $`\bar{\mathbf{v}}_f = [\mathbf{v}^T_f, \mathbf{0}, \cdots, \mathbf{0}]^T \in \mathbb{C}^{C(K+1)}`$, and $`(\cdot)^*`$ denotes complex conjugate. While the original <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> can perform denoising and dereverberation simultaneously with an elegant formulation, it is only designed for speech enhancement of the single-speaker input. In addition, the steering vector $`\bar{\mathbf{v}}`$ in Eq. (<a href="#eq:wpd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:wpd">[eq:wpd]</a>) is needed for calculating the beamformer weights, which requires the direction information of the sound source or needs to be approximated by eigenvalue decomposition of a complex matrix .

Based on the above formulation, we first derive another equivalent formula that no longer requires the steering vector $`\bar{\mathbf{v}}_f`$, and then extend the original <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> to the multi-speaker case. Consider the padded speech signal $`\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime T}_{t, f} = [\mathbf{x}^{\prime T}_{t, f}, \mathbf{0}, \cdots, \mathbf{0}]^T \in \mathbb{C}^{C(K+1)}`$, it is easy to derive from Eq. (<a href="#eq:signal" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:signal">[eq:signal]</a>) that:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}_{t, f} &= \bar{\mathbf{v}}_f s_{t, f} \,, \label{eq:stacked_obv} \\
    \!\!\!(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}})_{f} &= \sum_{t=1}^T \dfrac{m_{t,f} \tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}_{t, f} \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t, f}^{\prime H}}{\sum_{\tau=1}^T m_{\tau,f}} = \bar{\mathbf{v}}_f \phi_{f} \bar{\mathbf{v}}_f^H \!=\! \bar{\mathbf{v}}_f \bar{\mathbf{v}}_f^H \phi_{f} \,, \label{eq:cov_stacked} \\
    v_f^{\text{(ref)}} &= \bar{\mathbf{v}}^T_f \bar{\mathbf{u}}  \,, \label{eq:ref_ch}
\end{align}
```
where $`(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}})_f \in \mathbb{C}^{C(K+1) \times C(K+1)}`$ is the cross-channel <span acronym-label="psd" acronym-form="singular+short">psd</span> matrix of the padded speech signal $`\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}_{t, f}`$, $`\bar{\mathbf{u}} = [\mathbf{u}^T, \mathbf{0},`$ $`\cdots, \mathbf{0}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{C(K+1)}`$ and $`\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{C}`$ is the reference vector denoting the reference microphone estimated by an attention mechanism. Substitute Eq. (<a href="#eq:stacked_obv" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:stacked_obv">[eq:stacked_obv]</a>) – (<a href="#eq:ref_ch" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:ref_ch">[eq:ref_ch]</a>) into Eq. (<a href="#eq:wpd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:wpd">[eq:wpd]</a>), we can derive that:
``` math
\begin{align}
    \bar{\mathbf{w}}_f = \dfrac{\mathbf{R}^{-1}_f (\mathbf{\Phi}_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}})_{f}}{\operatorname{Trace}\left[\mathbf{R}^{-1}_{f} (\mathbf{\Phi}_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}})_{f}\right]} \bar{\mathbf{u}}  \label{eq:new_wpd} \,.
\end{align}
```
This new formula is equivalent to Eq. (<a href="#eq:wpd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:wpd">[eq:wpd]</a>), but no longer requires the steering vector for calculating the filter weights.

Furthermore, we can easily extend <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> to the multi-speaker case. For each speaker $`j`$, the corresponding covariance matrix $`\mathbf{R}_f^{j}`$ can be derived from Eq. (<a href="#eq:corr_mat" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:corr_mat">[eq:corr_mat]</a>) and (<a href="#eq:psd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:psd">[eq:psd]</a>), where the estimated mask $`\mathbf{m}^j`$ for speaker $`j`$ is used for both dereverberation and beamforming. Then the <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> beamformer for speaker $`j`$ can be calculated from Eq. (<a href="#eq:new_wpd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:new_wpd">[eq:new_wpd]</a>). Finally, the separated speech of each speaker can be derived from Eq. (<a href="#eq:wpd_enh" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:wpd_enh">[eq:wpd_enh]</a>), using the corresponding <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> beamformer. Note that the masks for calculating $`\mathbf{R}_f^{j}`$ and $`\mathbf{\Phi}_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}}^{j}`$ for each speaker $`j`$ can be either shared using a single mask estimator or estimated by two separate mask estimators. The <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> based architecture is illustrated in Fig. <a href="#fig:MIMO_WPD" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:MIMO_WPD">3</a>.

# Experiments

To make our experimental results comparable to previous results of MIMO-Speech , we evaluated the proposed methods on the same spatialized wsj1-2mix dataset as in , which consists of two sub-datasets: anechoic and reverberant. The reverberation time (RT$`_{60}`$) of the reverberant data ranges from 200 ms to 600 ms. In each sub-dataset, the duration of the spatialized speech for training, development and evaluation is 98.5 hr, 1.3 hr and 0.8 hr respectively. We also adopt the multi-condition training in , i.e. include the WSJ train_si284 in training to improve the performance. We also test our methods for single-speaker speech recognition on the REVERB dataset , which uses 2-channel simulated reverberant data for training and 8-channel real data for evaluation.

For feature extraction, the <span acronym-label="stft" acronym-form="singular+short">stft</span> is performed with a 16-kHz sampling rate and a 25-ms Hann window with a 10-ms stride, and the spectral feature’s dimension is $`F=257`$. After the frontend processing, 80-dimensional log Mel-filterbank features are extracted from the enhanced spectrum of each separated speech, where the global mean and variance normalization is applied using the statistics from the single-speaker WSJ1 training set. The number of channels for training in our experiments is $`C=2`$. But it can be extended to an arbitrary number of channels as described in .

## Experimental Setup

All our proposed end-to-end multi-channel speech recognition models are implemented based on the ESPnet framework .[^1] The AdaDelta optimizer with $`\rho=0.95`$ and $`\epsilon=10^{-8}`$ is used for training. The data in both anechoic and reverberant conditions are used for training.

In the mask-based <span acronym-label="wpe" acronym-form="singular+short">wpe</span> module, the mask estimation network is a 3-layer bidirectional long-short term memory with projection (BLSTMP) network with 300 cells in each direction. The number of iterations for performing mask-based WPE is set to 1. The prediction delay $`D`$ and the number of taps $`K`$ is set to 3 and 5 respectively. The mask estimators in both the <span acronym-label="mvdr" acronym-form="singular+short">mvdr</span> beamformer and the <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> beamformer are 3-layer BLSTMP networks with 512 cells. Note that in all our experiments except Section <a href="#ssec:exp3" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ssec:exp3">4.3</a>, we used a shared mask estimator instead of two separate ones in Fig. <a href="#fig:MIMO_WPD" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:MIMO_WPD">3</a>. In the <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span> module, following the configurations in , we use the CNN-Transformer based encoder, which consists of 2 CNN blocks and 12 Transformer layers, and the 6-layer Transformer-based decoder. The self-attention in all Transformer layers has the same configuration as in , i.e. 4 heads and 256 dimensions. As for decoding, a word-level language model trained on the official text data included in the WSJ corpus is used. The interpolation factor between CTC and attention losses is set to 0.2.

For experiments on REVERB, the network configuration and experimental conditions are the same as . The reference microphone is fixed as the second channel. Both dereverberation and denoising subnetworks are trained to predict two dimensional time-frequency masks.

# Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed an end-to-end multi-channel far-field speech recognition framework with unified dereverberation and beamforming, which is capable of performing speech dereverberation, separation and recognition simultaneously. The whole model is optimized via only the <span acronym-label="asr" acronym-form="singular+short">asr</span> criterion but can still learn relatively good dereverberation and separation skills. Two novel frontend architectures are explored, and promising performance is achieved on the spatialized wsj1-2mix corpus compared to the previous MIMO-Speech model. Experimental results on REVERB dataset also demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed <span acronym-label="wpd" acronym-form="singular+short">wpd</span> based architecture.
