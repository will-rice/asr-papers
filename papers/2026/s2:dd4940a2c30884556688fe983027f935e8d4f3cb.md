## Abstract

Amharic, the official language of Ethiopia, presents distinct challenges for automatic speech recognition due to its complex phonological system. Features such as ejective consonants, systematic gemination, and context-dependent vowel realizations create acoustic variability that conventional HMM and standard deep learning approaches struggle to capture effectively. We address these challenges by combining a phoneme-aware Transformer-based speech recognition system with systematic context-sensitivity analysis. Our approach fine-tunes a pretrained Wav2Vec2-Large-XLSR-53 model using phoneme-level representations and applies alignment-based diagnostics to quantify error patterns across phonetic environments, while employing Connectionist Temporal Classification loss and a specialized phoneme tokenizer that explicitly encodes 88 distinct Amharic phonetic units, including ejectives, geminates, and vowel length contrasts. Working with 20.03 hours of speech data, the system achieves a word error rate of 4.25% and a character error rate of 3.32%. Our proposed system operates in phonemic transliteration space rather than Ethiopic orthography; reported metrics therefore measure phoneme-recognition accuracy rather than orthographic transcription accuracy. For illustrative comparison, this corresponds to an approximately 85.44% relative reduction in word error rate compared to a recent Transformer-based Amharic speech recognition system. Beyond these aggregate metrics, our alignment-based context analysis identifies specific phonetic environments that drive recognition errors. This diagnostic framework enables targeted improvements for morphologically complex and low-resource languages without requiring manually engineered phonological rules.
