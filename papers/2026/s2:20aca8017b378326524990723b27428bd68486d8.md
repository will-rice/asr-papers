## Abstract

Recently, a few-shot learning (FSL) framework termed MNCTC (Matching Networks - Connectionist Temporal Classification) was proposed for cross-lingual E2E ASR. MN-CTC is a FSL approach which simultaneously falls within meta-learning, metric-learning and embedding-learning frameworks, realized via episodic-training. The framework enables cross-domain inference on new target domains with classes not seen during pre-training, potentially requiring no intermediate fine-tuning of the embedding functions. We now extend and generalize this framework along primary aspects such as 1) small-scale pre-training for a range of low source data sizes (10-178 hours from a single source language), as well as up to 1000 hours pre-training to demonstrate the scalability of the proposed framework, 2) ultra lowresource target language inference conditions under FSL setting, calling for very small fine-tuning and inference data (15 min to 2.5 hours of target languages), 3) handling diverse language sets from the Indo-Aryan and Dravidian families, 4) establish Character Error Rate performance benchmarks comparing the proposed MN-CTC with four baselines: a) supervised transfer-learning, b) Foundation Models (FMs) pretrained from proportional unsupervised data by self-supervised learning from scratch and c) off-the-shelf FMs pre-trained from very large unsupervised data ($\sim$17000 hours from 40 languages) by self-supervised learning and d) weakly-supervised Whisper pretrained from very large supervised data ($\sim$680,000 hours). The results show that the proposed FSL framework offers superior performance over the baselines at smallscale pre-training under few-shot inference conditions. Importantly, it exhibits competitive performances relative to FMs, thereby potentially heralding new directions in FSL-informed pre-training and inference for ultra low-resource E2E ASR.
