# BanglaRobustNet: A Hybrid Denoising-Attention Architecture for Robust Bangla Speech Recognition\

Md Sazzadul Islam Ridoy, Mubaswira Ibnat Zidney, Sumi Akter and Md. Aminur Rahman\

###### Abstract

Bangla, one of the most widely spoken languages, remains underrepresented in state-of-the-art automatic speech recognition (ASR) research, particularly under noisy and speaker-diverse conditions. This paper presents BanglaRobustNet, a hybrid denoising-attention framework built on Wav2Vec-BERT, designed to address these challenges. The architecture integrates a diffusion-based denoising module to suppress environmental noise while preserving Bangla-specific phonetic cues, and a contextual cross-attention module that conditions recognition on speaker embeddings for robustness across gender, age, and dialects. Trained end-to-end with a composite objective combining CTC loss, phonetic consistency, and speaker alignment, BanglaRobustNet achieves substantial reductions in word error rate (WER) and character error rate (CER) compared to Wav2Vec-BERT and Whisper baselines. Evaluations on Mozilla Common Voice Bangla and augmented noisy speech confirm the effectiveness of our approach, establishing BanglaRobustNet as a robust ASR system tailored to low-resource, noise-prone linguistic settings.

## I Introduction

Bangla, spoken by over 250 million people, is a low-resource language for Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), with only around 500 hours of labeled data compared to over 10,000 hours for languages like English. This data imbalance, combined with challenges such as environmental noise, dialectal variations, and complex phonology, hampers the development of robust ASR systems for Bangla, especially in real-world conditions \[[24](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib1 "Challenges in developing bangla asr for real-world deployment")\], \[[22](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib2 "Environmental noise and dialectal variation in bangla asr: an empirical study")\].

Existing models like Wav2Vec 2.0 \[[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib3 "wav2vec 2.0: A Framework for Self-Supervised Learning of Speech Representations")\] and Whisper \[[21](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib4 "Robust speech recognition via large-scale weak supervision")\] perform well in multilingual settings but struggle with high Word Error Rates (WER) in noisy Bangla environments and dialectal variations. For example, Whisper has a WER exceeding 30% in noisy Bangla speech \[[8](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib5 "Evaluating whisper on south asian languages: the case of noisy bangla")\], reflecting its difficulty in handling the phonetic complexities and environmental factors of the language. These models also fail to effectively capture Bangla-specific phonetic features and speaker-related variability, highlighting the need for tailored ASR solutions \[[3](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib6 "Phonetic complexity in bangla asr: challenges and opportunities")\].

To address these challenges, this paper proposes BanglaRobustNet, an ASR architecture that builds on Wav2Vec-BERT, incorporating two key innovations: a Diffusion-Based Denoising Module and a Contextual Cross-Attention Module. The denoising module uses a diffusion model to iteratively remove noise while preserving phonetic integrity, while the cross-attention module adapts the model to speaker variability by conditioning acoustic features on speaker embeddings (e.g., gender, age, and dialect).

The contributions are threefold:

1.  1.  Phonetic-aware Denoising: A novel diffusion-based denoising approach that removes environmental noise while preserving critical phonetic features, improving transcription accuracy \[[2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib7 "Diffusion models for robust speech enhancement in noisy low-resource settings")\],\[[14](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib8 "Phoneme-preserving diffusion for noisy speech recognition")\].
2.  2.  Speaker-Conditioned Attention: A cross-attention mechanism that adapts ASR to diverse speakers, improving performance across dialects, genders, and ages \[[18](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib9 "Speaker adaptation in low-resource african languages: lessons for south asia")\],\[[20](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib10 "Cross-lingual transfer with speaker embeddings for indic languages")\].
3.  3.  Empirical Results: BanglaRobustNet achieves significant reductions in WER—12% in clean conditions, 18% in noisy conditions, and 15% across dialects—setting a new benchmark for Bangla ASR.

This work advances ASR for Bangla and offers broader implications for low-resource languages. The model and code are open-source, promoting further research and development in the field.

## II Related Work

### II-A ASR Models

State-of-the-art ASR systems such as Wav2Vec 2.0 \[[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib3 "wav2vec 2.0: A Framework for Self-Supervised Learning of Speech Representations")\], HuBERT \[[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib11 "HuBERT: Self-Supervised Speech Representation Learning by Masked Prediction of Hidden Units")\], and Whisper \[[21](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib4 "Robust speech recognition via large-scale weak supervision")\] perform well on high-resource languages but remain limited for Bangla due to scarce training data, phonetic complexity, and dialectal variation. Whisper, for example, reports WER above 30% on noisy Bangla speech \[[23](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib16 "Adaptability of asr models on low-resource language: a comparative study of whisper and wav2vec-bert on bangla")\], underscoring the need for more noise-robust, phoneme-aware approaches.

### II-B Low-Resource and Indic ASR

Indic-specific models like IndicWav2Vec \[[12](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib12 "IndicWav2Vec: Self-Supervised Speech Recognition for Indic Languages")\] have advanced low-resource ASR, but Bangla remains challenging. Resources such as Common Voice Bangla \[[17](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib13 "Common Voice: A Massively-Multilingual Speech Corpus")\] provide valuable data, yet lack sufficient diversity in noise conditions and speaker traits for robust real-world deployment.

### II-C Denoising and Attention

Robustness has been improved through techniques like SpecAugment \[[19](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib14 "SpecAugment: A Simple Data Augmentation Method for Automatic Speech Recognition")\], though feature masking often distorts phoneme integrity. Diffusion-based denoising \[[11](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib15 "DiffWave: A Versatile Diffusion Model for Audio Synthesis")\] better preserves phonetic detail, while transformer attention mechanisms enhance recognition but rarely address speaker variability directly \[[6](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib11 "HuBERT: Self-Supervised Speech Representation Learning by Masked Prediction of Hidden Units")\].

### II-D Contributions of BanglaRobustNet

The proposed model BanglaRobustNet, introduces (i) a phonetic-aware denoising module that preserves Bangla phonemes while suppressing noise, and (ii) a speaker-conditioned attention mechanism that adapts to dialectal and demographic variation. These innovations jointly tackle the key limitations of existing ASR systems for Bangla.

## III Methodology: BanglaRobustNet Architecture

![Refer to caption](https://arxiv.org/html/paper-banglanet_2.png)

Figure 1: The proposed BanglaRobustNet architecture integrating denoising and attention mechanisms for robust Bangla speech recognition.

### III-A Overview

This paper proposes BanglaRobustNet, a hybrid architecture designed (Fig. [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#S3.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ III Methodology: BanglaRobustNet Architecture ‣ BanglaRobustNet: A Hybrid Denoising-Attention Architecture for Robust Bangla Speech Recognition")) for robust Bangla ASR. The model tackles two central challenges: (i) maintaining phonetic fidelity under noisy acoustic conditions, and (ii) adapting transcription to speaker variability, including dialects, gender, and age \[[4](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib20 "Challenges in bangla asr: noise, dialects, and data scarcity")\]. To this end, BanglaRobustNet builds on a self-supervised Wav2Vec-BERT backbone and integrates two key modules: a Diffusion-Based Denoising Module (DBDM) and a Contextual Cross-Attention Module (CCAM).

### III-B Architectural Design Principles

#### III-B1 Design Philosophy

The architecture follows three guiding principles:

- •
  Phonetic Preservation: Noise reduction must retain Bangla-specific phonemes such as aspirated consonants (/p^(h)/, /t^(h)/, /k^(h)/), fricatives (/x/, /a/), and nasal vowels \[[9](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib21 "Phonetic analysis of noisy bangla speech for robust asr"), [5](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib22 "Preserving nasal vowels in bangla asr under noise")\].
- •
  Speaker Adaptivity: The model adapts dynamically to diverse speaker traits without requiring speaker-specific retraining.
- •
  Computational Efficiency: The system is optimized for real-time inference while maintaining high recognition accuracy.

#### III-B2 Architectural Innovations

BanglaRobustNet incorporates three main innovations:

1.  1.  Diffusion-based denoising with explicit phonetic consistency constraints \[[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib23 "DiffWave: A Versatile Diffusion Model for Audio Synthesis")\].
2.  2.  Cross-modal attention conditioned on speaker embeddings for adaptive recognition.
3.  3.  Joint optimization of acoustic, phonetic, and speaker-consistency objectives \[[16](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib24 "Speech representation learning through self-supervision and multi-task training")\].

#### III-C3 Diffusion-Based Denoising Module (DBDM)

Theoretical Foundation: The DBDM adapts denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) for acoustic feature denoising, preserving phonetic integrity\[[13](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib25 "Diffusion-based speech enhancement with perceptual consistency")\]. The forward process is:

$`\displaystyle q(\mathbf{z}_{t}|\mathbf{z}_{t-1})`$$`\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}_{t};\sqrt{1-\beta_{t}}\mathbf{z}_{t-1},\beta_{t}\mathbf{I}),`$

where $`\mathbf{z}_{0}`$ is the clean feature, and $`\{\beta_{t}\}`$ is the noise schedule.

The reverse process denoises features:

$`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}|\mathbf{z}_{t})`$$`\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{z}_{t-1};\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{t},t),\sigma_{t}^{2}\mathbf{I}).`$

U-Net Architecture: A U-Net with encoder-decoder layers (Table [III](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#S3.T3 "TABLE III ‣ III-C3 Diffusion-Based Denoising Module (DBDM) ‣ III-C Detailed Architecture Description ‣ III Methodology: BanglaRobustNet Architecture ‣ BanglaRobustNet: A Hybrid Denoising-Attention Architecture for Robust Bangla Speech Recognition")) performs denoising.

| Component        | Configuration                                |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Encoder Layers   | 5 layers, \[512, 256, 128, 64, 32\] channels |
| Decoder Layers   | 5 layers, \[32, 64, 128, 256, 512\] channels |
| Skip Connections | Concatenation between encoder-decoder pairs  |
| Time Embedding   | Sinusoidal encoding, 128 dimensions          |
| Activation       | SiLU (Swish)                                 |

Phonetic Consistency Constraint: A phonetic consistency loss preserves Bangla phonemes:

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{phonetic}}=\lambda_{1}\cdot\text{MSE}(\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{\text{clean}}),\Phi(\mathbf{z}_{\text{denoised}})),
```

where $`\Phi`$ is a pre-trained Bangla phoneme classifier.

Training Objective:

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DBDM}}=\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{z}_{0},\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}\left[\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\theta}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\mathbf{z}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon},t)\|^{2}\right]+\mathcal{L}_{\text{phonetic}}.
```

#### III-C4 Contextual Cross-Attention Module (CCAM)

Speaker Embedding Extraction: A multi-task neural network (Table [IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#S3.T4 "TABLE IV ‣ III-C4 Contextual Cross-Attention Module (CCAM) ‣ III-C Detailed Architecture Description ‣ III Methodology: BanglaRobustNet Architecture ‣ BanglaRobustNet: A Hybrid Denoising-Attention Architecture for Robust Bangla Speech Recognition")) extracts speaker embeddings from $`\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{T^{\prime\prime}\times 512}`$, classifying gender, age (18-25, 26-40, 41-60, 60+), and six Bangla dialects.

| Component              | Configuration                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Input                  | Acoustic features $`\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{T^{\prime\prime}\times 512}`$    |
| Global Average Pooling | $`\mathbf{z}_{\text{global}}=\text{mean}(\mathbf{Z},\text{dim}=\text{time})`$ |
| MLP                    | \[512 $`\rightarrow`$ 256 $`\rightarrow`$ 128 $`\rightarrow`$ 64\]            |
| Gender Output          | softmax(Linear\_(64→2))                                                       |
| Age Group Output       | softmax(Linear\_(64→4))                                                       |
| Dialect Output         | softmax(Linear\_(64→6))                                                       |
| Speaker Embedding      | $`\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{128}`$ (concatenated task embeddings)              |

Cross-Attention Mechanism: The CCAM conditions transformer outputs $`\mathbf{H}_{\text{transformer}}`$ on speaker embeddings $`\mathbf{s}`$:

$`\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}`$$`\displaystyle=\text{Linear}_{1024\rightarrow 1024}(\mathbf{H}_{\text{transformer}})`$$`\displaystyle\mathbf{K}`$$`\displaystyle=\text{Linear}_{1024\rightarrow 1024}(\mathbf{H}_{\text{transformer}})`$$`\displaystyle\mathbf{V}`$$`\displaystyle=\text{Linear}_{1024\rightarrow 1024}(\mathbf{H}_{\text{transformer}})`$$`\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{s}`$$`\displaystyle=\text{Linear}_{128\rightarrow 1024}(\mathbf{s})`$$`\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\text{cross}}`$$`\displaystyle=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{(\mathbf{Q}_{s}\odot\mathbf{Q})\mathbf{K}^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)`$$`\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{\text{conditioned}}`$$`\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{\text{cross}}\cdot\mathbf{V}+\mathbf{H}_{\text{transformer}},`$

where $`\odot`$ denotes element-wise multiplication with broadcasting.

Speaker Consistency Loss:

$`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{speaker}}`$$`\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}\cdot\text{CE}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{gender}},\mathbf{y}_{\text{gender}})`$$`\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{3}\cdot\text{CE}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{age}},\mathbf{y}_{\text{age}})`$$`\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{4}\cdot\text{CE}(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{dialect}},\mathbf{y}_{\text{dialect}})`$

#### III-C5 CTC Decoder with Multi-Objective Optimization

CTC Head:

$`\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{\text{conditioned}}\in\mathbb{R}^{T^{\prime\prime}\times 1024}`$$`\displaystyle\mathbf{P}=\text{softmax}(\mathbf{H}_{\text{conditioned}}\cdot\mathbf{W}),\quad\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{1024\times 52},`$

where 52 is the Bangla grapheme vocabulary size (11 vowels, 39 consonants, special tokens).

Joint Loss Function:

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{CTC}}+\alpha_{1}\mathcal{L}_{\text{phonetic}}+\alpha_{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{speaker}}+\alpha_{3}\mathcal{L}_{\text{consistency}},
```

where $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{CTC}}`$ uses the CTC forward-backward algorithm, and $`\mathcal{L}_{\text{consistency}}=\text{KL}(p(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{z}_{\text{clean}}),p(\mathbf{s}|\mathbf{z}_{\text{denoised}}))`$.

### III-D Training Methodology

#### III-D1 Multi-Stage Training

Model training was conducted in three stages (Table [V](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#S3.T5 "TABLE V ‣ III-C5 CTC Decoder with Multi-Objective Optimization ‣ III-C Detailed Architecture Description ‣ III Methodology: BanglaRobustNet Architecture ‣ BanglaRobustNet: A Hybrid Denoising-Attention Architecture for Robust Bangla Speech Recognition")). Stage 2 introduced diverse noise conditions—Gaussian, traffic, crowd, and music—with signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) ranging from $`-5\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}`$ to $`20\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}`$, enabling the model to generalize across noisy environments.

#### III-D2 Data Augmentation

To enhance robustness, both acoustic and phonetic augmentation strategies were applied. Acoustic augmentations included speed perturbation (factors: \[0.9, 1.1\]), volume scaling ($`\pm 3\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}`$), additive noise (SNR: $`5\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}20\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}`$), and room impulse response simulation. Phonetic augmentations targeted Bangla-specific variations, such as consonant cluster modifications, vowel length adjustments, and changes in aspiration intensity.

#### III-D3 Optimization Configuration

Training used the AdamW optimizer \[[15](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib18 "Decoupled weight decay regularization")\] with $`\beta_{1}=0.9`$ and $`\beta_{2}=0.999`$. A batch size of 16 was employed, effectively scaled to 64 through gradient accumulation across 4 steps. Input sequences were normalized to a maximum duration of 30 seconds via truncation or zero-padding, ensuring consistent and efficient processing.

## IV Experiments

### IV-A Experimental Setup

#### IV-A1 Datasets

The evaluation datasets are designed to assess Bangla automatic speech recognition (ASR) performance across diverse conditions, including clean and noisy settings.

Clean Test Set:

- •
  Common Voice Bangla v20: Test split from Mozilla Common Voice 20.0. This corpus provides transcribed speech from diverse speakers across Bangladesh.
- •
  OpenSLR Bengali (SLR53): Evaluation subset of the OpenSLR Bengali corpus \[[25](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib19 "A Step-by-Step Process for Building TTS Voices Using Open Source Data and Framework for Bangla, Javanese, Khmer, Nepali, Sinhala, and Sundanese")\], which contains read speech under controlled recording conditions.
- •
  Bhasha-bichitra: ASR for Regional Dialects: A curated dataset focusing on regional Bangla dialects (e.g., Sylheti, Chittagong, Barishal). Exact hours and utterance counts are not publicly reported.\[[7](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17679v1#bib.bib17 "Bhasha-bichitra: asr for regional dialects")\]
- •
  Demographics: Approximately 65% male, 35% female; age range: 18–68 years (based on available metadata across datasets).
- •
  Dialect Coverage: Standard Bangla (majority), with supplementary representation of Sylheti, Chittagong, and other regional dialects.

Noisy Test Sets:

- •
  SNR Levels: 0, 2, 5, 8, 10 dB (5 test sets, 4 hours each).
- •
  Noise Types: Traffic (urban), crowd (market), music (café), office (workplace).
- •
  Base Audio: Subset of clean test set mixed with real-world noise recordings.
- •
  Total: 20 hours across varied noise conditions.

#### IV-A2 Evaluation Metrics

Primary Metrics:

- •
  Word Error Rate (WER):

  ```math
  \text{WER}=\frac{S+D+I}{N}\times 100\%
  ```

  where $`S`$ = substitutions, $`D`$ = deletions, $`I`$ = insertions, $`N`$ = total words.

- •
  Character Error Rate (CER): Fine-grained evaluation at the grapheme level.
- •
  Phoneme Error Rate (PER): Linguistic accuracy using forced alignment with Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA).

Secondary Metrics:

- •
  BLEU Score: Sentence-level BLEU-4 for semantic similarity.
- •
  Real-time Factor (RTF):

  ```math
  \text{RTF}=\frac{\text{Processing time}}{\text{Audio duration}}
  ```

- •
  Speaker Adaptation Accuracy: Classification accuracy for gender, age, and dialect prediction.

#### IV-A3 Baseline Models

The following state-of-the-art models serve as baselines:

- •
  Whisper-Small (242M parameters): OpenAI’s multilingual ASR model.
- •
  Whisper-Large (1.55B parameters): Larger variant for comparison.
- •
  Wav2Vec-BERT (580M parameters): Vanilla backbone without modifications.

Implementation Details: All models were fine-tuned on the Common Voice Bangla training set with consistent hyperparameters: learning rate $`1\times 10^{-5}`$, batch size 16. Evaluation used identical test sets and preprocessing pipelines. Statistical significance was assessed with paired t-tests ($`p<0.05`$).

#### IV-A4 Training Infrastructure

Hardware Configuration:

- •
  Training: 4$`\times`$ NVIDIA RTX4090 (24GB) GPUs, 32GB RAM, Intel Core i9 14900K CPU.
- •
  Inference: Single RTX 3060 (12GB) for real-time evaluation.
- •
  Storage: 1TB NVMe SSD for dataset access.

Training Schedule:

- •
  Total Training Time: 72 hours (3 stages: Pre-training: 48h, DBDM: 16h, End-to-end: 8h).
- •
  Checkpointing: Every 1000 steps with early stopping (patience=5).

Evaluation Protocol:

- •
  Cross-validation: 5-fold validation on development set.
- •
  Test Set Evaluation: Single evaluation on held-out test sets.
- •
  Multiple Runs: 3 independent runs with different random seeds, reporting 95% confidence intervals.

### IV-D Analysis

#### IV-D1 Robustness in Low-SNR Environments

BanglaRobustNet maintains 42.4% WER at 0 dB SNR (vs. 100.0% for baselines, 28–32% improvement) due to:

- •
  Phonetic consistency constraints in DBDM preventing over-denoising.
- •
  Conditional diffusion adapting to Bangla phoneme distributions.
- •
  Multi-stage training for noise robustness.

Paired t-tests confirm significance ($`p<0.001`$) with consistent 95% confidence intervals.

#### IV-D2 Computational Efficiency

BanglaRobustNet achieves real-time transcription (RTF 0.16) on RTX 3080 via optimized diffusion (10 steps) and linear-complexity DBDM. A 2.1GB footprint with INT8 quantization enables mobile deployment with $`<5\%`$ performance loss.

## V Conclusion

BanglaRobustNet represents a meaningful step forward in making speech recognition more accessible and reliable for Bangla speakers a community long underserved by current ASR technologies. Designed to confront the intertwined challenges of limited data, rich phonetics, dialectal diversity, and real-world noise, the model introduces two purpose built innovations: a phonetic-aware diffusion denoiser that cleans speech without erasing linguistic nuance, and a speaker-conditioned cross-attention mechanism that adapts to who is speaking not just what is said.

The results speak for themselves: significant WER reductions 12% in clean speech, 18% under noise, and 15% across dialects outperforming existing baselines and setting a new standard for Bangla ASR. But beyond metrics, this work is about empowerment. By open-sourcing both model and code, barriers are lowered for researchers, developers, and local communities inviting collaboration, adaptation, and innovation not only for Bangla but for other low-resource languages facing similar hurdles.

The hope is that BanglaRobustNet doesn’t just improve transcription accuracy it helps amplify voices that have too often been left out of the AI conversation.
