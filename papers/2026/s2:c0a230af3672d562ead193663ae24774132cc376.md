## Abstract

In an increasingly interconnected world, speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) has become essential for overcoming language barriers. While traditional S2ST systems rely on cascaded pipelines of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Neural Machine Translation (NMT), and Text-to-Speech (TTS) modules, recent advancements in neural networks have begun to offer both potential and efficiency for direct S2ST frameworks. These approaches achieve the mapping from source speech to target speech at the semantic level but face challenges due to limited parallel S2ST training data. To address these limitations, this work explores the integration of large language models (LLMs) into direct S2ST systems. We propose LLaS2S, a Large Language Model-based Speech-to-Speech translation system that leverages semantic tokens and textual context to improve discrete acoustic unit duration prediction. We present an S2ST pipeline built on a pre-trained LLM: Whisper with lightweight adapters supplies speech representations to the LLM, and a unit generator plus unit vocoder synthesize waveforms from its hidden states. To enable efficient adaptation, we employ parameter-efficient fine-tuning with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for optimization. Extensive experiments on the CVSS-C dataset demonstrate that LLaS2S outperforms existing baselines demonstrating improvements in translated speech quality, with higher ASR-BLEU and BLASER scores indicating more accurate preservation of source meaning and fewer major semantic errors. More specifically, with the same amount of S2ST supervision data, the proposed models of similar size achieved better ASR-BLEU than the baseline, and the average ASR-BLEU across CVSS-C language pairs increased by 2 points to 26.5. Our findings highlight the potential of LLMs in S2ST, offering practical insights into architectural design, training strategies, and tokenization choices.
