## Abstract

Federated learning (FL) can train models across Internet-of-Things (IoT) and Internet-of-Medical-Things (IoMT) devices without collecting raw data, but model updates still create three linked risks: update disclosure, poisoning by malicious clients, and participation leakage through network timing. Existing secure aggregation protocols hide individual updates, but usually do not validate them; robust filters often require the server to see plaintext updates; and timing defenses are seldom combined with privacy accounting. This gap is important for gateway-assisted IoT deployments, where a single coordinator is easier to operate than multi-server aggregation but must not learn individual or noise-free updates. This paper presents TriSAFE, a protocol composition for federated single-coordinator IoT learning. Clients send encrypted, fixed-point model updates with zero-knowledge range proofs. A plaintext-equivalence protocol (PEP) binds the values proven in zero knowledge to the exact packed Paillier ciphertexts that are later aggregated. Three threshold helpers add differential-privacy (DP) noise homomorphically before decryption, and fixed-cadence batching with cover traffic reduces timing leakage. The coordinator does not have decryption share and receives only the final noised aggregate. In Edge-IIoTset, N-BaIoT, and MNIST, TriSAFE maintains accuracy within 0.1–2.1 percentage points of the baseline of no-attack under Byzantine, label-flip, adaptive poisoning of FANG, and time-delay attacks. The attack success rate (ASR) stays below 1% ( $\lt \!0.1\%$ for FANG), timing-inference area under the curve (AUC) is close to chance, and end-to-end overhead is 7–36% relative to a non-defended baseline. The design is intended for gateway-assisted or edge-class IoT nodes, not bare microcontroller sensor motes, and its privacy guarantee assumes that the coordinator does not collude with two helpers and that at least two helpers contribute honest DP noise.
