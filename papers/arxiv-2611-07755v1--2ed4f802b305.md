# Automated Video-to-QA Pipeline: End-to-End Question Generation and Verification Using Large Language Models

Jaival Chauhan    Vikram Singh    Pruthwik Mishra Affiliation: Sardar
Vallabhbhai National Institute of Technology, Surat, India

###### Abstract

The exponential growth of educational and informational video content on
platforms like YouTube presents a challenge for efficient knowledge
retrieval and assessment. This paper proposes an end-to-end automated
pipeline for extracting structured summaries and generating
high-fidelity Question-Answer (QA) pairs from video streams. The
framework integrates a cascade of state-of-the-art models: audio
extraction is handled via FFmpeg, followed by automatic speech
recognition (ASR) using Whisper (A library in Python) to generate
accurate transcripts. Due to the token constraints of Large Language
Models (LLMs), transcripts are segmented and summarized using Facebook’s
BART.

A key innovation of this work is a Self-Verification and Fine-Tuning
Loop. We utilize meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct to generate candidate
QA pairs from transcript chunks, which are then fed back into the model
to rigorously assess their relevance and importance. Only verified,
high-quality pairs are retained to construct a synthetic dataset. This
dataset is subsequently used to fine-tune the Llama model using Low-Rank
Adaptation (LoRA), resulting in a specialized model capable of
generating domain-aligned assessment questions. This approach
demonstrates a scalable method for creating self-improving educational
tools without the need for manual data annotation

The proposed model can be downloaded from
[Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/models/jaivalchauhan/lora-llama-qna)
with all the codes from
[GitHub](https://github.com/Jaival111/Video-Summarization-and-Question-Generation-System).

## I Introduction

In the digital age, video has established itself as the dominant medium
for information dissemination. Platforms like YouTube host billions of
hours of educational content, ranging from university lectures to
technical tutorials. However, the sheer volume of this data presents a
significant challenge: video is an inherently unstructured modality.
Unlike text, which can be easily skimmed, searched, and indexed,
extracting specific knowledge from video requires linear consumption,
which is time-inefficient for learners and professionals alike.

Furthermore, the pedagogical value of video content is often limited by
the lack of accompanying structured resources, such as concise summaries
and assessment materials (Question-Answer pairs). Creating these
resources manually is a labor-intensive process requiring domain
expertise. While recent advancements in Deep Learning, specifically in
Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and Large Language Models (LLMs),
have enabled the automation of individual tasks, integrating these
technologies into a coherent, reliable pipeline remains a complex
engineering challenge. A primary hurdle in automated Question Generation
(QG) is the tendency of LLMs to ”hallucinate” or generate questions that
are not grounded in the source material, thereby reducing the utility of
the system for educational purposes.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes an end-to-end automated
pipeline designed to transform raw video data into structured,
high-quality educational assets. Our system leverages a multi-stage
architecture beginning with audio extraction via FFmpeg and
high-fidelity transcription using Whisper. To handle the context window
constraints of LLMs, we employ a chunking strategy combined with
abstractive summarization using Facebook’s BART model.

A distinguishing feature of our work is the implementation of a
closed-loop verification mechanism. Rather than relying solely on
zero-shot generation, we utilize meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct to
both generate questions and subsequently evaluate them for relevance and
answerability against the source transcript. This self-supervised
filtering process creates a curated dataset of high-quality QA pairs.
Finally, we demonstrate how this synthetic dataset can be used to
fine-tune the Llama model using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), resulting in
a specialized model that produces superior educational content with
significantly lower computational overhead than full model training.

## II Related Work

The development of automated educational content generation systems sits
at the intersection of multimedia processing, natural language
understanding, and generative AI. This section reviews key advancements
in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), text summarization, and Question
Generation (QG), while also discussing recent techniques in consistency
verification and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

### II-A Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) in Video Processing

Early video-to-text systems relied on Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and
GMM-HMM architectures, which often struggled with background noise and
multi-speaker environments common in YouTube videos. The introduction of
end-to-end deep learning models revolutionized this field. Whisper
represents the current state-of-the-art, utilizing a Transformer-based
sequence-to-sequence architecture trained on 680,000 hours of
multilingual, weakly supervised data. Unlike its predecessors, Whisper
demonstrates robust zero-shot performance across diverse accents and
noisy audio conditions, making it an ideal candidate for extracting
reliable transcripts from uncurated video content.

### II-B Abstractive Summarization

Text summarization is broadly categorized into extractive and
abstractive methods. While extractive approaches (e.g., TextRank) merely
select representative sentences, they often fail to capture the
synthesized meaning required for educational summaries. Abstractive
models, particularly Transformer-based architectures like BART
(Bidirectional and Auto-Regressive Transformers), have set new
benchmarks. BART combines a BERT-like bidirectional encoder with a
GPT-like autoregressive decoder, making it highly effective for text
generation tasks. In our pipeline, we leverage BART’s ability to handle
long-range dependencies to generate coherent summaries from transcript
chunks, addressing the challenge of maintaining context in lengthy video
tutorials.

### II-C Automated Question Generation (QG)

Traditionally, QG systems were rule-based, relying on syntactic
templates and named entity recognition to fill ”cloze” style questions.
These systems offered high precision but low diversity and pedagogical
value. The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4 and Llama 3
has shifted the paradigm toward generative QG, where models can produce
complex, inference-based questions. However, a persistent challenge with
LLM-based QG is ”hallucination,” where generated questions may reference
content not present in the source text. Our work addresses this by
implementing a secondary verification layer, moving beyond simple
generation to a generate-and-validate framework.

### II-D Consistency Verification (LLM-as-a-Judge)

Recent research highlights the efficacy of using LLMs as evaluators or
”judges” to assess the quality of AI-generated content. Studies have
shown that strong instruction-tuned models can achieve high correlation
with human judgment when tasked with grading relevance and factual
consistency. By employing a ”teacher-student” dynamic where Llama 3.1
critiques its own outputs, our pipeline automates the data cleaning
process. This eliminates the bottleneck of human annotation, creating a
closed-loop system for high-quality synthetic data generation.

### II-E Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)

Fine-tuning massive LLMs (7B+ parameters) for specific domains is
computationally expensive and memory-intensive. Low-Rank Adaptation
(LoRA) offers a solution by freezing the pre-trained model weights and
injecting trainable rank decomposition matrices into the Transformer
layers. This reduces the number of trainable parameters by up to 10,000x
while maintaining model performance. Our project utilizes LoRA to adapt
Llama 3.1 to the specific format of educational Q&A, demonstrating that
high-performance, domain-specific models can be trained on
consumer-grade hardware using high-quality, verified synthetic data.

## III Methodology

This section details the architectural design and implementation of the
proposed video-to-QA pipeline. The system is designed as a modular
framework consisting of five distinct stages: Data Ingestion, Audio
Transcription, Summarization, Consistency Verification, and Model
Fine-Tuning. Figure 1 illustrates the high-level data flow.

### III-A Data Acquisition and Preprocessing

The pipeline accepts a YouTube video URL as input. We utilize yt-dlp, a
command-line media downloader, to fetch the video content. To optimize
processing efficiency, we do not process the visual data stream;
instead, we extract the audio track using FFmpeg. The audio is converted
to a monaural waveform sampled at 16kHz, which is the required input
format for the transcription model.

Tools: yt-dlp, FFmpeg

Output: 16kHz .wav audio file.

### III-B Automatic Transcription (ASR)

For transcription, we employ OpenAI’s Whisper model, implemented via the
open-source Python whisper library. Unlike API-based solutions, running
the library locally allows for greater control over model sizing and
inference parameters.

Implementation: We utilize the turbo model architecture, which offers an
optimal balance between transcription accuracy and inference speed.

Process: The audio file is processed to generate a raw text transcript
with associated timestamps. The model’s robust encoder-decoder
architecture effectively handles technical jargon and varying audio
qualities often found in user-generated content

### III-C Chunking and Summarization

Raw transcripts from long-form videos often exceed the token context
window of standard Transformer models. To address this, we implement a
semantic chunking strategy:

Segmentation: The transcript is divided into overlapping chunks of
approximately 1024 tokens. Overlap ensures that context is preserved
across segment boundaries.

Abstractive Summarization: Each text chunk is passed to
facebook/bart-large-cnn, a model pre-trained on the CNN/DailyMail
dataset. BART generates concise abstractive summaries for each segment.

Aggregation: The individual summaries are concatenated to form a global
summary of the video, providing a dense information representation.

### III-D Question-Answer Generation (The Teacher)

We utilize meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as the primary generator.
For every transcript chunk, the model is prompted to act as an educator.

Prompt Strategy: The prompt provides the raw transcript chunk and
instructs the model to: ”Generate 5 questions and their corresponding
correct answers based strictly on the provided text.”

Output: A set of candidate $`(Q,A)`$ pairs associated with specific
context chunks.

### III-E The Verification Loop (The Critic)

A critical component of our methodology is the Self-Verification Module.
LLMs are prone to ”hallucinations,” creating questions about topics not
present in the source text. To mitigate this, we implement a second pass
where Llama 3.1 acts as a critic.

Input: The Source Chunk ($`C`$) and a Candidate Question ($`Q`$).

Verification Prompt: ”Given the text segment below, is the following
question answerable using ONLY this information? Answer ’Yes’ or ’No’.”

Filtering: If the model outputs ”No”, the QA pair is discarded. If the
model outputs ”Yes”, the pair is labeled as ”High Quality” and added to
the training dataset. This automated filtering process creates a
cleaned, robust dataset without human intervention

### III-F Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)

The final stage involves specializing the Llama model to generate
high-quality questions natively, reducing the need for extensive prompt
engineering or post-hoc filtering in future inference.

Dataset: The curated dataset from Section III-E.

Technique: We employ Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). Instead of updating all
8 billion parameters, we inject trainable low-rank matrices into the
attention layers of the model.

Configuration: Rank (r): 16; Alpha: 32; Target Modules: q_proj, v_proj,
k_proj, o_proj; Lora Dropout: 0.5;

Outcome: A fine-tuned version of Llama-3.1 capable of generating
strictly context-aware questions with higher reliability than the base
model

## IV Experiments & Results

To evaluate the efficacy of the proposed pipeline, we conducted a series
of experiments focusing on three key dimensions: the quality of the
abstractive summaries, the effectiveness of the self-verification
filtering mechanism, and the performance improvements gained through
LoRA fine-tuning.

Hardware: All experiments were conducted on a local GPU NVIDIA RTX 4060
with 8 GB of VRAM.

Software: The pipeline was implemented in Python 3.10, utilizing
transformers for BART and Llama integration, peft for LoRA training, and
the openai-whisper library for ASR.

Dataset: We curated a test dataset consisting of YouTube videos focused
on technical education (e.g., coding tutorials, data science lectures).
The average duration of the videos was 20-30 minutes

Qualitative Analysis: A manual review of the rejected questions revealed
that the majority were either too generic (e.g., ”What is the main
topic?” without context) or hallucinated details not present in the
specific chunk. This validates the necessity of the verification loop
for creating a clean training dataset.

To complement automated metrics, we performed a human evaluation on a
random sample of 25 generated QA pairs from the fine-tuned model. We
graded them on a scale of 1-5 for Relevance (is it related to the
video?) and Factuality (is the answer correct?).

## V Discussion

The results indicate that while the Base Llama 3.1 model is a capable
zero-shot generator, the addition of the verification loop significantly
increases the precision of the resulting dataset. Furthermore, LoRA
fine-tuning allowed us to ”lock in” this high performance, creating a
specialized model that produces educational content with high fidelity
and lower latency than prompting the base model with massive context
instructions.

## VI Future Work

While the current system demonstrates robust performance, several
avenues for future research remain:

Multimodal Analysis: Currently, the pipeline relies solely on audio
transcripts. Future iterations could incorporate visual encoders (e.g.,
CLIP) to extract information from video frames (slides, diagrams, or
code snippets) that is not explicitly spoken.

Multilingual Support: Extending the pipeline to support non-English
videos by leveraging Whisper’s translation capabilities and fine-tuning
Llama on multilingual datasets.

Real-Time Processing: Optimizing the inference pipeline to allow for
real-time question generation during live streams or webinars.

## VII Conclusion

This paper presented an end-to-end automated framework for transforming
unstructured video content into structured educational resources. By
integrating state-of-the-art models for speech recognition (Whisper),
abstractive summarization (BART), and generative reasoning (Llama 3.1),
we successfully addressed the challenge of extracting actionable
knowledge from raw video streams.

The central innovation of this work lies in the Self-Verification Loop,
where the Large Language Model serves a dual role as both the generator
and the critic. Our experiments demonstrated that this consistency check
significantly reduces the rate of hallucinations, ensuring that the
generated questions are strictly grounded in the source material.
Furthermore, we showed that utilizing this high-quality, synthetic
dataset to fine-tune the model via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) results in
a specialized ”Teacher Model” that outperforms the base model in
maintaining consistent formatting and domain relevance.

The proposed pipeline offers a scalable solution for educators,
e-learning platforms, and content creators, eliminating the manual
bottleneck of quiz creation and content summarization.
