# Introduction

For a satisfying user experience of human-machine interfaces it is crucial to ensure a high accuracy in automatically recognizing the user’s speech. However, as soon as no close-talking microphone is used for capturing the desired speech signal, the recognition accuracy suffers from additional reverberation, background noise and active interfering speakers which are picked up by the microphones . Techniques for robust speech recognition in such reverberant and noisy environments can be categorized into either front-end (e.g., speech enhancement ) or back-end (e.g., acoustic-model adaptation ) processing techniques.

The <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> targets the performance of state-of-the-art <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> systems in real-world scenarios. The primary goal is to improve the <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> performance of real recorded speech of a person talking to a tablet device in realistic everyday noisy environments by employing front-end and/or back-end signal processing techniques. To this end, a baseline <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system has been initially provided and updated as follow-up of <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> to achieve a high recognition accuracy in everyday real-world scenarios. Front-end processing of the updated baseline now employs the BeamformIt toolkit for processing the recorded microphone signals by a <span acronym-label="WDS" acronym-form="singular+short">WDS</span> beamforming technique. The beamformer output is used as input to the <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> back-end system which contains a <span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span>-based acoustic model and a <span acronym-label="RNN" acronym-form="singular+short">RNN</span>-based language model.

In this article, we extend the updated <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> baseline system by a low-complexity coherence-based postfilter which is applied to the beamformer output signal to further remove reverberation and non-stationary background noise from the latter. The postfilter is realized as a Wiener filter, where, in contrast to the classical Wiener filter, see, e.g., , we use an estimate of the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> as an approximation of the short-time <span acronym-label="SNR" acronym-form="singular+short">SNR</span> to compute the time- and frequency-dependent Wiener filter gains. The <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>, which is the ratio of the power of direct and diffuse signal components, needs to be estimated from the microphone signals. We compare and evaluate two <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent and two <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators. Two of the evaluated <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators have been proposed and shown to be very effective for dereverberation by Schwarz and Kellermann in . The remaining two <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators have been proposed by Jeub et al.  and Thiergart et al.  earlier than , and are evaluated as reference methods. In contrast to the previous work in , where the dereverberation performance was evaluated using <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="plural+short">WERs</span> of a <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="GMM" acronym-form="singular+short">GMM</span>-based <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system trained on clean speech, we now evaluate the efficacy of the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>-based Wiener filter realizations with a state-of-the-art <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span>-based <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system trained on noisy training data from different acoustic environments (provided by <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> ). Moreover, the new <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators in were proposed and evaluated for a dual-channel microphone array, whereas the recognition task of <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> involves signal enhancement using a six-channel microphone array. We therefore extended the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimation procedure to a multi-channel (here: six-channel) scenario. To summarize, the contributions of this article are as follows:

1.  First-time evaluation of new and previously known <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators with a state-of-the-art <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span>-based <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system in challenging acoustic scenarios.

2.  First-time application of coherence-based dereverberation using the new <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators to a multi-microphone scenario with more than two microphones.

# Front-end enhancement techniques

The front-end speech enhancement considered in this article consists of a <span acronym-label="WDS" acronym-form="singular+short">WDS</span> beamformer (based on the BeamformIt toolkit ) and a single-channel coherence-based Wiener filter. In the following, we introduce the signal model which is used throughout this paper. Then, the baseline <span acronym-label="WDS" acronym-form="singular+short">WDS</span> beamformer is briefly reviewed, followed by a detailed presentation of the coherence-based Wiener filter based on <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent and <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimation.

## Signal model

For a consistent presentation of the front-end speech enhancement, we first introduce a signal model which will be used throughout this article.

The $`N`$ microphone signals of the microphone array in the <span acronym-label="STFT" acronym-form="singular+short">STFT</span> domain at frame $`l`$ and frequency $`f`$ are given as:

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}(l,f) = \mathbf{h}(l,f) S(l,f) + \mathbf{n}(l,f),
    \label{eq:signalModel}
\end{equation}
```

where vector

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}(l,f) = [X_0(l,f),\, X_1(l,f),\, \ldots,\, X_{N-1}(l,f)]^{T}
    \label{eq:signalModel_X}
\end{equation}
```

contains the microphone signals, $`S(l,f)`$ denotes the clean source signal, and $`\mathbf{n}(l,f)`$ includes sensor noise as well as interference and diffuse background noise components and is defined analogously to $`\mathbf{x}(l,f)`$ in (<a href="#eq:signalModel_X" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:signalModel_X">[eq:signalModel*X]</a>). Assuming free-field propagation of sound waves, $`\mathbf{h}(l,f)`$ represents the steering vector modeling the sound propagation of a plane wave arriving from the target source <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span> $`(\phi*\mathrm{d},\theta\_\mathrm{d})`$ (azimuth and elevation angle $`\phi`$ and $`\theta`$ are measured with respect to the positive $`x`$- and $`z`$-axis, respectively, as in ) to all $`N`$ microphones:

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}(l,f) = [e^{j 2 \pi f \tau_{0}(l)},\, e^{j 2 \pi f \tau_{1}(l)}, \, \ldots,\, e^{j 2 \pi f \tau_{N-1}(l)}]^{T},
    \label{eq:signalModel_H}
\end{equation}
```

where $`\tau_{n}(l)`$ is the (possibly time-varying) <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="singular+short">TDOA</span> for the target source direction of the $`n`$-th channel with respect to the origin of the coordinate system :

```math
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{n}(l) = \frac{\mathbf{a}^{T}(l) \mathbf{p}_{n}}{c}.
    \label{eq:signalModel_TDOA}
\end{equation}
```

In (<a href="#eq:signalModel_TDOA" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:signalModel_TDOA">[eq:signalModel*TDOA]</a>), vector $`\mathbf{p}*{n}`$ contains the position of the $`n`$-th microphone in Cartesian coordinates and the unit vector $`\mathbf{a}(l)`$ is defined as :

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a}(l) = - [ \ensuremath{\mathrm{sin}(\theta_\mathrm{d})}\ensuremath{\mathrm{cos}(\phi_\mathrm{d})},\, \ensuremath{\mathrm{sin}(\theta_\mathrm{d})}\ensuremath{\mathrm{sin}(\phi_\mathrm{d})},\, \ensuremath{\mathrm{cos}(\theta_\mathrm{d})} ]^{T}.
    \label{eq:signalModel_directionVector}
\end{equation}
```

Note that $`\mathbf{a}(l)`$ points from the target source <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span> towards the origin of the coordinate system (hence the minus sign) and that the time-dependency of $`\phi_\mathrm{d}`$ and $`\theta_\mathrm{d}`$ in (<a href="#eq:signalModel_directionVector" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:signalModel_directionVector">[eq:signalModel*directionVector]</a>) has been omitted for brevity. Moreover, $`c`$ represents the speed of sound and operator $`(\cdot)^T`$ denotes the transpose of a vector or matrix. The beamformer output $`Y*\mathrm{BF}(l,f)`$ is obtained by multiplying each microphone signal with a complex-valued beamformer coefficient $`W\_{n}(l,f)`$, followed by a summation over all microphone channels:

```math
\begin{equation}
    Y_\mathrm{BF}(l,f) = \mathbf{w}^{T}(l,f)\mathbf{x}(l,f),
\end{equation}
```

where

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{w}(l,f) = [W_{0}(l,f), \ldots, W_{N-1}(l,f)]^{T}
    \label{eq:BFweights_vector}
\end{equation}
```

contains the beamformer coefficients $`W_{n}(l,f)`$.

Subsequently, the postfilter is applied to the beamformer output signal, yielding the overall output signal

```math
\begin{equation}
    Y(l,f) = G(l,f)Y_\mathrm{BF}(l,f),
\end{equation}
```

where $`G(l,f)`$ describes the postfilter gain at frame $`l`$ and frequency $`f`$. The enhanced signal $`Y(l,f)`$ is used as input of the acoustic back-end system (described in Section <a href="#sec:BackEnd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:BackEnd">3</a>).

## Weighted delay-and-sum beamformer

The employed beamformer is provided by the BeamformIt toolkit and based on the <span acronym-label="WDS" acronym-form="singular+short">WDS</span> beamforming technique . The $`n`$-th beamformer filter coefficient $`W_{n}(l,f)`$ at frame $`l`$ and frequency $`f`$ is given as

```math
\begin{equation}
    W_{n}(l,f) =  w_{n}(l) e^{- j 2 \pi f \tau_{n}(l)},
    \label{eq:W_BF_main}
\end{equation}
```

where $`w_{n}(l)`$ is the frequency-independent weight for the $`n`$-th channel. By modifying $`W_{n}(l,f)`$, one can control the shape and angular direction of the beamformer’s main beam.

The <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="plural+short">TDOAs</span> are estimated using the <span acronym-label="GCC-PHAT" acronym-form="singular+short">GCC-PHAT</span> localization technique, see, e.g., . Before the signals are time-aligned a two-step post processing is applied to the estimated <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="plural+short">TDOAs</span>: First, a noise threshold is estimated and employed to remove non-reliable <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="singular+short">TDOA</span> estimates which may have been obtained from non-speech or noisy segments. Second, Viterbi decoding of the remaining <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="singular+short">TDOA</span> values is performed to maximize the speaker continuity, i.e., to avoid steering the beam to noise sources which are only present at a very short time span.

The channel weights are chosen adaptively over time, starting with the classical delay-and-sum as initial value: $`W_{n}(0,f) = 1/N`$. Furthermore, automatic channel selection and elimination is performed to avoid using microphone signals of poor quality. Both, channel weight adaptation and channel selection/elimination are based on the cross-correlations between the microphone channels.

For a more detailed explanation of the baseline beamformer (including localization), as provided by the BeamformIt toolkit, we refer the reader to .

## Coherence-based postfilter

As illustrated in Figure <a href="#fig:ASR_pipe" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ASR_pipe">1</a>, we apply a postfilter to remove diffuse noise components from the beamformer output signal. The postfilter gain $`G(l,f)`$ at frame $`l`$ and frequency $`f`$ is given as :

```math
\begin{equation}
    G(l,f) = \mathrm{max} \left\{ 1 - \mu \frac{1}{1 + \mathrm{SNR}(l,f)} ,\, G_\mathrm{min} \right\},
    \label{eq:WienerFilter}
\end{equation}
```

with overestimation factor $`\mu`$, and gain floor $`G_\mathrm{min}`$. The postfilter in (<a href="#eq:WienerFilter" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:WienerFilter">[eq:WienerFilter]</a>) is a Wiener filter which uses the short-time <span acronym-label="SNR" acronym-form="singular+short">SNR</span> to compute the filter gain $`G(l,f)`$. In this work, we approximate the short-time <span acronym-label="SNR" acronym-form="singular+short">SNR</span> in (<a href="#eq:WienerFilter" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:WienerFilter">[eq:WienerFilter]</a>) by an estimate of the so-called <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>, which is the power ratio between direct and diffuse signal components. From (<a href="#eq:WienerFilter" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:WienerFilter">[eq:WienerFilter]</a>), it can be seen that a low <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> value, which corresponds to strong diffuse signal components being present at the input of the system, leads to low filter gains and vice versa.

The <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> between two omnidirectional microphones is defined as :

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{CDR}(l,f) = \frac{\Gamma_\mathrm{n}(l,f) - \Gamma_\mathrm{x}(l,f)}{\Gamma_\mathrm{x}(l,f)-\Gamma_\mathrm{s}(l,f)},
    \label{eq:CDRgeneral}
\end{equation}
```

where $`\Gamma_\mathrm{x}(l,f)`$, $`\Gamma_\mathrm{s}(l,f)`$, $`\Gamma_\mathrm{n}(l,f)`$ denote the coherence functions of the observations, of the direct-path signal, and of the noise between two observation points (microphones), respectively. In the following, the two microphones are indexed by the variables $`p=1,...,N`$ and $`q=1,...,N`$, respectively. To this end, the spatial coherence functions for the direct-path signals and diffuse noise components are given as

```math
\begin{align}
    \Gamma_\mathrm{s}(l,f) &= e^{j 2 \pi f (\tau_{p}(l) -\tau_{q}(l)) },\\
    \Gamma_\mathrm{n}(f) &= \frac{\sin(2 \pi f \frac{d_{pq}}{c})}{2 \pi f \frac{d_{pq}}{c}},
    \label{eq:GammaDiff}
\end{align}
```

respectively, with <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="singular+short">TDOA</span>s $`\tau_{p}(l)`$, $`\tau_{q}(l)`$ calculated in (<a href="#eq:signalModel_TDOA" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:signalModel_TDOA">[eq:signalModel*TDOA]</a>) and microphone spacing $`d*{pq}`$. Moreover, a short-time estimate $`\hat{\Gamma}_\mathrm{x}(l,f)`$ of the coherence function of both microphone signals $`\Gamma_\mathrm{x}(l,f)`$ in (<a href="#eq:CDRgeneral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDRgeneral">[eq:CDRgeneral]</a>) can be obtained using

```math
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\Gamma}_\mathrm{x}(l,f) = \frac{\hat{\Phi}_{x_px_q}(l,f) }{\sqrt{\hat{\Phi}_{x_px_p}(l,f)\hat{\Phi}_{x_qx_q}(l,f)}}
    \label{eq:CoherenceEst}
\end{equation}
```

by estimating the auto- and cross-<span acronym-label="PSDs" acronym-form="singular+short">PSDs</span> $`\hat{\Phi}_{x_px_q}(l,f)`$ from the microphone signals $`X_p(l,f)`$ and $`X_q(l,f)`$ based on recursive averaging

```math
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\Phi}_{x_px_q}(l,f) = \lambda \hat{\Phi}_{x_px_q}(l-1,f) + (1-\lambda) X_p(l,f) X^*_q(l,f)
    \label{eq:RecAv}
\end{equation}
```

with forgetting factor $`\lambda`$. Operator $`(\cdot)^*`$ creates the conjugate complex of $`(\cdot)`$. However, inserting the coherence estimate $`\hat{\Gamma}_\mathrm{x}(l,f)`$ into (<a href="#eq:CDRgeneral" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDRgeneral">[eq:CDRgeneral]</a>) is not feasible due to the mismatch between coherence models and actual acoustic conditions as $`\mathrm{CDR}(l,f)`$ might become a complex-valued quantity . Thus, the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> needs to be estimated. In this work, we use two <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators, which have been proposed and shown to be especially effective in , and which are given by

```math
\begin{align}
        & \widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{DOAindep} = \notag \\ &  \max \left(0, \frac{\Gamma_\mathrm{n}\, \operatorname{Re}\{\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}\} -{|\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}|}^2 - \sqrt{\Gamma_\mathrm{n}^2\, {\operatorname{Re}\{\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}\}}^2 - \Gamma_\mathrm{n}^2\, {|\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}|}^2 + \Gamma_\mathrm{n}^2 - 2\, \Gamma_\mathrm{n}\, \operatorname{Re}\{\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}\} + {|\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}|}^2}}{{|\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}|}^2 - 1} \right)
        \label{eq:CDR_estimator_prop2}
\end{align}
```

and

```math
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{DOAdep} =  \max \left(0, \frac{1-\Gamma_\mathrm{n} \cos(\arg(\Gamma_\mathrm{s}))}{|\Gamma_\mathrm{n} - \Gamma_\mathrm{s}|} \left| \frac{\Gamma_\mathrm{s}^* (\Gamma_\mathrm{n}-\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}) }{\operatorname{Re}\{ \Gamma_\mathrm{s}^* \hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}\}-1} \right| \right),
    \label{eq:CDR_estimator_prop3}
\end{equation}
```

respectively, where $`\operatorname{Re}\{\cdot\}`$, $`|\cdot|`$, and $`\arg\{\cdot\}`$ represent the real part, magnitude, and phase of $`(\cdot)`$, respectively. The maximum operation is required to prevent negative results for the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate. Note that frame- and frequency-index have been omitted in (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop2" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop2">[eq:CDR*estimator_prop2]</a>) and (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop3" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop3">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop3]</a>) for brevity. As can be seen from (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop2" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop2">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop2]</a>), this estimator does not require the <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="singular+short">TDOA</span> of the target source, since $`\Gamma*{s}(l,f)`$ is not required for calculating $`\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{DOAindep}`$. This is not the case for the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent estimator $`\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{DOAdep}`$ in (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop3" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop3">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop3]</a>). On the one hand, the advantage of <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent estimators is that these are easier to realize, since no additional information, i.e., the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span> (or <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="singular+short">TDOA</span>) of the target source, is required. On the other hand, <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent estimators have a directional filtering effect due to the incorporation of the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span> into the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate, which is expected to provide a more accurate <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate and, therefore, lead to a better suppression of diffuse noise and reverberation.

In addition to (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop2" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop2">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop2]</a>) and (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop3" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop3">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop3]</a>), we evaluate two <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators as reference methods, which have been chosen due to their good speech dereverberation performance in previous experiments .

The first reference <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimator is a <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent estimator and was proposed by Thiergart et al. in :

```math
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{Thiergart} =  \max \left(0, \operatorname{Re}\left\{ \frac{\Gamma_\mathrm{n}-\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}}{\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}-e^{j \arg \hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}}} \right\} \right).
    \label{eq:CDR_estimator_thiergart2}
\end{equation}
```

This estimator is <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent, because it uses the instantaneous phase of the coherence estimate $`\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}(l,f)`$ as phase estimate for the direct signal model, i.e., $`\hat\Gamma_\mathrm{s}(l,f) = e^{j \arg \hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}(l,f)}`$.

The second reference <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimator is based on Jeub’s <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimator . The estimator is given as:

```math
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{Jeub} =  \max \left(0, \frac{\Gamma_\mathrm{n}-\operatorname{Re}\{\Gamma_\mathrm{s}^* \hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}\}}{\operatorname{Re}\{\Gamma_\mathrm{s}^* \hat\Gamma_\mathrm{x}\}-1} \right)
    \label{eq:CDR_estimator_jeub}
\end{equation}
```

and is <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent. Jeub et al. relied on the same assumption as McCowan and Bourlard, who derived a Wiener postfilter for a coherent signal in diffuse noise , to explicitly formulate their <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_jeub" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_jeub">[eq:CDR_estimator_jeub]</a>). Hence, (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_jeub" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_jeub">[eq:CDR_estimator_jeub]</a>) can also be derived using McCowan’s and Bourlard’s signal and noise <span acronym-label="PSD" acronym-form="singular+abbrv">PSD</span> estimates .

In it was shown that for non-zero <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="plural+short">TDOAs</span> both $`\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{Thiergart}`$ and $`\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\text{Jeub}`$ provide biased <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimates, whereas for <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="plural+short">TDOAs</span> equal to zero, their <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate is unbiased. A more detailed investigation of the employed <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop2" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop2">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop2]</a>)–(<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_jeub" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_jeub">[eq:CDR_estimator_jeub]</a>) with respect to their properties and dereverberation performance for a two-channel microphone array using an <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="GMM" acronym-form="singular+short">GMM</span>-based <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system (trained on clean speech) and signal-dependent measures, can be found in .

When applying the coherence-based postfilter to the output of a beamformer, two aspects need to be considered: First, since the microphone array of the <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> challenge consists of five forward-facing microphones (in this work, the additional backward-facing microphone was excluded from the experiments), the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimator (initially designed for a pair of microphones) has to be adapted to exploit all available microphone signals. To do so, we apply the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators in (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop2" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop2">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop2]</a>)–(<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_jeub" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_jeub">[eq:CDR_estimator_jeub]</a>) to every pair of non-failing microphones (detection and elimination of failing channels is carried out by the BeamformIt toolkit, see Subsection <a href="#subsec:MVDR" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="subsec:MVDR">2.2</a>) to obtain the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate for each microphone pair.

From each of the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimates, we calculate the respective diffuseness values as

```math
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{D}(l,f) = (1 + \widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}(l,f))^{-1}.
    \label{eq:diffuseness}
\end{equation}
```

Subsequently, we take the arithmetic mean of all microphone pair-specific diffuseness values to obtain an average diffuseness estimate $`\overline{\mathrm{D}}(l,f)`$. We then calculate the final <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate as:

```math
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\mathrm{In}(l,f) = \frac{1 - \overline{\mathrm{D}}(l,f)}{\overline{\mathrm{D}}(l,f)}.
    \label{eq:CDRestimate_input}
\end{equation}
```

We take the average of the diffuseness estimates instead of averaging the microphone pair-specific <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> values directly, since the latter can take values between zero and infinity, whereas the diffuseness lies in the interval $`0 \leq \mathrm{D}(l,f) \leq 1`$. Note that McCowan and Bourlard also applied their Wiener postfilter to a multi-microphone setup . The second aspect is that $`\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\mathrm{In}(l,f)`$ is a <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate at the input of the signal enhancement system, i.e., at the microphones. However, what we need is the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> at the output of the beamformer. This can be obtained by applying a correction factor $`A_\mathrm{\Gamma}(l,f)`$ to $`\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\mathrm{In}(l,f)`$. Thus, the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimate at the output of the beamformer $`\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\mathrm{BF}(l,f)`$ is defined as

```math
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\mathrm{BF}(l,f) = \frac{\widehat{\mathrm{CDR}}_\mathrm{In}(l,f)}{ A_\mathrm{\Gamma}(l,f) },
    \label{eq:CDR_bf}
\end{equation}
```

where $`A_\mathrm{\Gamma}(l,f)`$ is the inverse of the array gain for diffuse noise, given by

```math
\begin{equation}
    A_\mathrm{\Gamma}(l,f) = \mathbf{w}^{H}(l,f) \mathbf{J}_\mathrm{diff}(f) \mathbf{w}(l,f),
    \label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
```

where $`(\cdot)^H`$ denotes the Hermitian of a vector or matrix and $`\mathbf{J}_\mathrm{diff}(f)`$ is the $`N \times N`$ spatial coherence matrix of a diffuse noise field with the ($`p,q`$)-th element given by (<a href="#eq:GammaDiff" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:GammaDiff">[eq:GammaDiff]</a>). Note that we assume a distortionless beamformer response for the target source <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>. Hence, the denominator of $`A_\mathrm{\Gamma}(l,f)`$ in (<a href="#eq:A" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:A">[eq:A]</a>) is equal to one.

Figure <a href="#fig:CDRbased_postfilter" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:CDRbased_postfilter">2</a> shows the block diagram of the employed front-end enhancement system, consisting of beamformer and coherence-based postfilter. We would like to point out that knowledge of the array geometry is required to estimate the target source <span acronym-label="TDOA" acronym-form="plural+short">TDOAs</span> which are needed to realize the beamformer (<a href="#eq:W_BF_main" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:W_BF_main">[eq:W_BF_main]</a>) and the Wiener filter (if one of the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators in (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop3" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop3">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop3]</a>) or (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_jeub" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_jeub">[eq:CDR_estimator_jeub]</a>) is to be used for estimating the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>).

# ASR back-end system

In this section, we describe the employed <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> back-end system, which was updated by the organizers of <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> based on the findings of the challenge results. The employed <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system includes an <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="GMM" acronym-form="singular+short">GMM</span> system, consisting of $`2500`$ tied triphone <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span> states which are modeled by $`15000`$ Gaussians, as well as an <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span> system providing state-of-the-art <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> performance for real-world scenarios. The <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span> system employs a seven-layer feed-forward <span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span> with $`2048`$ neurons per hidden layer and is based on “Karel’s implementation“ of the Kaldi toolkit . The <span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span> training process is using noisy data from different acoustic environments and includes pre-training using restricted Boltzmann machines, cross entropy training, and sequence discriminative training using the state-level minimum Bayes risk (sMBR) criterion. We extract $`40`$ <span acronym-label="MFCC" acronym-form="plural+short">MFCCs</span> from the single-channel output signal of the acoustic front-end signal enhancement scheme. The resulting length-$`40`$ feature vector is passed through per-speaker mean and variance normalization, a <span acronym-label="fMLLR" acronym-form="singular+short">fMLLR</span> transformation and context extension, where the latter appends the feature vectors of $`3`$ (HMM-GMM system) or $`5`$ (HMM-DNN system) previous and successive frames. The CHiME-3 baseline system performs acoustic front-end signal enhancement during training and decoding (i.e., we train on enhanced features obtained from the output signals of the acoustic front-end including beamforming and postfiltering). In the decoding phase, we produce word lattices ($`3`$-gram language model), generate a $`100`$-best list ($`5`$-gram language model) and perform lattice rescoring using an <span acronym-label="RNN" acronym-form="singular+short">RNN</span>-based language model (one hidden layer with $`300`$ nodes) trained on the WSJ0 text corpus. More details about the <span acronym-label="RNN" acronym-form="singular+short">RNN</span>-based language modeling can be found in .

## Setup and parameters

To obtain the <span acronym-label="STFT" acronym-form="singular+short">STFT</span>-representation, we use a <span acronym-label="DFT" acronym-form="singular+short">DFT</span>-based uniform filterbank with window length $`1024`$, <span acronym-label="FFT" acronym-form="singular+short">FFT</span> size $`512`$, and downsampling factor $`128`$ . The signals were processed at a sampling rate of $`16`$ kHz. <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span> estimation and <span acronym-label="WDS" acronym-form="singular+short">WDS</span> beamforming were provided by the BeamformIt toolkit. The window and scroll sizes used for BeamformIt were the default baseline parameters, i.e., $`500`$ms and $`250`$ms, respectively. The array geometry was known a priori.

For realizing the coherence-based postfilter, we chose a gain floor $`G_\mathrm{min}= 0.1`$ and optimized the overestimation factor $`\mu`$ on the development data set (see Figure <a href="#fig:ResL" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ResL">5</a>). The short-time coherence estimates $`\hat{\Gamma}_\mathrm{x}(l,f)`$ were obtained by recursive averaging of the auto- and cross-<span acronym-label="PSDs" acronym-form="singular+short">PSDs</span> with forgetting factor $`\lambda = 0.68`$ in (<a href="#eq:RecAv" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:RecAv">[eq:RecAv]</a>).

The <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> task included sets of real and simulated noisy utterances in four different environments: café (CAF), street junction (STR), public transport (BUS), and pedestrian area (PED). For each environment, a training set, a development set, and an evaluation set consisting of real and simulated data were provided by <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> . The training data set consists of $`1600`$ real and $`7138`$ simulated utterances from a total of four (real), and 83 (simulated) speakers. The development data set contains $`1640`$ real and simulated utterances, whereas the evaluation data set consists of $`1320`$ real and simulated utterances. The utterances within each data set were obtained from different speakers. In this work, we focused on evaluating the recognition accuracy of the <span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span>-based <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system for the practically relevant case of real-world recordings.

However, it can be seen that the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent estimator in general attributes higher diffuseness values to time-frequency regions where the target signal is not active. The reason for this difference lies in the fact that the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent estimator exhibits a directional filtering effect, whereas the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent estimator does not. The DOA-dependent estimator only considers signal components arriving from the given target <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span> as desired signal components, while directional signal components from other directions will increase the diffuseness estimate. Consequently, the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent estimator will lead to stronger suppression in time-frequency regions where the target signal is not active. Even if no directional interferers are present, the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent estimator can achieve higher suppression of diffuse noise due to lower sensitivity to the variance of the coherence estimate for diffuse signal components .

## Evaluation of recognition accuracy

In the following, we evaluate the recognition accuracy obtained by the front-end signal enhancement in Section <a href="#sec:FrontEnd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:FrontEnd">2</a> combined with the <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system in Section <a href="#sec:BackEnd" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:BackEnd">3</a>. More precisely, we evaluate the impact of coherence-based single-channel Wiener filtering, realized using ...

- <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimation (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop2" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop2">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop2]</a>), termed WF<sub>DOAindep</sub>,

- <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimation (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_prop3" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_prop3">[eq:CDR_estimator_prop3]</a>), termed WF<sub>DOAdep</sub>,

- <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimation (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_thiergart2" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_thiergart2">[eq:CDR_estimator_thiergart2]</a>), termed WF<sub>Thiergart</sub>,

- <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimation (<a href="#eq:CDR_estimator_jeub" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:CDR_estimator_jeub">[eq:CDR_estimator_jeub]</a>), termed WF<sub>Jeub</sub>.

In Figure <a href="#fig:ResL" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ResL">5</a>, the resulting <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="plural+short">WERs</span> for real recordings of the <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> development set are illustrated for different values of the overestimation factor $`\mu`$ in (<a href="#eq:WienerFilter" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="eq:WienerFilter">[eq:WienerFilter]</a>). It is obvious that the recognition accuracy of the <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> ASR system without postfilter (“No WF” in Figure <a href="#fig:ResL" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ResL">5</a>) is consistently improved by incorporating coherence-based postfiltering , except for WF<sub>Thiergart</sub> with $`\mu \geq 0.8`$ which yielded <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="plural+short">WERs</span> between $`6.45\%`$ and $`7.94\%`$. Since these <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="plural+short">WERs</span> are higher than those without postfiltering, they are not shown in Figure <a href="#fig:ResL" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ResL">5</a> to allow for a clearer presentation of the remaining results. Moreover, it can be seen from Figure <a href="#fig:ResL" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ResL">5</a> that the Wiener filters which were realized using <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators (WF<sub>DOAdep</sub> and WF<sub>Jeub</sub>) yield a better signal enhancement performance compared to Wiener filters realized using <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators (WF<sub>DOAindep</sub> and WF<sub>Thiergart</sub>). Among the <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent Wiener filter realizations, WF<sub>Jeub</sub> yields a slightly better performance than WF<sub>DOAdep</sub> for almost every value of $`\mu`$.

Based on the results illustrated in Figure <a href="#fig:ResL" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="fig:ResL">5</a>, we selected one overestimation factor $`\mu_\text{opt}`$ for each Wiener filter realization, in order to obtain the best performance in the experiments using the evaluation data set (real data). The selected overestimation factors for each Wiener filter realization are summarized in Table <a href="#tab:table_best_mu" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tab:table_best_mu">1</a>. One can see that the overestimation factors chosen for WF<sub>Thiergart</sub> and WF<sub>Jeub</sub> are much smaller than those for WF<sub>DOAindep</sub> and WF<sub>DOAdep</sub>. To verify that there is no unfair comparison of the different Wiener filter realizations due to the potentially lower noise suppression performance, we also evaluated the real recordings of the evaluation data set for the different overestimation factors. Our results showed that larger overestimation factors as the ones in Table <a href="#tab:table_best_mu" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tab:table_best_mu">1</a> for WF<sub>Thiergart</sub> and WF<sub>Jeub</sub> do not lead to better <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="plural+short">WERs</span> for these Wiener filter realizations.

In order to investigate the statistical significance of the <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="singular+short">WER</span> improvements obtained with the various Wiener filter realizations for the real evaluation data set, we applied the <span acronym-label="MAPSSWE" acronym-form="singular+short">MAPSSWE</span> test to the results in Table <a href="#tab:Results_avgWERs" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tab:Results_avgWERs">[tab:Results_avgWERs]</a>. The <span acronym-label="MAPSSWE" acronym-form="singular+short">MAPSSWE</span> test uses knowledge of aligned reference and hypothesis (produced by the <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system) sentence strings to locate segments within the sentence strings which contain misclassified content. To compare two different systems, the number of errors in each segment for each system is computed, and the null hypothesis that the mean difference in the number of word errors per segment between the two systems is zero is tested, see, e.g. . In our experiments, a significance level of $`p=5\%`$, i.e., a $`95\%`$ confidence level for rejecting the null hypothesis, was chosen. We used the implementation of the <span acronym-label="MAPSSWE" acronym-form="singular+short">MAPSSWE</span> test provided by the <span acronym-label="NIST" acronym-form="singular+short">NIST</span> Scoring Toolkit . Table <a href="#tab:Results_MAPSSWE" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="tab:Results_MAPSSWE">2</a> compares the results of the <span acronym-label="MAPSSWE" acronym-form="singular+short">MAPSSWE</span> test. For every comparison, the better signal enhancement system is given in the corresponding field of the table if a statistically significant difference between the systems was found, and “same” otherwise. All Wiener filter realizations yield a statistically significant improvement of the signal enhancement baseline without postfiltering of the beamformer output signal. It can furthermore be observed that WF<sub>DOAdep</sub> performs significantly better than WF<sub>Thiergart</sub> and WF<sub>Jeub</sub>, but no significant difference between WF<sub>DOAdep</sub> and WF<sub>DOAindep</sub> is found. Thus, a consistent and statistically significant benefit of exploiting <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span> information for <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>-based postfiltering cannot be inferred from the results. An additional significance test for the results of the different Wiener filter realizations for the real development data set did also not show a consistent and statistically significant advantage of <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent over <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>-estimators.

# Conclusion

We proposed to extend the front-end speech enhancement of a state-of-the-art <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system by coherence-based postfiltering of the beamformer output signal. The postfilter is realized as a Wiener filter, where an estimate of the power ratio between direct and diffuse signal components at the output of the beamformer is used as an approximation of the short-time <span acronym-label="SNR" acronym-form="singular+short">SNR</span> to compute the time- and frequency-dependent postfilter gains. To estimate the ratio between direct and diffuse signal components, we used two <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent and two <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent estimators, which can be efficiently realized by estimating the auto- and cross-<span acronym-label="PSDs" acronym-form="singular+short">PSDs</span> at the microphone signals. As a consequence, the postfilter has a very low computational complexity. Baseline and extended front-end speech enhancement have been evaluated on real recordings provided by <span acronym-label="CHiME-3" acronym-form="singular+short">CHiME-3</span> with respect to <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="plural+short">WERs</span> of a state-of-the-art <span acronym-label="HMM" acronym-form="singular+short">HMM</span>-<span acronym-label="DNN" acronym-form="singular+short">DNN</span>-based <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system. The results confirmed that coherence-based postfiltering in general improves the recognition accuracy of the <span acronym-label="ASR" acronym-form="singular+short">ASR</span> system significantly, with relative improvements of up to $`11.31\%`$ and $`8.21\%`$ for the development and the evaluation data set, respectively. Consistent statistically significant differences between Wiener filters based on <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent and <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators could not be observed. The improved recognition accuracy in addition to the low computational complexity makes coherence-based postfiltering very suitable for real-time robust distant speech recognition. As future work, it should be evaluated whether <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>-based postfilters for dereverberation still yield a significant improvement of <span acronym-label="WER" acronym-form="plural+short">WERs</span> when a more powerful beamforming algorithm is used: On the one hand, if the beamformer has a higher directivity, i.e., a higher suppression of diffuse noise components, a CDR-based postfilter might be less effective because the beamformer partly fulfills the function of the <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span>-based postfilter. On the other hand, if the beamformer already partly suppresses the diffuse noise, then the postfilter sees a better input <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> and could possibly be tuned to be more aggressive, which could further improve speech recognition performance. Another aspect of future work is the investigation of the combination of <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-independent and <span acronym-label="DOA" acronym-form="singular+short">DOA</span>-dependent <span acronym-label="CDR" acronym-form="singular+short">CDR</span> estimators in different frequency areas, in order to obtain an even better diffuseness estimate.
